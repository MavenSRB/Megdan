## Timing, Active effects, and continuous sources

`CreateActiveEffect` produces a serializable instance with source card/action, controller, affected scope, trigger window, duration, ordering data, uniqueness key, effect definition, and cancellation conditions.

Supported timing definitions are introduced from verified needs and include:

- Before Attack and After Attack.
- Defense response and Defense/Attack end.
- This Turn and This Round.
- Next Turn and Next Turn after Playing Cards.
- End of Turn and End of Round.
- Action start, effect step, action end, and system checkpoints.

Simultaneous Active effects use the official tie procedure, record required choices, and do not flip the Tie Breaker in that ordering window.

Milestone 5.4 implements named pure checkpoints and definition-owned trigger, duration, cross-round, uniqueness, and early-cancellation data. Instances retain phase, source card/action, creation order, activation anchors, and ending checkpoint. The four named durations remain distinct; cancellation/expiry are idempotent, and activation does not also trigger the effect in the same visit. Retrieval, source-card-state change, applicable defeat, and explicit cancellation are available boundaries invoked by their later owning systems.

Milestone 8.12 gives the lifecycle's `Triggered` visit its production reader. An Active-effect definition or applicable continuous source may declare a catalog-owned typed effect reference for Action Ended; each visit starts that definition through the ordinary effect frame. Cross-team simultaneity follows revealed Tie-Breaker side first and stable source order without flipping. A question pauses on existing `PendingResolution`, withholds Initiative, and restores through the card-action Skill continuation, so schema v24 does not change. Cancellation and expiry remain ahead of trigger selection. Milestone 8.13's High Voltage source adds `AfterControllerPerformsBasicSkill`, derived only for its controller's completed basic Skill while that hero is on board; its questions and staged Defeat therefore need no new pending kind.

Milestone 8.19c adds the persistent-source families the roster needs and one more trigger condition. `AfterControllerPerformsBasicAction` reaches every completed basic action of its controller's, while 8.12's `AfterControllerPerformsBasicSkill` keeps its narrower reading: an explicit switch answers both, so widening one condition cannot silently widen the other. An Ultimate may instead declare a Minion-Battle rule, which is neither a modification nor a triggered effect - it is a named rule resolved through `MinionBattleRuleCatalog` at the end of round, and it is the only isolated handler the roster audit authorized. Two trigger windows are declared and owned by milestone 8.19c1 rather than executed here: the end-of-round moment before the Minion Battle, and the moment immediately after an applied Push. Both stop the game on a player decision at an altitude snapshot v26 cannot hold, so 8.19c declares the vocabulary and 8.19c1 supplies the persistence and the execution.

Milestone 8.19c1 executes both, in schema v27. The end-of-round moment runs through the ordinary triggered-effect visit before p.8's battle is planned, so an effect that moves a minion out of the Battle Zone changes what that battle then counts; a question standing there leaves the stage cursor exactly where it was, which is why the round persists that the moment has already run rather than inferring it from the cursor. The applied-Push moment is reached from inside the effect that pushed: the resolver stops at the node the Push finished, the authority runs whatever declared `after-push-trigger@1` for each object the Push actually displaced, and the enclosing effect then carries on from that exact node. That needs two effect positions at once, which one `PendingResolution` cannot hold, so `PendingEffectInterrupt` carries the trigger's frame beside the continuation that owns the interrupted one. The applied-Push window is deliberately not on the Active-effect lifecycle's supported list: it is an Ultimate's own declaration, and an Active effect naming it would never fire.

Milestone 8.25a corrects when the turn lets go of an action whose ability may still repeat it. Milestone 8.23's `AfterControllerPerformsBasicAction` source can open a decision at the `ActionEnded` checkpoint, and the action that reaches that checkpoint has finished, so the turn used to resolve its card and acknowledge Initiative before the decision was answered and withhold only the advance. The repeat the answer then owed had no actor selected and no unresolved card to run on: nothing started it, it survived the command that settled it, and it was finally started by whichever action completed next - in whoever's turn that was - where every command the projection offered for it was refused because the pending resolution was not the selected actor's. **The action is not over while something may still repeat it.** While a repeat subject is held, the turn holds the card and the acknowledgement too, and the two commands that can finish a standing trigger - the effect choice and the removal prevention - start any owed repeat before doing anything else, so the deferred case is the same case as an ability that asks nothing and repeats inside the same command. A repeated Attack instance also carries `ActionInvocationContext.Repeated` on its resolution from this milestone, which is what tells its own `ActionEnded` checkpoint that it was a repeat and so what makes p.10's "You cannot repeat a repeated action!" true of an Attack; the offer it runs under is still the printed one its card prints, because the card prints one action and the repeat is that action again. Milestone 8.25d finishes the sentence: only that path minted the context, while milestone 8.10's card-text repeat copied the completed instance's printed one, so a repeat a card's own text asked for still reported itself as printed - Xargatha's narrowing never fired, and nothing bounded a chain of them. The second instance now carries the repeated context and a resolution identity of its own, and card text applied inside p.14 is told when its enclosing attack is already a repeat, which is where p.10's bar lives on that path.

Milestone 5.8 integrates Defense response and Attack-result comparison as durable timing checkpoints. In the turn authority, an accepted Hero target visits Action Start and Before Attack before Attack Total, opens Defense response before the defender choice, visits result comparison only after an accepted discard/decline, then visits After Attack before Attack completion and action end before source-card resolution cancellation. Rejected or stale commands visit none of these checkpoints. Snapshot schema v5 rejects v4 so older code cannot silently deserialize the new timing/value discriminators without executing their meaning.

`ContinuousRuleSource` is separate. Ultimates apply only while their hero is on the board; token properties persist while the token remains. Their applicability is queried directly and does not inherit card Active-effect cancellation.

## Control flow and action composition

Widget's Skill reuse keeps two distinct pools: owned resolved face-up Skills in the turn area, and owned face-up Skills in the turn area or discard pile (including the current unresolved Movement source). Neither admits the hand or changes the borrowed source's state. `PerformedMovementAction` is produced at actual Movement completion, including zero spaces; direct Move, Swap, Place and Fast Travel never produce it. The existing Ultimate timing/grant pipeline handles the on-board unlocked owner, optional choice, borrowed primary and held source-card resolution.

Fight as One's final after-Attack clause publishes the original Hero/Minion beside its borrow request. `BorrowedSkillTargetScope` encodes actor and unit identity in the existing serialized turn-key set; the live and restored rules derive `ActionTargetExclusion` before rebinding choices. It persists through the entire borrowed chain and suspended Skill parents, then clears with the running grant. `TargetQuery.IsPrevented` applies it separately from immunity, so immunity overrides cannot bypass it and presence still counts the unit. Token/space-only Skills remain legal. No schema field, registered capability identity or previous hero definition changes; schema v42's existing corpus remains byte-compatible. Widget's later dealing owns its new content-catalog compatibility decision. Before-Attack borrowing, an Attack clause continuing after its borrow, differently scoped overlapping grants and new operations outside this target-query seam require a fresh extension check.

Milestone 8.41c adds the resolved-card-discard moment for Garrus's Battle Fury. The sole current producer, `discard-resolved-card@1` inside Skill execution, first commits the state transition and cancels the discarded source's Active effects, then yields its typed card outcome before the next node. `ResolvedCardDiscardTriggerQuery` admits only the on-board owner's unlocked watching Ultimate. Its optional trigger grants that exact card through `borrow-named-discarded-primary@1`; the card stays discarded. The existing interrupt frame and borrowed-Skill parent stack preserve nested grants and resume the interrupted clause, including after ordinary repeats or a granted Attack with no target. `TriggeredCard` is rebound from the interrupted frame's completed discard outcome; no second card identity or new snapshot field is stored. Existing schema v42 bytes and dealt content identities remain unchanged. These contracts stay deferred until dealing; a producer outside Skill execution or trigger text continuing after its grant requires a fresh extension check. Core retained-prefix replay is covered here; the configured Garrus Undo/player journey remains the authorized 8.41f gate.

Reusable structured nodes include:

- `Sequence` for exact printed order.
- `Optional` for `may`, `up to`, and other source-defined optionality.
- `Conditional` and presence/target predicates.
- `Choose` for modal one/multiple options and chooser-owned order.
- `RepeatAction` with explicit maximum, repeat ancestry, no recursive repeat, different modal choices allowed, no replacement of the repeated action, and Active-effect uniqueness. Milestone 6.7 implements the last four and reaches the first two by construction rather than by declaring them: non-recursion bounds the whole thing at two instances, so the maximum needs no parameter and the ancestry needs no record beyond the repeated invocation context itself.
- `PerformAction` for text that explicitly grants another action, preserving the difference from a direct effect such as `move`.
- `ReplaceAction` for Fast Travel, Clear, or a verified card replacement.
- `SimultaneousBatch` for one shared choice applied to multiple objects atomically.

Control nodes store stable node IDs so a pending resolution can serialize and resume at the exact location.

Milestone 8.41d stages ordinary, repeated and borrowed Skill hero-Defeat requests before the next effect node, matching the existing minion-Defeat boundary. Card authority completes the consequences, then resumes the saved node continuation; completion does not emit the same request again. Heavy Pilum proves Defeat before its optional Move and no duplicate life spend after restore. This adds no persisted field or schema revision. Hero-removal decisions that can themselves pause are not claimed by this extension.

The target-binding feasibility walk may read a Conditional's branch from a handler that explicitly declares non-mutating anticipated facts, when its inputs are decidable and the actor's position is not already hypothetical. Owned-card presence supplies this contract for Garrus's pursuit attacks: the discard-present branch carries Move and target binding together, while the absent branch binds without movement. Unknown predicates retain the conservative walk. A condition after hypothetical state mutation requires a fresh extension check rather than an assumed proof.

## System-transition capabilities used by cards

Some card effects can trigger or depend on larger systems. These remain dedicated authoritative transitions:

- `ReturnMinionsToBattleZoneAfterAction` applies only to intrinsic minion miniatures and excludes objects that merely count as minions. Each miniature uses a shortest empty path; equal paths use the official team choice. If no path exists or it cannot move, Place it into a nearest empty Battle-Zone space. When both teams require correction, the team holding the Tie Breaker resolves first without flipping it. The checkpoint runs after the entire action and therefore between separate Repeat instances, not after each atomic displacement.
- `RequestLanePush` is emitted only from a recorded Defeat or successful Remove of the last relevant minion; an ordinary Move, Object Push, Swap, or prevented standalone Remove never emits it. A recorded Defeat remains qualifying if its later removal is prevented because prevention does not erase the Defeat outcome. The plain-data request records lane, winning/losing teams, cause outcome ID, immediate/end-of-round context, checkpoint, and state revision.
- `ResolveLanePushTransition` consumes that request through one ordered system transition: flip one Wave counter and stop on the final-Wave victory; otherwise advance the Battle Zone one Zone toward the losing team's Throne and stop on Throne capture; otherwise remove all minions in the previous Battle Zone and respawn the matching type at every verified new-zone Spawn point.
- During that respawn, remove a token covering a Spawn point before spawning. If a non-token obstacle covers a Spawn point, the owning team chooses a nearest empty Battle-Zone space and Places the minion there; when cross-team order matters, the Tie-Breaker-side team starts without flipping the coin.
- An immediate mid-turn request is consumed before a Repeat instance or later initiative action continues. An end-of-round request is consumed only after token/marker cleanup and before card retrieval.
- `ResolveMinionBattle` is implemented at its end-of-round checkpoint. It compares only minions in the declared current starting Battle Zone, completes ties without effect, removes the exact difference or all remaining losing minions, and enforces one-at-a-time Heavy-last selection through round-owned serializable choice state. That choice carries stable resolution/choice/team/Battle-Zone identities, the required count, intrinsic typed candidates, the legal select/deselect sets, the current selection and exact typed invocations; confirming it removes exactly that selection through ordered non-Heavy-before-Heavy events and advances the stage once. Removing the losing team's last minion records an end-of-round `RequestLanePush` that remains unconsumed through cleanup. End-of-round cleanup, card retrieval, mandatory leveling, upgrades, and Pity Coin ordering remain separate system checkpoints.
- Victory evaluation runs immediately at the source-defined Life/Wave/Throne/level condition and can stop later resolution when the game ends.

A card effect requests or triggers these transitions through typed operation facts/checkpoints; it does not duplicate their logic in a card handler. A generic `OccupancyChanged` event is insufficient because Move, Push, Swap, Remove, and Defeat have different systemic consequences.

The implemented repeated-round scaffold uses one serializable end-of-round stage cursor over these stable checkpoint identities. A stage can complete, pause on a typed pending choice, stop on a game result, or report `NotApplicable` from authoritative state, and every one of those outcomes leaves through the same tail: the stage reports what it did, and the round then stops, advances, or closes. A game-ending stage is not an exception to that and must not route around it, or the ordered stage stream skips the step the match ended at.

`NotApplicable` means this match does not run the step at all, which is narrower than it looks: a configured match declares all six of rulebook p.8's ordered steps, so the outcome is reachable only from the board-free synthetic declaration, which has no Lane, no board and no space a token could stand on. "The step ran and had nothing to do" is a completion - a round whose Minion Battle awarded no push completes the push step having pushed nothing. A subsystem declared available without its required handler remains an authority error.

Minion Battle and owner-validated eligible-card retrieval populate the cursor without replacing its continuation or event-order contract. The Minion-Battle choice is owned by the round rather than its stage handler: its identities, losing team/side, required count, intrinsic candidate types, current selection, and exact invocations serialize directly and are rederived/validated after restore. A recorded Lane-Push request prevents the incomplete transition from being silently skipped when its checkpoint arrives. The next round begins only after all applicable stages complete, at which point the prior round's four slots and terminal stage outcomes become immutable history, only round-local state resets, and the projection's current-round event stream is emptied - the whole-match event log and the frozen completed round are what keep the round that ended.
