# Guards Digital - Rules Ledger

## Purpose

This ledger prevents silent rule drift. It connects each game system to its source, approved deviations, implementation status, and focused tests.

## Authority hierarchy

When sources conflict or a rule is ambiguous, use this order:

1. Published second-printing errata or another published official ruling that clearly supersedes the source.
2. Exact card text.
3. *Guards of Atlantis II* second-printing rulebook.
4. Direct publisher or designer clarification.
5. A temporary interpretation explicitly approved by the user and recorded below.

Card text overrides conflicting general rules unless a higher official correction specifically changes that card.

Do not use an unofficial forum answer, convenience behavior, or remembered tabletop practice as authority without verification.

## Status vocabulary

- **Not inventoried:** Required source material has not been checked for completeness.
- **Source reviewed:** The named local source section has been inspected; this does not prove card-corpus completeness or that all applicable errata have been found.
- **Source verified:** Rule or card data has been checked against the authority hierarchy.
- **Architecture specified:** Digital boundaries needed to represent the rule are documented but not implemented.
- **Specified:** Exact digital behavior and edge cases are documented but not implemented.
- **Implemented:** Authoritative rule behavior exists.
- **Focused tests passing:** Narrow automated scenarios cover the implemented behavior.
- **Milestone verified:** The applicable focused, regression, source, presentation, and gate checks from `TESTING.md` pass for the approved milestone.
- **Blocked:** A missing source or decision prevents faithful implementation.

Recorded at 8.2 as the Wasp-scoped question W-05, generalised and **answered from source on 2026-08-22 at milestone 8.9**: what does a card's "discards a card, or is defeated" require - is the second arm a choice, or the consequence of being unable to take the first? It is the consequence of inability, and no choice arises. p.10's Mandatory and Optional Steps settles it: "Steps which include `you may`, `up to` and `if able` are non-mandatory, all other steps are mandatory", and this clause carries none of the three, so the step is mandatory; p.10 stops an action only at a mandatory step that "for any reason you cannot complete", and the printed second arm is what makes this one always completable. p.17's `If Able` entry supplies the contrast the wording is built on - that keyword makes the preceding clause non-mandatory, so `if able` and `or is defeated` are two different answers to the same question, "what if you cannot?", and a card carries exactly one of them. The reading is not a choice for either player: p.10's Making Choices would give an unassigned choice to the card's controller, and its worked example assigns the affected hero only the choice of *which* card, so a choice reading would make the printed words "discards a card" unreachable. It is also not an election by the affected hero, because p.13 shows what the rulebook writes when it means one - "If the attacked hero has no cards in hand, is unable to defend, **or chooses not to**, that hero is defeated" - and this clause says no such thing. It is recorded here rather than in a hero shard because the construction spans heroes: the rulebook illustrates Arien the Tidemaster's Dangerous Current printing it on p.6, and Wasp's Electroblast prints it. No published official correction naming Nimble Strike was located through the [publisher's current rules route](https://wolffdesigna.com/products/guards-of-atlantis-ii-1), the [official second-printing project](https://gamefound.com/en/projects/wolffdesigna/guardsofatlantis2new), its published updates, or a broader indexed correction search; the rulebook itself decides the classification, and a later published official correction supersedes this reading.

The shipped build already conforms, and this was measured rather than asserted: the official single-lane map models the edge as 82 discrete terrain spaces forming a complete ring around 210 playable spaces, every playable space has zero genuinely off-map neighbours, and terrain-neighbour counts run one to three (two spaces at four) - reproducing the designer's own "2-3" figure from the shipped map data. **Standing constraint:** the reading is per-hex and every future map, fixture board and piece of content must keep it so. `TerrainPresenceQuery` answers false for a genuinely off-map coordinate where `ObstacleQuery` answers true; on the official map that divergence is unreachable, but it becomes reachable on any synthetic fixture board without a terrain ring (so a test asserting "not adjacent to terrain" at a synthetic board's rim is asserting the wrong thing), on any future map whose playable region is not fully ringed, and the moment content COUNTS obstacle or terrain adjacency instead of asking a boolean - at which point the off-map arm must be reconciled the way `PushPathQuery` already reconciles it.

Recorded at 8.35z25 on 2026-09-06 from a user ruling given after reading both faces, and **answered**: where a card face prints two or more steps in one card-text moment, in what order are they composed and played? **In the order the card prints them, including when one of those steps is the Attack's own targeting.** A card printing "You may move ... Recorded at 8.35z31 on 2026-09-06 by explicit user decision, and **answered**: what does card text that says "perform the primary action on one of your \<colour\> cards" actually perform? **A full action instance OF THE BORROWED CARD.** Its own printed values, Range, Radius, text and icons apply, including any Active effect its own text grants; the borrowed card does not change state; the borrowed card's supertype carries, so borrowing a basic card performs a basic action; the turn still spends one action; and it is **not** p.10's Repeat, so a restriction scoped to that keyword does not reach it. It is recorded here rather than in a hero shard because two heroes' printed sets consume it - three of Ursafar's faces and one of Tali's - and the build states it once, as `borrow-primary-action@1`, whose offer names the borrowed card so that every clause above follows by construction rather than by copying. A second, narrower ruling of the same date is recorded with it because it is equally roster-wide: **an "If X, do Y" clause whose condition is false simply SKIPS, and the card carries on.** p.10's stop-and-skip applies to steps you attempt and cannot complete, never to conditions that are merely false - so a face whose conditional clause does not fire still resolves the rest of its text. That coexists with the designer's own 2026-07-24 answer that a mandatory action step which cannot be performed does stop the card: a false condition and an impossible step are different things and the page treats them differently.
