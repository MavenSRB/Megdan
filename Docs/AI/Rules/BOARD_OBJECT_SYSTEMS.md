## Board and object rule systems

Printed rulebook page numbers are used below.

| System | Primary source | Current status | Required verification notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Targeting, presence checks, geometry, immunity, and object type | Rulebook pp.9-10, 13, 17-19 plus cards | Targeting, presence, shared immunity, effective type and Radius implemented and milestone verified; straight line and zone queries remain specified; Wasp not inventoried | `ImmunityQuery` combines exact-activity declarations with standing p.13 Heavy immunity and is consulted at candidate and final-mutation time by Attack, direct Move, Object Push, and Place. It uses effective-minion accompaniment while the protected subject remains an intrinsic Heavy. Presence deliberately consults no immunity. `EffectiveUnitKindQuery` applies p.17 replacement and `IntrinsicKindQuery` preserves explicit miniature rules. Milestone 7.2b adds `RadiusQuery`, which asks those same two questions of p.9's inclusive disk, taken from a composed Radius Total with no line of sight, and returns the complete set without making its order a resolution order. |
| Obstacles and occupancy | Rulebook pp.18-19 | Generic, minion, and token obstacles implemented; broader effective-characteristic queries remain | The shared board index enforces one obstacle per space for heroes, minions, generic obstacles, and tokens. Tokens block destinations/transit by declaring their occupant kind, while milestone 6.3 exposes their catalog-owned rules properties as separate continuous sources. Ignore-obstacle behavior, terrain map data, concrete property consumers, and obstacle entry/exit effects remain later work |
| Object Push | Rulebook p.18 plus cards | Implemented at 6.4 with synthetic effects and exposed on the configured Red Needle Skill at 6.5a through a second target-named declaration over the same implementation; no verified card exposes it yet | Straight directly away from the pushing unit through the shared straight-line query; blocked targets move as far as possible, including zero, with transit and endpoint asked as p.18's two separate questions so a permitted target crosses obstacles but still lands empty. An `up to X` multi-target Push records one chosen distance, resolves every target against one pre-effect state, and commits one batch inside the capability. Immunity filters the target set through `RuleActivity.ObjectPush`, which this milestone is the first to read, while presence would still count an immune unit. Tokens are pushed as p.18 requires and hold no immunity of their own, since p.19 says tokens are not units. Object Push is not Lane Push, Movement, or Place, and displacing a minion emits no Lane-Push request |
| Place | Rulebook p.18 plus cards | Implemented at 6.5 as one shared operation and exposed on the configured Red Needle Skill at 6.5a; no verified card exposes it yet | Moves a piece from the board or the supply into a different empty space and is not movement. One immutable `PlacePolicy` states the source container, eligible piece kind, destination query, chooser, and no-destination policy and cannot be constructed with any of them absent. Destinations are geometric, never a route: an inclusive rulebook-distance disk excluding the piece's own space (p.18's "a different space"), or the equally-nearest empty spaces of a declared set, returned as ties because p.18 gives that choice to the piece's own team. The general capability, milestone 6.2's token placement, and milestone 6.4a's correction fallback are three declarations against the one operation |
| Spawn and Respawn | Rulebook pp.7-8, 19 plus cards | General unit Spawn implemented at 6.5; p.7 Hero Respawn subset at 6.5aa; minion Respawn remains scheduled | General Spawn retains its supply, own-point, token-clearance, and obstacle contract. Hero Respawn derives the optional turn-opening destinations from empty own-side Hero Spawn points inside the Throne Zone, falling back to nearest empty adjacent spaces only when required, and offers resolve-without-action. p.8/p.18 minion Respawn and full defeat integration remain later work. |
| Board-object Swap and Replace | Rulebook p.19 plus cards | Implemented at 6.6 with synthetic effects; no verified card exposes either yet | Swap exchanges two objects' places in one transaction and is neither movement nor placement; Replace is one typed substitution that commits in a space never left empty, so it is not a Remove followed by a Place. Both stay distinct from action replacement and from card upgrade. What each decides and records is stated once in [Project/BOARD_OPERATIONS.md](../Project/BOARD_OPERATIONS.md) |
| Remove, Defeat, prevention, and Clear | Rulebook pp.8, 12-14, 18-19 plus cards | Minion and hero consequences, Clear token removal, and general Remove implemented; verified at 7.4 | Remove has no defeat reward; Defeat is staged through rewards and a preventable removal attempt, carrying separate typed facts. Clear removes chosen adjacent tokens through a distinct replacement action. The general Remove declares an eligible piece kind and destination, reports removed or removal-prevented, collects no coins, stages no Defeat, and admits no hero, because p.13 takes a hero off the board through Defeat instead - and p.13's Defeat, minion and hero alike, asks that same one removal decision rather than hard-coding its result, so a subject whose removal is prevented keeps its recorded Defeat and its collected coins, which is p.13's own Bolster wording. The only prevention source implemented is declared immunity asked immediately before the mutation; the general prevent-removal family that example names needs verified card text no source can yet supply. A successful standalone Remove or Defeat of the last effective Battle-Zone minion of a side records p.8's qualifying cause, naming which of its two sentences applies, and a prevented one records none |
| Token instances, supply, properties, and interactions | Rulebook pp.8, 18-19 plus cards | Instance, supply, occupancy, placement/reclamation, persistent properties, Clear, and spawn-over-token implemented; broader interactions remain; Wasp not inventoried | Every placed token has a stable identity, a trusted definition reference, a finite derived supply, occupancy and authoritative placement/reclamation, and its definition declares properties: each token exposes stable continuous sources for its own lifetime, survives its placing hero leaving the board, and loses them only when removed. Clear atomically removes any chosen adjacent-token subset including none, and an already validated unit Spawn removes a token covering its configured Spawn point first. Displacement, Swap, Replace and the general Remove all operate over token instances; a second token definition exists because p.19's Replace requires a different object and substituting two instances of one definition would change nothing the rules can see. p.8's end-of-round cleanup runs over every placed token, against the removal condition p.19's "unless the card text says otherwise" names, which carries only the baseline member no verified text yet overrides |
| Marker attachments, properties, and return | Rulebook pp.8, 13, 18-19 plus cards | Attachment, supply, explicit return state, the return transition, and both general cleanup checkpoints implemented and milestone verified; Wasp not inventoried | A marker attachment records owner, holder, source and return condition with no coordinate of any kind, which is p.18's "unlike tokens, markers are usually not placed on the board" made structural. Giving one is an authoritative choice of holder against a finite supply; returning one is a transition rather than a choice, because p.8 and p.13 return markers to every applicable holder rather than to a chosen one. Both checkpoints read one shared statement of which markers each takes: p.8's end-of-round stage returns every attachment declaring the general cleanup, whoever holds it, and p.13's "Any markers they had are returned" fires at the recorded Hero Defeat and returns only what the defeated hero held, so a marker that hero gave away stays with its holder. The defeat return touches no token, because p.19 says a token outlives the hero who placed it. No effect currently gives a marker in a configured live action |
| Minions outside Battle Zone | Rulebook p.18 | Source reviewed; architecture specified; core authority milestone 6.4a and player-facing choice milestone 6.4b scheduled after Object Push | Applies only to intrinsic minion miniatures, not objects that merely count as minions; correct after the whole action by shortest empty path with team choice, or Place in a nearest empty Battle-Zone space when blocked. Tie-Breaker-side team resolves rare ordering cases first without a coin flip; presentation reuses 6.1a's shared authority-owned minion-choice interface and only projects/forwards correction-specific candidates |
