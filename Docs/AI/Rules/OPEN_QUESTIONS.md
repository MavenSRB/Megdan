11. Recorded at 6.5b, answered on 2026-08-16 by explicit user decision: may a card's `Choose one` offer an option the actor cannot perform at all? Hands-on validation found that it does - both of Red Bombard's modes are unperformable from the saved scene's starting position, and choosing either spends the card in silence. The local rulebook was searched on 2026-08-16 with `pdftotext`, which reads the second-printing PDF here even though page rendering does not - the earlier claim that it could not be searched was wrong and is corrected here rather than left standing. That search found no clause either way: page 10 says only to choose a bullet point and apply its text, and no page states whether an option the actor cannot perform is offerable. The approved interpretation is therefore still recorded as an interpretation rather than presented as sourced text: an option the actor cannot perform is refused rather than offered, and the player is told that it cannot be done rather than left to discover it by spending a card. A later official clarification supersedes this under the authority hierarchy. Corrective milestone 6.5ac implements it: the option is projected greyed and unselectable, with no reason text by the same decision's refinement, and where no option of a choice can be performed the node reported itself impossible rather than pausing, so in that one case the player was not told before the action stopped - recorded in 6.5ac's completion evidence with the reason. Corrective milestone 6.5ae closed it on the user's 2026-08-16 decision that End Turn is the one legal command there, offered rather than performed: a mandatory choice with nothing performable now pauses with every option greyed and offers that stop, and the player clicks it. The decision is a decision about presentation rather than about the rule, so p.10's stop is unchanged in what it does. A further search on 2026-08-16, prompted by the user asking what the rules already do about a card that can achieve nothing, found the clause the earlier search had missed, and it supports rather than contradicts the interpretation: **p.12's Hold action**. Hold has no icon, can be performed with any Action card, and means doing nothing that turn; the page names an impossible or disadvantageous action as the common reason to perform one, and states that because any card can Hold, a player is never forced to perform an action. That does not settle whether an unperformable option may be *offered inside a choice*, which is what this question asks and which remains an approved interpretation. What it does settle is the surrounding expectation: the rules assume a player can see that an action is impossible before choosing it, which is why corrective milestone 6.5af judges a Skill when its action is offered rather than after it is started. **AMENDED 2026-09-06 at milestone 8.35z35 by the ruling row 23 now carries, which is the "later official clarification" this row reserved: the question this row answered as one is two questions, and they take different answers.** An option that **cannot be performed** - a mandatory step of its branch stops the resolution - is still refused rather than offered, still projected greyed with its blocker named, and a node whose every option is such an option still pauses with 6.5ae's stop; that half is this row's decision and it is unchanged. An option that **can be taken and would apply nothing** is now offered, and choosing it is a legal no-op that completes the node and lets the rest of the card run. The p.12 Hold clause this row already found supports the split rather than the collapse: a player is never forced to perform an action, so a bullet that does nothing is a move the page expects to exist, and on a modal the card hands to an opponent it is the escape hatch that decision exists to offer. **Red Bombard is unaffected and this row's measurement stands exactly as recorded**: both of its modes terminate in mandatory steps - the direct-move branch's target selection and the spawn - so both are blocked rather than empty, verified at 8.35z35 from the composition rather than from the wording.

Recorded at 8.2 as the Wasp-scoped question W-05, generalised and **answered from source on 2026-08-22 at milestone 8.9**: what does a card's "discards a card, or is defeated" require - is the second arm a choice, or the consequence of being unable to take the first? It is the consequence of inability, and no choice arises. p.10's Mandatory and Optional Steps settles it: "Steps which include `you may`, `up to` and `if able` are non-mandatory, all other steps are mandatory", and this clause carries none of the three, so the step is mandatory; p.10 stops an action only at a mandatory step that "for any reason you cannot complete", and the printed second arm is what makes this one always completable. p.17's `If Able` entry supplies the contrast the wording is built on - that keyword makes the preceding clause non-mandatory, so `if able` and `or is defeated` are two different answers to the same question, "what if you cannot?", and a card carries exactly one of them. The reading is not a choice for either player: p.10's Making Choices would give an unassigned choice to the card's controller, and its worked example assigns the affected hero only the choice of *which* card, so a choice reading would make the printed words "discards a card" unreachable. It is also not an election by the affected hero, because p.13 shows what the rulebook writes when it means one - "If the attacked hero has no cards in hand, is unable to defend, **or chooses not to**, that hero is defeated" - and this clause says no such thing. It is recorded here rather than in a hero shard because the construction spans heroes: the rulebook illustrates Arien the Tidemaster's Dangerous Current printing it on p.6, and Wasp's Electroblast prints it. The general rule is stated in the [Rule-system ledger](RULE_SYSTEMS.md#rule-system-ledger)'s ordered mandatory/optional row. **Milestone 8.10 implemented Wasp's instance on that reading**: Electroblast's rider is a discard that reports whether the chosen hero had a card, and one conditional arm running p.13's Defeat list where they did not - a branch, and no question either player is asked. A later official ruling supersedes the reading. An official designer ruling of 2026-07-06, cross-checked at milestone 8.35z21, corroborates the neighbouring `if able` half of that same row from the other direction - without a `may`, `if able` forces the step and opens no choice - and is recorded there rather than here.

Recorded at 8.35z21 on 2026-09-06 from an official designer ruling (rules channel `#rules-questions-goa2`, 2026-07-30), and **answered**: how does the board edge behave for adjacency and obstacle purposes - as one edge entity, or as hexes? **As multiple virtual obstacle/terrain hexes, one per adjacent edge hex**, so a coastal space can be adjacent to two or three of them rather than to a single edge. The shipped build already conforms, and this was measured rather than asserted: the official single-lane map models the edge as 82 discrete terrain spaces forming a complete ring around 210 playable spaces, every playable space has zero genuinely off-map neighbours, and terrain-neighbour counts run one to three (two spaces at four) - reproducing the designer's own "2-3" figure from the shipped map data. **Standing constraint:** the reading is per-hex and every future map, fixture board and piece of content must keep it so. `TerrainPresenceQuery` answers false for a genuinely off-map coordinate where `ObstacleQuery` answers true; on the official map that divergence is unreachable, but it becomes reachable on any synthetic fixture board without a terrain ring (so a test asserting "not adjacent to terrain" at a synthetic board's rim is asserting the wrong thing), on any future map whose playable region is not fully ringed, and the moment content COUNTS obstacle or terrain adjacency instead of asking a boolean - at which point the off-map arm must be reconciled the way `PushPathQuery` already reconciles it.

Recorded at 8.35z31 on 2026-09-06 by explicit user decision, and **answered**: what does card text that says "perform the primary action on one of your \<colour\> cards" actually perform? **A full action instance OF THE BORROWED CARD.** Its own printed values, Range, Radius, text and icons apply, including any Active effect its own text grants; the borrowed card does not change state; the borrowed card's supertype carries, so borrowing a basic card performs a basic action; the turn still spends one action; and it is **not** p.10's Repeat, so a restriction scoped to that keyword does not reach it. That coexists with the designer's own 2026-07-24 answer that a mandatory action step which cannot be performed does stop the card: a false condition and an impossible step are different things and the page treats them differently.

The unpredicted one, found by probe before anything was written: milestone 8.25d enforces p.10's bar for card text as a single boolean about the enclosing attack, so a second clause would have been skipped in silence inside the very instance it exists to fire in, whatever it declared. Both have ONE cause. Card text is always started with no completed repeats recorded against its frame, so the bounded-chain counter every Skill repeat uses reads zero in every Attack instance, and 8.25d's boolean is a SUBSTITUTE for a counter that path never had. The answer is to give it one that costs nothing: the chain already mints each instance's identity by appending to the last one's - which milestone 8.25d documented as what makes each identity unique, because p.13's Defeat outcome pays once per identity - so the chain position is already restorable state, and reading it moves no schema version. A card printing ONE clause therefore behaves exactly as it did before, which is every face in this build but Rain of Arrows, and a test asserts that containment over every composed hero rather than trusting it. Recorded here rather than in her shard because the next face to print a chain inherits it.

The fix is not a new capability - `SyntheticTargetingCapabilities.ChooseTargetV4` already exists for exactly this shape, taking the distance as a literal (`range`) the definition writes rather than a total the roster reads off state, and Red Needle's Push and Blue Sentinel's Swap already name their own adjacent object through it. Recorded here rather than in her shard because the next face that prints a fixed, un-iconed distance inherits it.

34. Recorded at 8.37a, **open**, and implemented under a named interpretation: at the END of the turn that played it, is a card that has been played and fully resolved "in your discard"? No located source answers it, because at the table the question does not arise - a played card lies face-up in front of its owner and nobody distinguishes that pile from the discard mid-round. This build does: `CardLocation.TurnArea` holds a played card for the round and `CardLocation.DiscardPile` is reserved for a card SPENT as a rulebook p.14 Defense or taken by a discard clause, and the two piles are already read differently by the roster (`RosterCardRetrievalKind.OwnedDiscardedCard` admits only the spent card). **Tali's Blizzard is the first face to ask about ITSELF after its own resolution** - "If Blizzard is in your discard, End of turn: May repeat once" - so the narrow reading is false by construction and makes the whole second sentence dead text on every board.
