# Guards Digital - Player-Facing Match HUD

Canonical active shard. The player-facing HUD the configured match draws - its panel, its standing regions, the card tray, and the one contextual region every decision is asked in. Split out of [CONFIGURED_PRESENTATION.md](CONFIGURED_PRESENTATION.md) at milestone 7.8a, which is the response governance names for sustained growth: that shard reached 90% of its word threshold as this milestone landed, and moving precise active authority to an indexed shard is the normal answer rather than compressing it.

## The panel and its regions

Milestone 7.8a rehouses every surface above on one runtime UI Toolkit panel, by the recorded user decision, and the retained visual tree replaces the immediate-mode drawing the HUD used from milestone 4.4c to here. `ConfiguredMatchHudRenderer` still owns the drawing surface and still holds no session; it creates the panel and its layout in code rather than from assets, lays out five absolutely-placed regions from pure functions of the viewport, and answers where the pointer is blocked from the live element bounds with those same rects as the fallback.

The settings object is the one exception, and milestone 7.9 made it one. Unity's text shaper needs ICU data, and the player only ships that data when the **build contains at least one `PanelSettings` asset** - the player says exactly that in its own log. A HUD that built its settings entirely with `ScriptableObject.CreateInstance` gave the build no such asset, so the data was absent and every label the HUD redrew raised a `NullReferenceException` out of `UITKTextHandle.ShapeText`. The Editor never showed it, because the Editor has the data loaded either way.

`Assets/Resources/UI/GuardsRuntimePanelSettings.asset` is therefore committed, and the panel is copied from it with `Instantiate` when it loads, falling back to a bare instance when it does not. Two things are worth knowing before anyone edits it. The asset's own `m_ICUDataAsset` field is empty on disk and stays empty - it is not what carries the data; its presence in the build is. And it is instantiated rather than used directly, so the per-panel values set beside it stay runtime state and never dirty a tracked asset. The layout is still code-owned, which is what the paragraph above is about. The player-build smoke gate is what found this, and only because milestone 7.9 made its smoke path play whole rounds instead of accepting one command - one redraw never reached the shaper. Every presenter, view model, typed invocation, and passive-view interface is unchanged: the migration stops at the `I*View` seam, which is why it changed no rule, no command, and no legality.

The standing HUD is the approved [milestone 7.8a mockup](../References/MILESTONE_7_8A_UI_MOCKUP.html), with the hero rail's own layout coming from the approved [milestone 8.7a current-card rail mockup](../References/MILESTONE_8_7A_CURRENT_CARD_RAIL_MOCKUP.png). One status strip carries round and turn, both teams' Life counters, the Wave counter, and the Tie Breaker, which is relocated into it rather than dropped - the same counters and revealed side that the retired top-right box guaranteed, with the whole of it still available as one line for the chip's assistive reading. Two compact rails sit on the screen edges, one per projected hero and repeated rather than a pair of singletons: a team-coloured disc bearing the hero's initial from an optional configured icon field, the projected display name with `LVL n` above it, p.16's item bonuses as compact channel-abbreviation chips carrying a signed value, four fixed slots under a `RESOLVED TURNS` caption drawing each resolved card as a compact card-shaped icon in that card's authoritative colour, and a discard row holding one such icon per projected discarded card. Both rails together take less than half the width, so the board stays the dominant surface. A small `DIRECTOR` toggle opens a compact drawer holding `UNDO`, `RESET` - presentation terminology for the existing Restart - and `EVENT LOG`; opening or closing it submits nothing.

The rail on the right edge is the one on the left mirrored rather than repeated - the user's 2026-08-21 decision - so both read outward from the board and each hero's marker sits on its rail's inner corner. Two rows are deliberately not mirrored: the four resolved-turn slots, because turns one to four are a sequence rather than a decoration, and the discard row, because a pile reads left to right on either edge and two piles are compared side by side rather than as each other's reflection. Which team a hero is on is not written on the rail; two edges, two accent colours and the hero disc's own hover and assistive reading carry it, which is the treatment milestone 8.5a gives a card's printed colour.

## The card in play, and the four turns behind it

Milestone 8.7a splits what the rail shows into the card a hero has in play now and the turns already behind them. A dedicated marker in the header's inner corner holds the current card while it is committed or revealed and unresolved; the four numbered slots are resolved turn history, and a turn is numbered only once that turn's exact card instance reaches `CardResolutionStatus.Resolved`. Before this, all four slots occupied themselves the moment a card was revealed, because the round records its assignment history inside the same command as p.7's joint reveal - so the rail presented a turn as history while it was still being played.

Both halves are one question asked of the card's own resolution status, and nothing here remembers an answer, which is what makes the split survive a snapshot restore, a deterministic replay, a retained-prefix Undo and a restart without a presentation-owned lifetime. The tray asks the same question: one turn is resolved hero by hero in Initiative order, so a real position exists with the first hero's card resolved and the second's not, and milestone 8.5b's "card in play" reading had to be qualified by the same status or the two surfaces would have disagreed about one card. That card's lifecycle line reads `RESOLVING` wherever it is drawn.

A facedown commitment is a neutral card back and the word `FACEDOWN`, and it carries no card at all rather than a card drawn differently. That is the information boundary rather than a drawing choice: the projection itself refuses to name a committed instance until joint reveal, so the marker is built from the commitment status alone and could not leak the card even from the director's complete definition list. A revealed marker carries the card's own printed colour and p.7's Initiative and no caption - the user's 2026-08-21 decision, the word `RESOLVING` staying in its hover and assistive reading and on the revealed face's own lifecycle band. The marker's footprint is always reserved whether or not a card is in it, so the header does not move four times a round.

Resting the pointer on an occupied turn slot, on a discard icon, or on a revealed marker reveals that card's whole printed face beside the rail it belongs to - the user's 2026-08-21 decision, and the reason those slots stop being the one place a spent card is unreadable. It is drawn by the same builder the tray draws a card with, so one card cannot look like two, and one preview element serves all three so two faces can never stand at once and moving between icons replaces the one that is up. The reveal is a reading of the model the rail was already rendered from: it submits nothing, adds no focus stop or keyboard-order change, is dropped whenever the rail is redrawn, and an empty slot or a facedown marker reveals nothing rather than an empty card. Each icon's assistive label names the exact card and where it is in the card's lifecycle, so the fact is available without a pointer.

Milestone 8.11b is what made the discard row a third revealable surface, and it took the last two numbers off the rail with it. The four slots carried the ordinals `1` to `4` and the discard row carried a count beside one pip per colour present, so a slot said which turn it was and not what was played there, and the pile said how big it was and not what was in it - two of one colour read as one pip. Each is now one compact card-shaped icon per card, in the card's own printed colour, carrying no text at all: sequence is the row's fixed left-to-right positions, the count is how many icons there are, and everything else is one pointer rest away at full size. An unoccupied resolved-turn slot stays an empty outline that reveals nothing and answers no pointer, because a turn a hero has not finished is not a card. The pile has no authoritative order and none is invented from discard events the presenter watched: the icons are ordered by card instance identity, which is what the tray already orders its own discarded group by, so one card sits in the same place on both surfaces and the order survives a restore and a replay.

A resolved slot whose card is still sourcing a live Active effect wears the same violet outline and the same word `ACTIVE` milestone 8.5b gives that card in the tray, derived from the one projected active-source list, so both surfaces mark and unmark it on the same refresh. The outline is drawn around every slot and only its colour changes, and the word's line is always present and only its text changes, so nothing moves when an effect starts or ends.

The board framing reserves a share of the viewport width along the left edge, where the tray stands, also by that decision: the map is framed into the band that is left rather than centred in the whole viewport, and widens only when it no longer fits there. The reserve was set by eye to where the board looked right rather than to the value that would clear the tray completely, so a little of the map is still behind the cards' corner. The official map is close to square and the viewport is 16:9, so most of what the tray needs is width the board was never using. [CONFIGURED_PRESENTATION.md](CONFIGURED_PRESENTATION.md) owns the framing itself.

## The card tray

The card tray shows one hero's whole current round, colour-coded from each card's own projected `CardColor` and never from team, hero, hand position, display name, art, or action type, with the colour's name in the card's own assistive reading. It sorts already played cards in ascending turn order, then the ones still available, then the discarded ones; a played card reads `PLAYED · TURN <n>` and never names a round, an available card sits visibly higher through a margin that changes no hit target or keyboard order, and the three groups are read from projected card lifecycle plus the round's own assignment history rather than from movement the presenter watched. Which hero it shows is local state with no control of its own outside the map: it follows the hero the match is waiting on, and a map click pins another until the awaited hero changes. A hero click is offered to the authority first, so a legal Attack target stays a spatial decision, and only a click the authority has no use for becomes a view selection.

## What one tray card shows

A tray card is portrait, and draws its whole printed face in the regions the approved [milestone 8.5a mockup](../References/MILESTONE_8_5A_CARD_TRAY_MOCKUP.png) gives them: rulebook p.7's Initiative on a banner hanging from the card's upper-left corner, the card's name centred beside it in the same header strip, every printed value belonging to an alternate action in a secondary strip, the primary action's kind with its own numeric value attached to it, the exact words the source face prints on a light rules panel, and the lifecycle label along the bottom. p.9's one Range or Radius icon sits on a badge at the panel's lower left, because the page prints it beside a primary action rather than as one of its numbers. The banner is taken out of the layout flow and its width is reserved on *both* sides of the title, which is the only arrangement that keeps the title concentric with the card and clear of the banner at once: laid out beside the banner in a row the title was pushed off centre, and centred in a box that merely started clear of it the title broke long names mid-word. Milestone 8.7a moved the title up into that strip, following the current-card rail mockup, and measures the reservation from the band's own edge rather than inside its inset - reserving a whole banner width inside the inset costs twelve pixels the longest printed name needs. Each band above the rules panel has a floor height, so five cards of one hand line their values, actions and panels up with each other even when a name takes two lines, and the tray's own actions - `PASS` and `CONFIRM FACEDOWN` - sit at the top right of its frame where they stay in view and do not move when a card is selected.

Selecting a card changes the outline's colour and never its width. That is not a detail: a card's width is fixed and its height comes from its content, so the shared treatment's one-pixel-to-three widening re-wrapped the selected card's rules text, made it taller, and stretched the whole row with it.

## A card whose Active effect is live

A card that is the source of at least one Active-effect instance currently in `ActiveEffectPhase.Active` takes a violet outline outside its own edge and the word `ACTIVE` beside its lifecycle label. Milestone 8.5b. The outline is drawn on a slot around the card rather than on the card, because the card's own border is the shared selection treatment and both states can stand together - a player can be holding a selected card while an effect they started last turn is still running, and two meanings on one border would be one meaning lost. The slot is always drawn and only its colour changes, so nothing moves when an effect starts or ends, and the violet is the one hue on this HUD that is neither a printed card colour nor selection's cyan nor the confirm accent's orange.

Which cards those are is `MatchSessionProjection.ActiveEffectSourceCardInstanceIds`, derived by the application from the round authority's own Active-effect lifecycle: only `Active` instances count, one card appears once however many of its instances are live, and a card whose definition merely *could* create an effect is not in it. Presentation asks the projection and infers nothing. That is what makes the indicator survive a snapshot restore, a deterministic replay and a retained-prefix Undo without a presentation-owned lifetime or a new serialized answer - each of those paths rebuilds the lifecycle it already preserved, and the same list falls out.

The card the current turn is being resolved from is drawn at full strength. It carries no commitment command - the round is past choosing - and it takes no focus stop, but "carries no command" and "is spent" are different facts, and the shared unavailable treatment was saying the second about the first.

Which region a value belongs to is decided by the action it is printed on and by its `PrintedValueChannel`, never by the card's name and never by reading the sentence: the primary action is the one whose origin says `PrintedPrimary`, the secondary strip is every other action the card itself prints, the two qualifiers a label can carry are p.13's Ranged classification and p.14's Block, and p.16's Ultimate - which prints no Initiative and has no action at all - reads its icon from the permanent ability instead. That rule is what keeps this build without a printed-text parser, and `MatchHudPresentationTests` asserts the four regions partition the definition's printed values exactly, each drawn once.

The words themselves are never colour-coded, exactly as a printed card's are not, and the three states `CardSourceContent` distinguishes stay three: transcribed words are shown verbatim and complete, a read face that prints none says so, and a synthetic fixture with no source face says that instead. Every plate a card draws - flag, bands, badge - is a shift of the card's own colour away from that colour's text colour rather than a uniform darkening, so contrast can only rise; the ratios are measured per printed colour rather than eyeballed. Every band above the rules panel has a floor height, so five cards of one hand line their values, actions and panels up with each other even when one card's name takes two lines.

The card's printed colour is not written on its face - the user's 2026-08-21 decision - because a card wearing its colour and also spelling it says the same thing twice, five times over, in the space the printed text needs. The word is not lost: `ColorName` stays on the view model and in each card's hover and assistive reading, which is exactly where milestone 7.8a already keeps the colour of a hero rail's turn slot. Nothing on this surface means something by colour alone.

## The one contextual region

One contextual region above the tray presents whatever the match is currently asking, and the tray is hidden rather than merely inert while a mandatory decision stands, so exactly one interaction context is usable. Milestone 8.8a draws p.15's Upgrade a Card there as cards rather than as labelled controls, because the decision is a choice between cards: each option is the candidate it would put in the hand, drawn by the tray's own face builder so one card cannot look like two, and the whole face is the control. The six sit in a grid of one column per upgradable card, two candidates deep, and the columns are read Blue, Green, Red. That colour order is the one ordering presentation decides here and it is stated once in `MatchHudTheme.ColorReadingOrder`: the pause orders its pairs by card instance identity, which is a different left-to-right order for every hero, while which options exist, which pair each belongs to, and which candidate is drawn above the other all stay the projection's. A candidate is a card in the deck and its lifecycle band says `IN DECK`, which is `TrayCardGroup`'s fourth member and the only place it is produced. Under each face is the one fact neither card carries: p.16's item, printed on the candidate that option does *not* take, so choosing between two cards of a colour is also choosing which item the hero ends the round holding. The card returned to the deck and the sibling set aside stay in each option's hover and assistive reading rather than being written under six faces - the user's 2026-08-22 decision, on the same ground as milestone 8.5a's printed colour. Two rows of whole card faces are taller than the share an ordinary one-row tray may take, so while this grid stands the region is bounded by the height under the status strip instead; nothing competes for it, and the board's framing is computed from the ordinary bound either way, so the map does not move when the prompt opens or closes. The `DIRECTOR` toggle moves for the same interval, to the band beside the Red rail under the status strip, with its drawer opening downward: the enlarged region would otherwise cover it, and it is the only route to Undo, which a mandatory decision does not make less legitimate. The bound reserves that band, so the toggle is clear even when the grid takes all of it. That region now also draws the paused multi-step system transition no view drew before - a gap open since milestone 6.11 built rulebook p.8's Lane-Push respawn pause, and made ordinary by milestone 7.6, because p.8's Pity Coin leaves a hero able to afford a level and p.15's Upgrade a Card then stops the end of round on a real choice from the second round onward. The options and their meanings are projected: the rules that paused describe their own pause as typed facts - hero, level, paid cost, remaining coins, and per option the card returned to the deck, the card taken, the sibling set aside and the printed icon whose dashboard slot makes it an item, or the destination space p.8's sidebar Place offers - and presentation composes the words from those facts without ever decoding an option identity. Answering forwards the invocation the continuation itself projected; an option this prompt did not project is refused before it reaches the authority. No End Turn and no end-of-round advance is drawn beside a pause, because the authority projects no stop and would refuse the advance. The scene runtime's milestone 7.6 command surface is retained explicitly as the director and headless path, for the build-smoke driver and every fixture that reaches a pause without a pointer.

Milestone 8.11b brought p.11's Defense response onto the same footing, for the same reason: it is a choice between cards in the defender's own hand, and it was being asked as two lines of abbreviated text while those same cards stood on the tray as whole faces. Every legal offer is now drawn by that shared builder, at one fixed width so choosing one cannot re-wrap or resize another, and the whole face forwards exactly the invocation the authority projected for it. Nothing about the decision moved onto the card: the authority-computed Attack/Defense/result preview - including the offer-bound `PRINTED DEFENSE` reading and milestone 8.11's Block verdicts - is still what hover and keyboard focus raise, still read from the offer's own projected breakdown, and presentation recomputes no total, no eligibility and no predicted outcome. Visible order is the projected response order, which is the keyboard order because the controls are added in it. `DECLINE DEFENSE` is about no card, carries none, and stays the compact content-sized control it was. One row of four faces plus Decline fits milestone 5.1c's 1280-pixel bound and the tray's ordinary height at the validated desktop viewport, so this response needs no taller context than it has always had and short viewports keep the existing bounded scroll.
