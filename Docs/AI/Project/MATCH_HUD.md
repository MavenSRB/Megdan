# Guards Digital - Player-Facing Match HUD

Canonical active shard. The player-facing HUD the configured match draws - its panel, its standing regions, the card tray, and the one contextual region every decision is asked in. Split out of [CONFIGURED_PRESENTATION.md](CONFIGURED_PRESENTATION.md) at milestone 7.8a, when that shard reached 90% of its word threshold and governance's answer to sustained growth is an indexed shard rather than compression.

[CONFIGURED_PRESENTATION.md](CONFIGURED_PRESENTATION.md) keeps the board-facing surfaces - highlights, board cues, retained hero/minion/token views, and the projections behind them - and the presentation contract itself stays in [CURRENT_IMPLEMENTATION.md](CURRENT_IMPLEMENTATION.md): that presentation is passive, forwards only projected typed invocations, and withholds what the authority would refuse. ## The panel and its regions

Milestone 7.8a rehouses every surface above on one runtime UI Toolkit panel, by the recorded user decision, replacing the immediate-mode drawing the HUD used from 4.4c. `ConfiguredMatchHudRenderer` owns the drawing surface and holds no session: it creates the panel and its layout in code, lays out five absolutely-placed regions from pure functions of the viewport, and answers where the pointer is blocked from live element bounds with those rects as the fallback.

Unity's text shaper ships ICU data only when the build contains a `PanelSettings` asset. The committed `Assets/Resources/UI/GuardsRuntimePanelSettings.asset` supplies that build dependency and is copied with `Instantiate` (bare instance as fallback), keeping per-panel values runtime-only; its `m_ICUDataAsset` remains empty on disk because asset presence carries the data. Layout stays code-owned behind the `I*View` seam, with no rule, command or legality change.

The standing HUD follows the approved [7.8a mockup](../References/MILESTONE_7_8A_UI_MOCKUP.html), [8.7a rail mockup](../References/MILESTONE_8_7A_CURRENT_CARD_RAIL_MOCKUP.png), and 8.29a plaque composition. Its 96-unit status strip is a 56-unit identity row over a 40-unit presenter-derived narration lane, carrying round/turn, both Life counters, Wave and Tie Breaker with the same assistive reading. A centred 160-unit solid side-coloured Tie Breaker coin is clipped at the strip edges, has no white outline, says `TIEBREAKER`, and retains the complete revealed-side tooltip; clipped geometry neither paints nor blocks the map. The full-width gold lane uses its warm background only for `YourMove`, behind the coin and rectangular 264-by-96 End Turn aligned to the responsive Blue rail.

Full-height edge rails hold three projected hero plaques each. Every rounded team-edged plaque leads with a 50-unit official face in a 56-unit medallion, bold 16-point display name and `LVL n`; inset bands show p.16 item bonuses as signed printed symbols, four fixed authoritative-colour resolved-card icons, and one icon per discard. Its two-unit top selection edge is always reserved, so changing the viewed hero changes colour only. Both rails remain below half the viewport width. `DIRECTOR` opens a compact non-commanding drawer with `UNDO`, `RESET` (Restart) and `EVENT LOG`. When one hand card is selected, `CONFIRM FACEDOWN` appears alone 18 units below the resolved toggle and stays absent until real geometry exists, preventing origin flashes during resize or late layout.

The rail on the right edge is the one on the left mirrored rather than repeated - the user's 2026-08-21 decision - so both read outward from the board and each hero's marker sits on its rail's inner corner. Two rows are deliberately not mirrored: the four resolved-turn slots, because turns one to four are a sequence rather than a decoration, and the discard row, because a pile reads left to right on either edge. Which team a hero is on is not written on the rail; the two edges, the accent colours and the hero disc's own hover and assistive reading carry it, which is the treatment milestone 8.5a gives a card's printed colour. Hero identity is permanently linked across surfaces: pointing at a plaque gives that hero's board piece the warm-ivory six-corner glow, while pointing at the board piece changes only the plaque's permanently reserved two-unit top edge to ivory. The fixed edge leaves the plaque, portrait, text, rows and neighbours stationary. The same channel also accepts a tied-Initiative hero button and survives ordinary rail/view recomposition without submitting a command.

## The card in play, and the four turns behind it

Milestone 8.7a splits what the rail shows into the card a hero has in play now and the turns behind them. A marker in the header's inner corner holds the current card while it is committed or revealed and unresolved; the four numbered slots are resolved turn history, and a turn is numbered only once that turn's exact card instance reaches `CardResolutionStatus.Resolved`. Before this the slots filled at reveal, presenting a turn as history while it was still being played.

Both halves are one question asked of the card's own resolution status, and nothing here remembers an answer, which is what makes the split survive restore, replay, Undo and restart without a presentation-owned lifetime. The tray asks the same question: a turn resolves hero by hero in Initiative order, so a real position exists with one hero's card resolved and another's not, and milestone 8.5b's "card in play" reading had to be qualified by the same status or the two surfaces would have disagreed about one card. That card's lifecycle line reads `RESOLVING` wherever it is drawn.

A facedown commitment is a neutral card back and the word `FACEDOWN`, carrying no card at all rather than a card drawn differently. That is the information boundary: the projection refuses to name a committed instance until joint reveal, so the marker is built from the commitment status alone and cannot leak the card. A revealed marker carries the card's printed colour and p.7's Initiative and no caption - the user's 2026-08-21 decision, `RESOLVING` staying in its hover and, when the generated fallback is in use, on that face's lifecycle band. Its one- or two-digit Initiative label absolutely fills the fixed marker so both axes centre without changing the badge. A prepared printed front adds no synthetic lifecycle strip. The marker's footprint is reserved whether or not a card is in it, so the header does not move four times a round.

Resting the pointer on an occupied turn slot, on a discard icon, or on a revealed marker reveals that card's whole printed face beside the rail it belongs to - the user's 2026-08-21 decision. It is drawn by the tray's one artwork-aware complete-face builder, so one card cannot look like two: the exact private front appears when prepared and the generated face appears otherwise. One preview element serves all three, so two faces never stand at once. The reveal is a reading of the model the rail was already rendered from: it submits nothing, adds no focus stop, is dropped whenever the rail is redrawn, and an empty slot or a facedown marker reveals nothing rather than an empty card. Each icon's assistive label names the exact card and where it is in its lifecycle, so the fact is available without a pointer.

Milestone 8.15 added the one card a hero owns that is neither in the round nor in the pile: a card standing in rulebook p.16's Ultimate area draws one more captioned mini icon on its hero's rail, under `ULTIMATE`, revealing the same full printed face every other rail icon reveals and reading `UNLOCKED` in its assistive label and, on the generated fallback, its lifecycle band - `TrayCardGroup`'s fifth member, produced only here, because every other lifecycle word claims a turn, a hand, a pile or a deck the unlocked card is exactly not in. It reads the card's own projected location - the unlock *is* the position, by milestone 7.3's modelling - so it appears when p.15's seventh level turns the card faceup, survives restore, replay and Undo with no presentation-owned state, and most of a match has no row there.

The discard row is a third revealable surface, and it leaves no number of its own behind. Each entry is one compact card-shaped icon per card, in the card's own printed colour, carrying no text at all: sequence is the row's fixed left-to-right positions, the count is how many icons there are, and everything else is one pointer rest away at full size. An unoccupied resolved-turn slot stays an empty outline that reveals nothing and answers no pointer, because a turn a hero has not finished is not a card. The pile has no authoritative order and none is invented from discard events the presenter watched: the icons are ordered by card instance identity, which is what the tray orders inside its own discarded group too, so one card sits in the same place on both surfaces and the order survives a restore and a replay.

The rail carries two readings that belong to the hero rather than to any card, and milestone 8.23a recomposed the rail around them. The coin balance is `HeroProgressionProjection.Coins` read straight through - the field p.13's reward, p.8's Pity Coin, a card effect's grant and p.15's Level Up all move - so it follows every authoritative change with no presentation-owned accounting, no new projected field and no schema cause. Milestone 8.21a put it on the `RESOLVED TURNS` caption's line; 8.23a deleted that caption instead, at the user's 2026-08-26 direction, and the balance moved into the item strip where the rail already keeps the numbers true of a hero right now. It is a 24-unit struck disc with a centred 13-point amount rather than the word `COINS` beside a number - composed from the filled circle, darker rim and centred label the header's hero disc uses, never an imported sprite - with `3 coins` in its hover. It is public and drawn whether or not a coin decision stands: a balance visible only while a player is asked to spend it arrives too late to plan with. What the four slots are is carried by each slot's own hover and assistive label, `Turn 1: Blue card - Static Barrier` or `Turn 3: empty`, on the terms 8.7a moved the team label onto the hero disc.

The second reading is rulebook p.18's markers, drawn since 8.23a on the rail of the hero **holding** one. A marker property affects its holder where a token property affects its controller, and p.18 describes markers as components "usually given to other heroes", so Tigerclaw's Poison lands on the rail of the enemy he named and not on his: a rule lowering three of a hero's values has to be visible to the player it is applied to. The row carries the marker's display name and one signed textual channel chip per property it changes. Item bonuses use printed symbols instead, while both readings retain the sign and full words in hover and assistive text. Every part is the trusted `MarkerDefinition`'s - supply, printed strength, channels - with no hero-name branch and no presentation-owned accounting. `MatchSessionProjection.HeldMarkers` is the register itself rather than a copy, which is why restore, replay, Undo, restart and a presentation rebuild redraw it with no serialized field and why no schema moved; the reading's lifetime is the attachment's, so it appears when the marker is given and is gone when p.13's holder Defeat or p.8's `Remove Tokens, Return Markers` returns it. Most of a match has no row there, the conditional the `ULTIMATE` row is drawn under.

The plaque prefers 264 reference units, enough for the medallion, full written name, and reserved current-card marker to read as one identity. That preference is clamped to one quarter of a narrow panel minus its outside margin, so two plaques always remain strictly below half the viewport rather than meeting over the board. Horizontal room also prevents the secondary rows buying narrowness back as extra wrapped height. Each edge column stacks rulebook p.6's three plaques below the status strip and clear of the hand; the outer frame clips no readable descendant, and live geometry checks keep every descendant inside its own plaque and column. The `DIRECTOR` toggle sits immediately below the third Red plaque. `CONFIRM FACEDOWN` occupies its own 300-by-44 lane below that toggle only while a card is selected; it is neither part of the contextual dock nor End Turn's corner. Printed Primary and Secondary actions no longer form a duplicate row there: their typed regions are attached to the resolving card. The event log and director drawer remain the only expanded surfaces in that lane. Non-item textual channel tokens are three letters throughout - Initiative became `INI` at 8.23a, having been the one four-letter token in that textual set. Hero-plaque item bonuses instead use the shared typed printed-symbol catalog introduced at 8.30k.

A resolved slot whose card is still sourcing a live Active effect wears the same violet outline and the same word `ACTIVE` milestone 8.5b gives that card in the tray, derived from the one projected active-source list, so both surfaces mark and unmark it on the same refresh. The outline is drawn around every slot and only its colour changes, and the word's line is always present and only its text changes, so nothing moves when an effect starts or ends.

The board artwork now uses the complete screen behind the HUD. Its 210 playable hexes, rather than the square artwork or decorative terrain inventory, are fitted into one exact L-shaped safe surface: the broad arm between and above standing HUD, plus the lower-right dead space beside the compact hand. Every complete playable hex retains a visible moat from cards, rails and the status dock; artwork and non-playable decoration may pass behind those surfaces. [CONFIGURED_PRESENTATION.md](CONFIGURED_PRESENTATION.md) owns the fit itself.

## The card tray

The card tray shows one hero's whole current round, colour-coded from each card's own projected `CardColor` and never from team, hero, hand position, display name, art, or action type, with the colour's name in its assistive reading. Every card still in the hero's hand and the one resolving card overlap inside the bottom-left footprint through panel x=732; temporary resolution exclusivity may remove a held card's command but never moves or dims that card. The resolving card is stable-sorted rightmost so its complete face and printed actions remain exposed. Only non-resolving cards whose lifecycle is genuinely played or discarded occupy the separate compact vertical fan anchored to the bottom-right margin below the Blue rail stack, using the same complete footprint and opaque local unavailable veil. Its four leading faces expose deterministic top strips and its last face is complete at rest; hover/focus redraws the addressed complete dimmed face above the HUD without moving either fan. The right fan is absent when empty, carries no selection or drag command, and uses one fixed rail-side reservation so lifecycle refreshes neither move the rails nor reframe the map. End Turn is independent in the status bar's top-right slot. All other relative order remains projection-derived from lifecycle and assignment history. The Director follows the awaited hero; a seat returns to its own projected hand. An unused map click may pin another hero. Only painted card/controller bounds block board input.

Moving an eligible card 12 reference units starts one pointer-captured drag drawn with the shared full-size complete-card face. The exact source slot becomes invisible without reflow while the card is held and returns on cancellation. Dropping on the existing L-shaped map submits that exact projected commitment once; the coordinate is discarded. Off-map, stale, hidden or replaced gestures cancel. Below-threshold click and keyboard selection still require `CONFIRM FACEDOWN`; another seat's absent card cannot enter the gesture.

## What one tray card shows

A tray card's immutable projected `CardDefinitionReference` selects one exact row in the validated 108-entry presentation catalog; no name, filename or pixel is interpreted. A locally prepared row uses its verified 372-by-520 PNG as the whole visible face, while an absent or unloadable resource uses the same builder's typed generated regions from the approved [milestone 8.5a mockup](../References/MILESTONE_8_5A_CARD_TRAY_MOCKUP.png). Both paths keep rules, legality, commands, ownership and text outside presentation; a clean tracked checkout remains playable, and a facedown projection names no card to look up.

Every complete tray, Defense, effect-choice, Upgrade and current/turn/discard/Ultimate preview face pins the same approximately 300.46-by-420 `CardFootprint` at the verified 372:520 ratio. Prepared artwork uses `ScaleToFit`; generated fallback uses the identical box. One shared pointer-transparent four-unit black perimeter follows the rounded silhouette entirely outside that unchanged content rectangle, while a dedicated inner clip preserves the prepared scan's corner crop. The helper scales to 1.5 units on occupied rail miniatures and the current/facedown marker. Tray and Defense slots reserve it before the separate Active-effect ring, Upgrade pairs reserve it transparently, and hover placement keeps it on-screen. Selection, availability, resolving, Active source, dragging and lifecycle treatments neither recolour nor replace it; the unchanged card control remains the only pointer/keyboard surface. The ordinary hand does not scroll, while a short mandatory context may scroll vertically without horizontal travel or card resizing.

## A card whose Active effect is live

A card sourcing any `ActiveEffectPhase.Active` instance takes the violet slot outline and, on generated fallback, the word `ACTIVE`; prepared fronts keep that word in typed hover text. `MatchSessionProjection.ActiveEffectSourceCardInstanceIds` derives the unique sources from authority, so presentation infers nothing and restore, replay and retained-prefix Undo rebuild the same answer. The always-reserved slot changes only colour, stays outside the invariant black perimeter, and remains independent of cyan selection and orange confirmation.

The card the current turn is being resolved from is drawn at full strength. It carries no commitment command - the round is past choosing - and it takes no focus stop, but "carries no command" and "is spent" are different facts, and the shared unavailable treatment was saying the second about the first.

Which region a value belongs to is decided by the action it is printed on and by its `PrintedValueChannel`, never by the card's name and never by reading the sentence: the primary action is the one whose origin says `PrintedPrimary`, the secondary strip is every other action the card itself prints, the two qualifiers a label can carry are p.13's Ranged classification and p.14's Block, and p.16's Ultimate - which prints no Initiative and has no action at all - reads its icon from the permanent ability instead. That rule is what keeps this build without a printed-text parser, and `MatchHudPresentationTests` asserts the four regions partition the definition's printed values exactly, each drawn once.

The words themselves are never colour-coded, exactly as a printed card's are not, and `CardSourceContent` still distinguishes transcribed words, a read face printing none, and a synthetic fixture with no source face. Transcriptions display verbatim; [RULES_LEDGER.md](../RULES_LEDGER.md) owns their current source-verification status. Every plate a card draws - flag, bands, badge - shifts the card's own colour away from its text colour, so measured contrast can only rise.

The card's printed colour is not written on its face - the user's 2026-08-21 decision - because a card wearing its colour and spelling it says the same thing twice, in the space the printed text needs. The word is not lost: `ColorName` stays on the view model and in each card's hover and assistive reading, which is exactly where milestone 7.8a already keeps the colour of a hero rail's turn slot. Nothing on this surface means something by colour alone.

## The one contextual region

One contextual surface presents the match's current request. During ordinary activation the fully exposed resolving card owns each silent typed Primary and ordered Secondary region. A prepared primary lights its own printed type and rules-panel lines through one fixed-geometry ring baked with a wide cool halo and near-white core, plus an aligned graphite contrast pass; a prepared secondary lights only its warm boot/value or shield/value badge. Every resting overlay is strong at real card size, and pointer or keyboard focus changes only opacity/contrast, never scale, translation or alignment. All 92 Movement slots reuse the user-approved full-boot silhouette at the shared rail anchor, while Defense and primary-ring geometry remain typed per card; the 386-slot generator/check owns those ignored private overlays without changing source artwork. Generated fallback faces light their typed chip. Every region retains the projection's card/action identity, context, availability and exact invocation; unavailable regions stay located but inert, and presentation neither parses artwork/text nor recomputes legality. Fast Travel replacing a prepared primary Movement and Clear replacing a prepared primary Attack are centred symmetric trapezoid tabs touching the printed title band; Secondary Fast Travel retains its side-mounted right-notch swallowtail. `FAST TRAVEL` uses black lettering in either placement, while Clear retains card-aware ink. Activating any replacement still means its printed action. End Turn remains external because Hold and Movement Stop are not printed regions; it stands alone as a 300-by-96 control anchored to the status bar's top-right edge. Empty-hand `PASS - NO CARD` uses that same mutually exclusive top-right slot and End Turn's rectangular, centred, full-shine treatment because it is that player's only response; Confirm does not share the slot. Each is absent rather than inert wherever not offered. When the phase is terminal, every control remains absent and the shared status narration names the public winning side and whether the final Wave flipped, the enemy Throne was captured, or the opposing team exhausted its Life counters; it never describes that state as waiting. Resolving-card hover cannot cover the live controls; other hand cards retain their preview. A mandatory decision hides the hand and takes the bounded context, leaving one usable interaction surface.

p.15's Upgrade a Card uses six shared whole-card controls. Transparent groups retain each source-card pair in Blue, Green and Red reading order without painting a comparison band, caption, container, option plate, padding frame, resting border or selection outline. `MatchHudTheme.ColorReadingOrder` owns only display order; the pause owns eligibility, pairing and invocations. Each candidate says `IN DECK`; a solid card-footer-grey box over its bottom item position shows the sibling's p.16 symbol and signed value, or `NO ITEM`. It hides only the candidate's conflicting item symbol, and no label remains beneath. `GAINS` is absent; returned and set-aside cards remain in hover and assistive text. Pointer or keyboard focus strengthens the box without moving the face. The bounded 550-unit reservation fits all six faces without horizontal scrolling at validated desktop views; a short view may scroll vertically. Hero columns, the board-safe surface and `DIRECTOR` stay fixed.

The same region draws the projected multi-step end-of-round transition, including the p.8 Pity Coin path into a real p.15 choice. Typed facts supply hero, level, cost, remaining coins and each option's returned, taken, set-aside, item or Place destination; presentation decodes no option identity. Lane-Push Place keeps its team/minion explanation and blocked-Spawn status but no destination control because the board owns every offered space. Effect choices use the candidate's projected display name or printed number. Card-valued discard and retrieval choices use whole shared faces in an unlabelled, frameless row: no outer option plate, inset, selection outline or visible `CARD CHOICE` gutter remains, while the card's shared black silhouette is unchanged; the narration names the affected hero and whether the selected face will be discarded or retrieved. Other status text states the pause's bounds and candidate kind above `NUMBER CHOICE`, `TARGET CHOICE`, `SPACE CHOICE`, `MODE CHOICE` or `OPTIONAL STEP`; a wholly board-answered pause draws no control section. Picking the last allowed candidate completes the choice without a second click; a partially filled variable choice exposes `DONE`, and a confirm-or-decline optional step exposes `ACCEPT`/`DECLINE`. Those two optional-step answers use the pewter banner shape, remain unlit at rest, gain the ivory halo only under the pointer, stand side by side immediately above the retained ordinary hand, and leave that complete hand visible but shielded from pointer, keyboard, drag, printed-action and commitment commands until the pause ends; other exclusive contexts still use their established dedicated surfaces. The generic `CONFIRM CHOICE` and spatial `UNDO OPTION` controls do not exist. Open Clear marks and toggles offered tokens on the board and keeps only its separately named confirm/cancel here; [CONFIGURED_PRESENTATION.md](CONFIGURED_PRESENTATION.md) owns cue derivation. Every enabled answer carries the standing `PendingDecisionContract` command and is revalidated by `MatchSession.Execute`; unprojected options have no enabled surface. A pause exposes neither End Turn nor end-of-round advance. The retained director/headless command surface enumerates the same contract for fixtures and build smoke.

Tied Initiative keeps the ordinary hand visible and inert beneath one fixed row headed exactly `WHO ACTS FIRST?`. The row contains only the current candidates' hero names: two equal buttons fill halves and three fill thirds. Candidate owners may publicly state or revise a preference, every seat reads who is waiting and whom each participant prefers, and unanimity alone reaches the existing rules choice; a non-owner sees status without a command. Hovering a hero-name button joins the permanent plaque/board identity channel above. Minion Battle remains map-first: the choosing team selects minions on the board, and the contextual surface publishes the exact standing proposal plus each of the three teammates' agreement state and that seat's `AGREE` or `WITHDRAW` control. Selection change clears all approvals; opponents see status only. Both agreements are Application-host projections rather than gameplay facts, and Director mode retains its direct development controls.

The mandatory context fits the validated desktop viewport; a short supported viewport uses bounded vertical treatment without horizontal scrolling or card resizing.
