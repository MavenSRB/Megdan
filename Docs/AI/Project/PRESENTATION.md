# Presentation and Accessibility Direction

Canonical active shard. Return to [../PROJECT_CONTEXT.md](../PROJECT_CONTEXT.md).

## Presentation direction

- Active-effect ring order is token, violet effect ring, then gold activity ring. Without an effect, activity contracts beside the token. The radius tint uses the shipped Unlit alpha-blended template unchanged, at the shared overlay alpha, so board markings remain visible in built players as well as the Editor.
- The Unity scene is 3D from the beginning, with the board on the XZ plane.
- Early implementation uses primitive meshes and a fixed camera.
- URP 17.5 is the accepted rendering baseline: one checked-in pipeline is active globally and at all six quality levels, and retained primitive authored/runtime materials use URP Lit or URP Unlit. Built-In-only shaders, production art direction, final lighting, and post-processing remain outside the retained baseline.
- The minimal-grid validation scene centers its fixed camera directly above the map and looks straight down.
- Pointy-top presentation maps `q` eastward and decreasing `r` northward; the same outer-radius value defines both the regular hex mesh and its center spacing.
- Placeholder hexes use one light-gray fill with a darker gray perimeter for separation; this styling is non-semantic and the logical origin has no unique treatment.
- The polished pitch uses a stylized tabletop presentation.
- Milestone 8.11a uses the private Tabletopia `GuardsII_Small_Board` artwork embedded in `Docs/Guards of Atlantis II - Tabletopia Cards/extracted/text-assets/g713916_s799373.sprite17__31.jpg` as the flat official-map underlay. The reviewed 292-space `official-single-lane` definition remains authoritative: existing tile views become a registered transparent interaction/highlight layer, while the manifest's 332 magnetic centres are calibration evidence only and never create rules spaces. The prepared Unity artwork remains ignored and private, and its absence retains the generated-board fallback required by a clean tracked checkout.
- Milestone 8.11b uses the private Tabletopia `Wasp` vertical-token entry's Red and Blue circular faces for the configured mirrored hero views, reuses the tray's complete card faces for pending Defense responses, and turns each resolved or discarded rail card into its own number-free mini-card icon with a complete face on mouseover. Hero/team identity, interaction, Defense legality, totals and card lifecycle remain projected authority rather than readings of art, and absent private assets retain the tracked primitive fallback.
- All artwork-backed board heroes now use the same 0.15-world-unit player-colour token rim inside the unchanged 0.81 outer radius and pointer footprint. Team-baked source faces are cropped within their original orange/blue printed edge before the runtime rim is drawn; team-neutral faces keep their existing sampling. The source artwork remains private and unmodified, and the generated-prism fallback is unchanged.
- Milestone 8.11d gives every projected hero, minion, or token target one static identity-keyed cue: a board-facing ring fitted to its local circular or convex base contour plus an exact-footprint translucent wash just above the actual art. `Eligible` is one thick ring of eight separated segments with wash alpha `0.18`; `Selected` is a double ring with alpha `0.23`; the reserved future `Hovered` treatment is a brighter solid ring with four connected outward ticks and the bounded-overlay maximum alpha `0.28`, but the current direct-click flow manufactures no hover state. `Attack` remains typed distinctly from `Removal`, while both share blood red RGB `194/10/24`; `Affect` uses dark green RGB `27/129/71`; all share the light-neutral keyline RGB `248/247/242`. Shape, count, luminance, and assistive reading preserve meaning without colour alone. The bounded tint leaves source art and labels legible, the cue remains distinct from destination-space fills, and it adds no collider or pointer surface. Its static wash is part of the existing cue, not animation, production VFX, source-art tinting, or a team-palette change.
- Heroes and minions appear as 3D miniatures on clearly marked team bases.
- Miniatures may briefly animate when acting.
- When the camera approaches top-down, units automatically crossfade to readable 2D tokens.
- Separate enter and exit thresholds prevent visual flicker near the switching angle.
- One logical unit owns interchangeable 3D-miniature and 2D-token representations.
- Standard minions use the circular artwork embedded along the top of the private reference atlas `Docs/Guards of Atlantis II - Tabletopia Cards/extracted/text-assets/g713916_s799373.sprite0__45.jpg` when they first become visible in milestone 4.4. Steam/orange maps to project Red and Crystal/light-blue maps to project Blue; the round riveted/broad spiked face is Heavy, the narrow helmet with cheek guards/armored shoulders is Melee, and the two-pronged helmet/narrow crystal crest is Ranged. The source manifest's `Melee_Steam` label on the Crystal melee sprite is a known metadata error. Milestones 9.3 and 9.5 retain these images as the 2D side of each minion's interchangeable presentation; authoritative team/type data selects the image, and rules identity is never inferred from art or the incorrect source label.

### Camera

- Board-focused rather than free flight.
- Bounded pan, orbit, and zoom.
- Supports angled tabletop and top-down views.
- Includes a whole-board reset/focus control.
- Cannot travel beneath or far outside the board.

### Animation and audio

- Rules resolve independently of animations.
- The presentation layer consumes ordered rule events and animates them.
- Skipping or accelerating animation never changes game state.
- Target animation coverage for each of the six pitch heroes: idle, movement, attack, hit/defeat, and respawn, reusing shared rigs/clips where suitable without sacrificing hero readability.
- Minions use a smaller shared animation set.
- The pitch receives a small sound-effects pass for UI confirmation, card reveal, movement, attack, hit/defeat, minion battle, and lane push.
- Music, voice acting, announcer systems, and elaborate adaptive audio are out of scope.

## Pitch HUD

Board hero pieces and rail portraits resolve prepared artwork through the same baked-first, neutral-second, primitive/empty-last adapter. Neutral faces use the existing narrower face radius inside the unchanged team rim; neither hero names nor art pixels decide team identity. Missing private resources remain supported. The earlier pure neutral-face policy alone did not wire these two Unity consumers; both now use it.

Private card fronts are prepared through `Tools/Prepare-CardArtwork.ps1`; `-Check` verifies dimensions and source-byte equality. The importer covers every configured hero and preserves existing metadata/overlays. Catalog identities and printed rules are unchanged; missing private resources still use the typed generated fallback. Extracted images stay ignored and local.

END TURN and the seat's UNDO use `HudElements.Control`'s shared chip, border, radius, text colour and hover/press motion, not overpainted pewter cloth. Their corner geometry and authority paths are unchanged. END TURN retains its sole-action outline and disabled metadata; unavailable seat Undo stays absent. Small text hover popups are suppressed globally at the HUD root, including unavailable-action explanations and nested labels. Full card previews and defense modifier breakdowns remain available; tooltip metadata is preserved without drawing a popup.

- The board is the primary focus.
- Director mode exposes all six hands through clear Red/Blue team and hero grouping. Private online clients expose only the local player's hand plus authorized shared played/revealed state; both modes keep the active resolver readable without presentation owning legality.
- The primitive player-facing HUD is proven against all six owner-scoped projections from the local transport-neutral session: every seat completes the deterministic records, every prompt family and ending is covered, and repeated seat composition retains no hidden identity, stale selection, duplicate handler surface, or monotonically growing owned resource. A development-only seat switcher may select each perspective for validation, but it remains outside match authority, cannot transfer ownership, and is unavailable from private-online client projections. This is deliberately no evidence about a network.
- The same primitive HUD and contextual-prompt meanings are retained behind the network client/session adapter. Lobby, connection, synchronization, reconnect, rejection, and host-loss status wrap that proven match UI rather than creating a second rules-facing presentation path.
- Show a compact read-only 6-hero-by-4-turn card history grouped as three Red and three Blue heroes; each entry derives from the authoritative `HeroId` assignment and reveals no committed card before the joint reveal. Milestone 7.8a builds the per-hero half of this as an edge-aligned overview rail carrying four fixed turn slots that take each played card's authoritative colour, repeated once per projected hero rather than tied to a fixed pair.
- The card tray shows one hero's current round at a time. The map pointer is the only hero-selection control there is, and selecting changes only the viewed hero: it submits no command and adds no second selection surface. The Director's default follows the hero the match is waiting on; a seat's default is its own hero and playable private hand. A map selection pins another until the awaited hero changes, then the Director follows the new wait and a seat returns home.
- Every complete hero-card face uses one shared approximately 300.46-by-420 native-aspect footprint across the ordinary tray, Defense responses, card-valued choices, Level Up/upgrade choices, previews, and later full-card surfaces. Hero identity, printed content, resource availability and active/selected/unavailable/hover/focus state never change it. Prepared art is the whole visible face with no synthetic shell and an aspect-preserving image fit; generated content wraps or fits inside the same box. The ordinary five-card hand overlaps full faces at the bottom-left anchor, keeping every exposed strip clickable and revealing one complete topmost face on hover/focus without a scroller; mandatory tall contexts may scroll vertically without scaling individual cards. A newly configured hero cannot complete its permanent player-path gate while either arm changes the footprint, distorts a prepared front, makes an exposed hand card unreachable, or clips, overlaps, or truncates meaningful fallback content.
- Experimental hero card-state pips retain the visibility-safe identity already supplied by the current viewer's inspection projection; concealed pips remain anonymous. The current card sits at the screen-facing top of the circular token edge, while hand pips are separated narrow vertical rectangles: each short edge is tangent to a screen-centred lower arc and each long axis points radially. Holding either Shift key while hovering an identity-bearing pip raises the same complete shared card face inside the board-safe HUD region; releasing Shift, leaving the pip, losing focus, entering terminal presentation, or hovering a concealed/stale pip closes it. The preview and pips remain collider-free and pointer-transparent, submit no command, and never resolve an identity outside the viewer's projected definition catalog.
- Show round, turn, active resolver, Lives, Waves, hero levels, and coins. Milestone 7.8a puts round, turn, both teams' Life counters, the Wave counter, and the Tie Breaker in one standing status strip; hero Level and item bonuses sit on that hero's own overview rail.
- One shared contextual region presents whatever the match is currently asking, and the ordinary card tray is hidden rather than left inert while a mandatory decision stands, so at most one interaction context is usable. Spatial decisions stay direct board interactions; the region states what is being chosen and draws only the projected non-spatial controls. Every such control says what pressing it does, in the card's own words (`DecisionLabelCatalog`, milestone 9.40: "never have option X label on the button, always write what the button does"); "CHOICE", "CHOOSE ONE" and the other generic words are window titles and section eyebrows above named controls, never a control's label, and the ordinal fallback is unreachable for dealt content.
- Use contextual prompts for actions, targets, and choices.
- Present one contextual player-facing **End Turn** control instead of separate Hold and Movement Stop controls. At ordinary unresolved-card action selection it invokes the exact authoritative Hold offer; while an optional Movement continuation is pending it invokes the exact authoritative Stop choice, ends that Movement action, and therefore ends the current turn. The control is hidden or disabled whenever the authoritative projection exposes no legal turn-ending invocation, including mandatory choices that cannot be bypassed.
- **End Turn** is presentation terminology, not a generic rules command. Its view model carries the exact currently legal typed offer or pending-choice invocation supplied by authority; the view never infers legality from its own phase state. Future turn-ending functionality reuses the control by projecting its own typed invocation, without making Hold, Movement Stop, or later rule operations interchangeable.
- Present eligible Fast Travel as a clickable `FAST TRAVEL` action-mode button only when the immutable application projection supplies a replacement with at least one legal destination. Eligibility alone does not activate the mode: normal actions and an unhighlighted board remain the default until the player selects the button, after which the board exposes only the projected destinations through Movement's green six-corner shine and forwards one selected coordinate through the existing typed command path.
- For every board-valued choice, one shared fixed template draws all six printed corner marks of each projected choice hex. Movement, selected Fast Travel, Move/Place/Spawn effect destinations, correction destinations and board placement transitions use strong green; Respawn uses cyan; Attack, Clear, Minion Battle and other removal use blood red; teammate targets use warm ivory; other effects use neutral grey; and a projected result uses darker neutral grey. Rest is steady and pointer hover strengthens only colour/alpha, never geometry. Configured play activates neither the retired whole-hex fill/rim nor the fitted target ring/wash; an identity-named Hero, minion or token is resolved only to its currently projected occupied hex for the corner cue, while the piece keeps its ordinary team frame and pointer surface. Presentation derives teammate colour from authoritative participant/controller facts but does not recalculate legality, distance, Range, Heavy immunity, or any other rule. Removed minions disappear when the next projection omits them and can return only when a later authoritative projection includes them again.
- Provide internal Undo, restart, animation-speed, and camera-reset controls. Milestone 7.8a groups the first two and the event log behind one side `DIRECTOR` toggle, where `RESET` is presentation terminology for the existing restart; opening or closing the drawer changes no match state.
- Provide a collapsible event log.
- A paused multi-step system transition is a decision like any other and is drawn in the same contextual region. Its options and their meanings are projected: the rules that paused describe the pause as typed facts, and presentation composes labels from those facts rather than decoding an option identity. No End Turn and no end-of-round advance appears beside a pause, because the authority projects no stop and would refuse the advance.
- The ordinary configured tray is a transparent full-width bottom layout band containing only the compact overlapping hand at its left edge; its unpainted remainder neither draws chrome nor blocks board input. Round/current-card status and routine controls occupy the fixed largest-state dock above it. Mandatory multi-card decisions replace the hand with the full-width bounded tall context, retain the standard face footprint, hide horizontal scrolling, and expose vertical overflow only when a short desktop viewport requires it, keeping every projected Defense-card and Decline control reachable.
- Each configured legal Defense-card invocation identifies its authoritative card and labels the offer-bound value as `PRINTED DEFENSE`; the exact typed command remains in the projected invocation, while modified Defense Total, eligibility, and response resolution remain outside presentation.
- Defense-choice controls must size or wrap from their complete rendered label so the card name and printed value remain readable; a model containing complete text does not satisfy this requirement when the configured control clips it.
- A compact pointer-pass-through Defense breakdown appears for the hovered or keyboard-focused legal Defense choice. Moving across choices updates the same preview without changing authoritative state; clicking retains the current one-click typed response. The immutable application projection supplies Attack and per-choice Defense contributions, totals, explicit Block/result meaning, and player-readable source identities, while presentation only formats them. The popup uses a fully opaque background plus explicit `ATTACK:`, `DEFENSE:`, and `RESULT:` sections, avoids the focused control, remains inside the viewport, and uses compact columns when a long breakdown needs bounded vertical space. Every standing hero body carries a tall tapered shield (0.572 token-radius wide and 0.754 high) with a black outline and light-grey face centered on its screen-left token rim, matching the card-pip rim centerline without a downward offset, showing its centered signed net standard-minion modifier (`-2`, `0`, `+1`) at all times; three independent default-off Experimental options may add held Defense items, Active effects, and card/continuous effects. It never shows `DEF` or the printed Defense value. The existing token art and player-colour rim are unchanged. Card-state pips keep their 0.12-unit radius and rim centerline, with hand-pip centers 0.28 units apart for a visible 0.04-unit gap. `HeroMinionDefenseQuery` derives immutable per-body readings from the public board using the same `DefenseValueQuery` as combat, once per refreshed projection; joined clients need no private card identities or new wire fields. Ordinary board hover and linked rail hover tint only those contributing minions green for positive and blood red for negative, with no minion numbers. The same reading and hover work during Attack target selection. Hover exit clears the contributor tints while every standing hero keeps its shield. Movement, removal, Undo and restore rebuild the readings from the current public board without submitting a command. Once the Defense response opens, its already-calculated attack-scoped minion sources remain the board's green/red six-corner contribution cues while the player moves across cards or Decline; those cues clear or rebuild only when the pending response or projection changes. Decline previews no Defense Total without changing the attack-scoped cues. Presentation never recalculates adjacency, teams, or Defense.
- When the authority pauses the end of a round on a Minion Battle removal choice, the configured HUD shows one contextual prompt naming the choosing team, the required removal count, and the current selection, plus one control for each candidate the immutable pending choice currently makes legal - a Heavy appears only once the authority itself offers it, and a state with no choice shows no prompt, no cues, and no end-of-round advance control. Each control reads `REMOVE` or `KEEP` followed by the candidate's team, intrinsic type, and ordinal, so two candidates of the same team and type stay distinguishable without colour. Pointing at or keyboard-focusing a control gives that identity the eligible blood-red fitted cue; a selected minion keeps the double blood-red cue while the player chooses the rest, with selected outranking eligible. The confirmation control appears only while the authoritative choice reports it can be confirmed. Presentation forwards only the projected select, deselect, and confirm invocations: it never compares minion counts, orders Heavy removals, removes occupancy, or advances the end-of-round cursor, and a refused control rebuilds the visible selection from the returned projection.
- A minimal private-session entry/lobby screen is required for host/join and connection flow. No general main menu, settings suite, tutorial, collection screen, or account UI is required for the pitch.

## Accessibility baseline

- Team identity never relies on color alone.
- Red and Blue also use distinct symbols, base markings, outlines, and labels.
- Cards and state values must remain readable at common desktop resolutions.
- UI structure must allow later scaling.
- A full accessibility settings suite is deferred.

## Motion and the attack spotlight

Hero consequence notifications name the affected target, not the attacker. Removed pieces deactivate immediately; their non-interactive echoes wait for the spotlight's actual strike before fading. A last minion that Lane Push removes and immediately respawns under the same miniature identity still dies at its pre-push space: the real respawn and all consequent wave movement remain withheld until that echo finishes. The strike-start gate is latched at the kinetic scene boundary, so a lunge that begins and ends between board-driver updates cannot strand the death; Undo or restoration cancels the echo and held respawn. Spotlight card spacing includes the tokens' projected radius, keeping the enlarged combatants clear of both faces. Attack rows reach this layer at a hero attack's final outcome or a non-defending minion attack's start; a standing after-Attack question is locally withheld through framing, the post-zoom beat and earlier queued scenes, then released when the framed lunge actually enters playback. The gate changes no authoritative timing, and first sight/reconnect primes old rows without replaying them.

- **Nothing teleports, and nothing is fully static.** Every state change travels: a committed card arcs from the hand to its facedown slot, a reveal flips it, a retrieved card flies back at round end, coins fly to the tray they join, a Level pops on the rail, and pieces glide, lunge and flash on the board. Panels ease into existence rather than popping, through UI Toolkit's own transitions, so a screen-wide floor of micro-motion costs no per-frame work. Curves are ease-in-out only and live in one enumerable place with an audit that samples each against the identity line; travel and appearance sit in a 200-450ms band and interaction under 200ms, because a hover must answer immediately.
- **One conductor paces the whole surface, and it is downstream of everything.** The unit it plays is a MOMENT, not a cue: an attack's lunge and the flash it caused play together, while six reveals are six moments and are staged with a deliberate beat, re-ranked by the revealed card's printed Initiative rather than by the order the engine emits them. The conductor holds no session and no projection - asserted structurally - so it can delay an animation and never a command, a prompt or a frame of the board. Six clients therefore watch one match at six different points in the same story over one authoritative board, and a viewer who has fallen behind is caught up by compressing the queue rather than by making anybody wait.
- **The attack spotlight is the one surface allowed to take the HUD away.** A resolved attack hides every child of the HUD root except its own layer, drives the board camera in to frame the two combatants - clamped both ways, the ceiling derived from the largest printed Range an attack can actually reach rather than from how far apart two pieces could stand - tilts to give the flat token discs a visible side, and stands each combatant's card beside its own piece, on the side away from the other, never covering either piece. A ranged pair gets a jab, a bounded-speed tracer and an impact ring; the treatment is chosen by the measured separation, not by the rules category, because a ranged attack on an adjacent target crossed no ground. It releases on a CONTINUE the viewer presses, or by itself on a short valve - short precisely because the chrome is hidden, and a hidden HUD is a client its player cannot use. On exit every hidden panel's inline `display` is restored verbatim and the board reasserts its own camera frame.
- **Every named control draws from one definition, and a re-composition redraws all of them.** Milestone 8.35z58 settled the button family on `MatchHudTheme`: one idle chip bright enough to read as a control against the darkest panel it sits on, one shared border promoted from the lobby's own former private edge tone, one corner radius held const-to-const with the top strip and match-log band, and a ~100ms press. The lobby's hero-selection option, added a milestone after that family shipped, routes through the same chrome and names its unavailable reason at the cursor. Its filters are two unlabeled rows immediately above the roster: zero or one star value first, then independently toggleable printed roles whose selected set is AND-combined. Stars, roles and CLEAR use that same button family, with only the selected filters taking the gold edge; there is no category or match-count label. CLEAR resets both rows, filtering a highlighted hero out clears that local highlight, and an empty result states that no hero matches. Milestone 8.35z62 then fixed what made every one of those surfaces stale after a lobby transition: the HUD's build was once-only for its whole body, which is right for the panel and wrong for the surfaces on it, because the scene runtime constructs a NEW view for each surface on every composition. It now separates the panel, built once, from the surfaces, composed every time - so the hero rails, hand fan, round actions and match log follow a recomposed match instead of drawing the previous one. The rule this leaves is that a surface is proven at the RENDERED tier or not proven at all: the projections were correct throughout the defect that forced this, and only a query against the live panel could see it.
- **The panel never shrinks below its declared reference in either dimension.** Milestone 8.35z59 replaced a halfway width/height match with `Expand`, after a live ultrawide playtest buried the hero rails: UI Toolkit lerps the two normalized axes linearly, so splitting the difference made a wider-than-16:9 screen produce a SHORTER panel than the reference, and the rail column - the only region sized from what is left over vertically - absorbed the whole loss. Following the height alone opened the mirror defect below 16:9, where the board slid into the round dock, so the board is additionally bounded against that dock. 16:9 is pixel-identical across the change. A composed HUD still lays out for the viewport it was composed in and does not re-lay-out on a mid-session resolution change; a shipped build takes its resolution at launch, so nothing reaches that today.
- **A mechanically active effect is visible on the board, in the violet its source card already wears.** Milestone 8.35z63 answers the user's directive - "when a hero has an active effect, we need to show it... If it is an active effect in radius, we need to paint the hexes in that radius that same color" - by giving `MatchHudTheme.ActiveEffectAccent`, milestone 8.5b's tray violet, two board consumers rather than minting a second purple. A hero carrying an active effect wears a violet annulus on its token; an active effect with a radius tints the hexes of its disk. A dormant future effect already marks its source card as a continuous reminder but lights neither target nor disk until activation. The two board treatments differ only in alpha and by the board's own established idioms: a thin mark is added light, a whole-hex area is a tint the artwork reads through. The disk is centred on its carrier's CURRENT hex on every rebind, which is rulebook p.17's "an Active effect's Radius moves with its hero" without storing anything, and it glides with the carrier's slide through the conductor's own displacement rather than jumping at the end of a move. It is derived from the mechanically active read-model list and is seat-agnostic, so every seat and any spectator sees the same marks. All 61 registered definitions across the eleven heroes resolve to a treatment (40 ring, 18 disk, 3 both, 0 silent), walked from the catalog so a scope this layer cannot place is a red test rather than a hero rendering nothing; Sabina ships none, which is her printed set. The aura is its own tile layer beneath the perimeter, the pieces and the six-corner marks, and never touches the retired choice fill: gold still means "waiting on you", violet on the board means "an effect is running", and where both apply both are drawn.
