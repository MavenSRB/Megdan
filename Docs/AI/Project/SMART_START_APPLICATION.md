## Problem — maximum 300 words

Some adult players still value the strategic coordination, distinct hero roles and shared victories of multiplayer online battle arena games, but no longer want continuous reflex-driven execution to determine their contribution. Our hypothesis is that these players lack a sufficiently appealing way to retain individual responsibility within a team while shifting competition toward deliberate tactical decisions.

The starting point is the applicant's experience building and playing a private tactical prototype. Its match histories provide behavioral evidence that this form of play can sustain extended sessions. They do not yet establish the size of the target segment or prove demand for the proposed original product.

We will test the problem with current and former MOBA players, distinguish execution pressure from time constraints and learning barriers, and compare responses with observed play. We will not assume that older players universally dislike real-time games or promise short sessions without evidence.

## Target group — maximum 200 words

The initial hypothesis is adult English-speaking PC players with present or past MOBA experience who retain an interest in team competition but prefer deliberate tactical execution. We will recruit both existing friend groups and participants outside the applicant's immediate network. Tactical and tabletop communities are secondary discovery channels, not automatically the same customer segment.

Segmentation will distinguish current versus former MOBA players, preferred session length, ability to assemble a team, and prior spending on comparable games. This will help identify whether the strongest early audience is defined by age, play preference, social group or a combination of these factors.

## Sustainable business and rollout — maximum 300 words

Our initial business-model hypothesis is a paid PC game, subject to price and group-purchase testing. We will compare individual purchase with a group-friendly offer and estimate net receipts after platform charges, taxes/refunds where applicable, customer support and online operating costs. No price or sales forecast has yet been validated.

The proposed rollout begins with invited, rights-cleared MVP testing, then a commercial-readiness decision based on user evidence and the cost model. A public demo/store presence and community-led discovery are potential later steps requiring separate approval and funding. Marketing expenditure will not be placed in an ineligible grant cost category.

Digital distribution could enable international reach without proportionally increasing production effort per copy. However, acquisition, content production, service operations and sufficient concurrent participation still constrain growth. The project will therefore test a credible small-scale launch path before promising broad service scale or additional platforms.
