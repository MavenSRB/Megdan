One dispatcher owns live typed commands, replay, Undo/restart, restore, ordered events and immutable projections, while five concrete backends own handler-specific pending state and mutation, reached by direct composition rather than through a registry. Its application transaction publishes an accepted command only after the resulting projection is built: authority, whole-match/current-round events, accepted history, version/checkpoints, and that immutable projection advance together. A projection fault is an `EngineFault` preserving the exact previous public projection, and restore plus replay-candidate adoption for Undo/restart use the same rollback guarantee. Action-kind dispatch is that direct composition; capability dispatch alone is data-driven lookup through `EffectCapabilityCatalog`, because a capability reference arrives as data inside the definition the resolver is walking and cannot be named at compile time. The contextual End Turn control forwards only a projected stop - the Hold, the Movement Stop, or an effect choice's own - and is suppressed during every mandatory choice that projects none. Action offers carry a decided availability, asked of the code the matching start asks: a Skill whose feasibility walk proves its effect can apply nothing, an ordinary Attack whose target query finds nobody, an Attack whose mandatory card text cannot complete its typed target binding, and - milestone 8.8b - a p.13 Clear with no token adjacent to the actor. Each is drawn greyed and still refused atomically at the start, and each answers true wherever it cannot be asked, so an action is refused for being proven empty and never for being unjudgeable. The Clear case draws the distinction the others draw: p.13's "any number" includes zero, so clearing none stays legal wherever a token *is* adjacent and only a Clear that could remove nothing whatever the hero chose is refused; `DEV-007` records it, because the page does permit performing one. A greyed offer carries no command at all; Clear projects no subset invocation. p.12 is the source - Hold exists so a player is never forced to act - and the turn stays spendable on the card's other actions, including a Clear that replaces an unavailable Attack and has something to take. The same walk reads an optional card-text step inside a resolution: a branch that can apply nothing is skipped rather than opening p.10's confirm-or-decline.

A Skill is an ordinary printed action offer resolved over an immutable, catalog-owned `EffectDefinition`: a flat table of stable nodes with `Sequence`, `Optional`, `Conditional`, `Choose` and `RepeatAction`, where `Mandatory`, `may`, `up to` and `if able` stay four distinct behaviors rather than one failure path. `PendingResolution` carries identity, a continuation stack, completed outcomes and accepted choices - references only, never an `EffectDefinition` object or a handler, so the snapshot written from it has nowhere to put semantics. Every decision inside an effect frame rides one `AnswerEffectChoice` command carrying the invocation the pending choice projected, including the token and marker decisions described below. Presentation forwards those invocations and nothing else; [CONFIGURED_PRESENTATION.md](CONFIGURED_PRESENTATION.md) owns what a paused frame renders and what it withholds. The Skill seams hold the same bar as Movement and Attack: a start revalidates the offer and invocation context, and the effect-answer path prechecks pending identity and source-card state, so a bad state rejects with a typed reason rather than faulting at completion. A Skill whose root completes during its own start resolves there and emits the ordinary started/node/completed stream. The chooser's own option filtering, and the pause a mandatory node whose every option is refused makes behind one projected stop, are stated once in [CARD_EFFECT_CAPABILITIES.md](../CARD_EFFECT_CAPABILITIES.md) with their surface in [CONFIGURED_PRESENTATION.md](CONFIGURED_PRESENTATION.md). The stop is derived rather than stored, so restore, replay and retained-prefix Undo re-derive it as the state then stands, and a stale or forged select naming a refused option is rejected atomically. A target selection's declaration of the node it selects for, which lets a step offer only the objects its consumer can act on, is maintained in [Card-System/RESOLUTION_AND_EFFECTS.md](../Card-System/RESOLUTION_AND_EFFECTS.md). Refusals report Skill-specific rejection reasons, Skill facts materialize under their own `SkillActionProgressed` turn event kind, a paused Skill frame suppresses projected Fast-Travel options as paused Movement and Attack do, and restore re-derives a paused capability's candidates from the persisted completed outcomes through the resolver's own binding path. A definition can also ask for its own action to happen again through a `RepeatAction` node, bounded by the maximum that node declares and by the count the frame carries; that node, its same-target binding, the dispatcher's handling of it and the states it crosses the wire in are maintained in [Card-System/RESOLUTION_AND_EFFECTS.md](../Card-System/RESOLUTION_AND_EFFECTS.md), which took this account at 7.1's threshold ownership review.

`EffectRosterState` binds live turn slots, typed hero-Defeat history, and the coin ledger into the effect frame; missing authority throws rather than masquerading as false history or zero balance. Live resolution, resumption, feasibility, triggers, and restore bind the same collaborator, and no candidate set or derived answer is serialized. The generic `Core.Effects` catalog factory contains only generic handlers, while `MatchContentCatalogs` supplies the six verified sets' declarations at the build composition root in one deterministic catalog; unreachable registrations remain rejected. Enum-like integer bindings retain their raw runtime integer value but carry their declared vocabulary type, and each matching capability parameter carries an immutable legal domain that definition validation enforces before a match starts.

### Movement and action architecture

The Phase 4 gate is closed; its record is indexed from the [Phase 4 archive](../Archive/Phase-Handoffs/PHASE_4.md). Segmented typed Movement continuation runs on the exact selected map: shortest and deliberately longer cumulative waypoint routes to the same destination commit their selected ordered adjacent steps through the configured match session, a typed evaluator applies prevention or a distance limit once at Movement-action start, and the effective allowance is captured in existing pending state for continuation, restore, replay, and Undo boundaries.

Fast Travel is a distinct typed replacement action on the match-session command path. It accepts empty destinations in the actor's current zone or a shared-border adjacent zone, requires no empty-space path, rejects non-adjacent zones and enemy hero/minion presence in either endpoint zone atomically, records ordered replacement/relocation/completion facts, resolves the played card, and suppresses source-card text even when replacing Primary Movement. The application projection exposes only currently eligible Fast Travel replacements and their authoritative legal destinations. The configured passive HUD shows an eligible replacement as a `FAST TRAVEL` button, leaving normal actions and an unhighlighted board as the default; only explicit button selection exposes that exact destination set through Movement's green six-corner shine. A lit coordinate then submits the existing typed `PerformFastTravel` command through `BoardInteractionPresenter` and `IMatchSession`; presentation performs no zone, enemy-presence, or destination-legality calculation. Replay and Undo use the same command path.

The registered backend split is sound for the implemented families. A completed Attack resolves its source card and advances Initiative; a card already acting is offered no ordinary action. Movement and Attack/Defense retain their serializable pending continuations; Fast Travel and Clear are immediate replacement backends, while Skill owns the catalog-bound effect frame and its serializable pending continuation. The ordinary HUD attaches each projected Fast Travel or Clear replacement chip to the exact typed printed region it qualifies without changing either backend or silently substituting the replacement when the printed region is activated. Clear deliberately uses the existing direct replacement seam rather than implementing the still-deferred generic `ReplaceAction` effect node. The outer application/round/turn dispatch continues to enumerate authority-level command families, while choices inside an effect definition use the shared `AnswerEffectChoice` command.

### Match state, replay, and configured scope

All four of rulebook pp.5-6's printed rows compose - 2v2 and 3v3 at Quick and Long - the Wave count derived from the length and the team shape from the count, with the format its own admission-tuple element rather than folded into the content digest. A Director-only staged board composes into the session's own initial state, so Undo, restart, replay and the event log are the ordinary ones and a restart returns to it; staged tokens and a staged round cursor are refused. Authoritative pure C# state currently covers map-scoped identity, the immutable selected map revision, participant `PlayerId`/`HeroId` relationships, initial Tie Breaker, hands whose size is content rather than a modelled rule - the default configuration and configured scene deal p.5's five over p.6's remaining thirteen, the showcase fixtures the same shape and the Skill fixture eight - owned commitment or empty-hand Pass for every configured hero, joint reveal, Initiative and tied-Initiative choice, Hold, printed Movement with cumulative waypoint routes and Stop, adjacent/Ranged Attack with one legal Defense discard or decline, immutable four-turn assignment history, and a resumable end-of-round cursor. Tied Initiative and Minion Battle remain rules-owned pending choices; the Application host's public, host-local agreement state authorizes only their existing final commands and clears on proposal change or host rebuild. A current-turn "played an Attack card" reading resolves the exact slot assignment to its definition's `PrintedPrimary` action and never substitutes performed-action history; completed ordinary or effect-invoked p.13 hero Defeats append one opaque outcome ID plus typed defeated `HeroId` to a current-round record that clears only at the next round. Roster predicates read those records directly, presence/count reads live occupancy without applying targeting immunity, and pre-consequence adjacency reads the target space already retained by the enclosing Attack. On a configured map with a current Battle Zone, Minion Battle compares current Red/Blue minion counts, completes ties without effect, auto-resolves a forced removal set, or pauses on a round-owned typed losing-team choice whose shape and confirmation are maintained in [Card-System/TIMING_AND_TRANSITIONS.md](../Card-System/TIMING_AND_TRANSITIONS.md). Losing the last minion records rulebook p.8's end-of-round Lane-Push cause; what that request and its mid-turn sibling carry, and where each of them stops, lives in [BOARD_OPERATIONS.md](BOARD_OPERATIONS.md). The cursor runs all six of p.8's ordered steps and declares none of them unavailable: a round with nothing to push completes that step having pushed nothing, and a push that ends the game reports the step it ended at. Eligible cards return to canonical owners, and the current-round event and hero-Defeat records empty when the next round opens, while the whole-match diagnostic log and completed-round record keep their own subjects.

The registered operations that change the board - tokens and markers with their derived supplies, direct Move and Object Push, Clear, and Place beside Spawn - are maintained in [BOARD_OPERATIONS.md](BOARD_OPERATIONS.md). The seam stays here: each asks the board for a validated relocation, placement or removal through a named port rather than reaching the authority, none is a Movement action, and every decision inside one rides the existing `AnswerEffectChoice` command rather than a pending-choice class of its own.
Modifiers, restrictions, permissions, immunity, `Counts As`, the three-way obstacle classification they qualify, and the per-activation identity every effect source mints are maintained in [RULE_MODIFICATIONS.md](RULE_MODIFICATIONS.md). The contract boundary stays here: a rule is definition content on a trusted catalog rather than snapshot state, the live set is derived per command from active sources, and every rules-consuming seam takes it as a required parameter.

Schema-versioned snapshots cover participants, selected map ID/revision, board occupancy, card facets, Initiative/Tie Breaker, current/completed assignments, current-round hero-Defeat outcomes, end-of-round state, pending Movement, Attack/Defense, Skill, or displaced-minion-correction continuations, the complete pending hero-or-minion Minion-Battle selection, and unconsumed end-of-round and mid-turn Lane-Push requests. The current persisted snapshot schema is v37. Moves have two causes: a new field a restore cannot re-derive, and the pinned registration list itself, which the payload records and a restore compares for equality - the list moving each time a roster hero became the first printed content to name more of the declared vocabulary. Every superseded version is rejected on its own equivalent ground; for a registration-list move, that a shorter list would let a restored match resolve against contracts its capture never had. Each version's cause is recorded at the constant, in its corpus fixture and in the completion evidence of the milestone that moved it, rather than restated here. Target sets, geometry, values, card candidates and coin choices are rederived rather than persisted, after trusted definitions and the live round/card/ledger authorities are rebound. The retained after-action correction re-derives its routes from the restored board; the owed repeated instance carries identity alone and rebinds its effect from trusted definitions; and the mid-turn Lane-Push request carries its cause, because a restored board cannot tell a side emptied by a Defeat or Remove from one emptied by a move p.8 excludes. Registered component and capability sets ride the payload too, so a payload naming a superseded revision or a set this build does not register is refused rather than silently acquiring current semantics; no persisted state exists outside the test corpus. Component instance IDs are the deliberate exception: nothing parses or re-derives them, so a change to their composition needs no migration. The sixteen representative fixtures are pinned at v37, including one non-empty hero-Defeat arm and one real Thunder Boomerang second Attack paused with a non-default excluded hero; both wire forms round-trip byte-identically with the aggregate-invariance equality groups unchanged. Milestone 8.25e7 kept the schema and made content identity explicit: a payload pinned to Wasp's superseded `wasp-8-11-rev-1` is rejected rather than rebound to `wasp-8-25e7-rev-1`. Director Undo retains immutable initial scenario inputs plus accepted typed commands, rebuilds fresh authority, replays the retained prefix through the same typed `Execute` path, and verifies byte-identical state/events across two rebuilds. Each seat has that mechanism inside its own turn: one press reverts one accepted command, floored at the turn's start and walled above an Attack whose target it chose, published as `IsSeatUndoAvailable` and authorized by the host; the Director's own stays unbounded. Turns do not auto-end: a completed resolution waits for its owning seat's END TURN, end-of-turn timing fires at that release, and the seat's rewind reaches back into the finished resolution until the release is taken. Snapshots remain the separate persistence/diagnostics/resynchronization boundary.

Every enum in the payload is decoded through `SnapshotEnumReader`, which reads the int32 and refuses any value that is not a declared member of that enum under the typed `SnapshotLoadRejectionReason.UnrecognizedEnumValue`, naming the field, the type, and the offending value. The boundary guarantees membership only: rejecting `None`, or a member illegal in a particular position, stays with the domain constructors that own those rules. Declared members are cached per enum type rather than resolved through `Enum.IsDefined`, because `MatchAggregate.Rollback` runs this decoder on every refused or faulted command. Code-truth check 6 in the living-document validator fails on any raw `(SomeEnum)reader.Read…` cast remaining under `Core/Snapshots/`, so the guarantee is uniform across readers rather than dependent on which constructor happens to validate.

The default setup declares `official-single-lane` revision 1 and the official six-player configuration summarized above. It projects team/type minion art and each hero's official circular token where prepared, with tracked generated fallbacks. The twelve minions are authoritative unit obstacles, Defense contributors, and legal typed Attack/Defeat/removal targets; [BOARD_OPERATIONS.md](BOARD_OPERATIONS.md) owns Battle Zone and Lane-Push behavior.

Minion Battle counts effective minions and its pending/removal identity is a hero-or-minion `RuleObject`; a removed counts-as hero leaves the board without Defeat, reward, or Life change. At that hero's next selected-card opportunity, a pending p.7 choice offers every legal own-Throne-zone Hero Spawn, or the nearest empty space on the whole map when none is available, or resolves the card without respawn/action; the surface that presents it is [CONFIGURED_PRESENTATION.md](CONFIGURED_PRESENTATION.md)'s. Attack, direct Move, Object Push, Place, and Swap share `ImmunityQuery` for exact-activity declarations plus standing Heavy immunity at candidate and final mutation boundaries. A trusted node may bypass only that standing Heavy answer for its exact `DirectMove` or `Swap` operation; an independent declared immunity and p.18's self-action exception still dominate.
