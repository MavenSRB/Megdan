# Private-session bots

Canonical implementation contract for the lobby's FILL WITH BOTS control. Return to [private-online architecture](PRIVATE_ONLINE_ARCHITECTURE.md) or [project context](../PROJECT_CONTEXT.md).

## Ownership and execution

`PrivateLobbyDriver` owns one `PrivateSessionBotFleet` beside its human TCP listener. FILL creates host-owned clients only for open seats before the match starts; it never takes a claimed or reserved human seat. Repeated presses cannot create duplicate live bot clients. No child player process, socket, worker thread, or separate update loop is used for these bots.

Every bot is an ordinary `PrivateSessionClient` over encoded, framed, queued bytes. Negative connection ordinals are private to the fleet; the real TCP listener continues to use positive ordinals. Human bytes are forwarded to that listener without entering a bot endpoint. Keyed admission, roster/readiness, alternating draft, owner-scoped projection, command mapping, version/correlation checks, and host acceptance use the existing session protocol. The initial admission grant predates drafting; gameplay policy and team participation use the final hero on the current owner projection.

The fleet pumps at most 256 queued deliveries per pump, caps queued deliveries at 1,024, and makes at most one bot decision per Unity update, round-robin across live bots. Default think spacing is two seconds per bot. The existing `GUARDS_TESTDRIVER_THINK_SECONDS` test override accepts finite values from zero through 60. `BotPolicy.SelectPrivateSessionTactics` scores only that bot's owner projection and published commands with the existing tuned weights and seeded near-equal selection. No lookahead is supplied: the practice host's full-state search remains unavailable to lobby bots. Refused commands are remembered only at their projection version. This remains a bounded practice opponent, not a guarantee that every possible match finishes unattended.

Milestone 8.36g keeps `SelectTierTwo` constructible as the static measurement control and adds three content-generic families for the real lobby policy. Tactical geometry values crossing the final one-hex gap into legal combat and rewards Fast Travel only when it reduces the distance to a public enemy unit. Typed effect-choice previews value enemy removal/Attack and useful self-positioning; card choices distinguish retrieval from discard through registered capability references and rank the card's action structure rather than its name or printed prose. Feasible Skill starts compete with Attack and Movement instead of falling to the die, and confirmation is policy-scored rather than unconditionally forced.

Threat knowledge is bounded by the seat projection. Revealed/current commitments, public discard/turn-area cards, active-effect sources, and cards the bounded public match log previously named remain exact known cards. A played card returned to an opponent's hand stays in `PublicCards` while its revealing log entry is retained; never-seen hand and deck cards remain absent. Known hand Defense and known current/future Attack or Skill structure may affect commitment and target ranking. The bot never enumerates an opponent's hidden hand or deck, treats an absent identity as possible rather than known, and never receives the Director projection.

Bots ready and pick from their own lobby offers. They submit team statements through the normal agreement channel. A reserved human seat pauses all bot decisions; filling cannot replace that seat. Leave and destruction dispose local clients and queued work, shut down the host session, and release the human listener. The legacy standalone test-driver/rehearsal paths remain available for their existing separate validation purposes; they are not the lobby button's implementation.

## Compatibility and evidence

Draft choices use a separate deterministic random stream over the currently legal options, not the first available hero. Tests inject a draft seed; ordinary hosting generates and logs a public `LOBBY_BOT_DRAFT_SEED` once. Gameplay-policy randomness is independent and unchanged. The accepted picks still enter the ordinary authoritative roster/composition path, so random drafting does not introduce randomness into rules or replay. This changes hero variety, not tactical intelligence.

This wiring changes no rules, snapshot, map, card-content identity, or wire shape: protocol is `guards-private-online/12`, snapshot schema is v41. The public-card list now preserves a bounded, already-public identity after retrieval; it does not add a payload field. New builds retain the normal unique build stamp and require matching-build peers. In-process execution does not change the trusted-operator threat model in the architecture owner.

Focused failing-control/passing-candidate decisions, public-known/hidden uncertainty, retrieval memory, paired match evidence and work bounds live in `BotPrivateTacticsTests`; actual encoded fleet Fast Travel/combat, ownership/lifecycle, non-default draft and commitment coverage live in `PrivateSessionBotFleetTests`. The panel-button and teardown regression remains in `PrivateLobbyPlayModeTests`. Their recipes follow [testing policy](../Testing/POLICY.md). The original in-process integration and Tier-2 packaged-player evidence belong to [8.35z69 and 8.35z85](../Roadmap/PHASE_8_CLEANUP.md).
