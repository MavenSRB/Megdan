## Minions, timing, combat, and rewards

The twelve configured standard-minion records bind directly into authoritative board state. Their stable identities, teams, intrinsic Melee/Ranged/Heavy types, map-qualified spaces, and coordinates now enforce one-obstacle-per-space construction, Movement destination/transit blocking, and relocation emptiness without a presentation-derived or parallel minion representation. A discarded Defense card's typed value ordinarily adds p.14's minion contributions with stable `MinionId` provenance - +1 per adjacent friendly Melee/Heavy, -1 per adjacent enemy Melee/Heavy, -1 per enemy Ranged within radius 2 - read from the declared standard-minion content that also prices a minion Defeat. A trusted Defense definition may instead declare `SuppressAll`, in which case none of those minion contributions enters the total; the printed icon and every other contribution kind remain. Decline or no legal card still produces no Defense Total, and restore rebinds the configured minion collection and revalidates pending contributions. A minion's position is authoritative mutable state: a sealed `MinionOccupancy` carries a minion's identity, team, type, spawn, current space and coordinate, and is replaced through `WithPosition` so reference-identity change detection in the presentation refresh path still holds. `QuickGameMinionSetupState` returns to setup-only duty, converted at the board-authority boundary. Authority-internal `TryCommitMinionMove` validates identity, playable-space membership and index occupancy, replaces the list entry, keeps the occupancy index synchronized and reports a typed `MinionMoveResult`; its consumers are Object Push and Lane Push rather than any `DirectorCommand`. The snapshot writes each remaining minion's identity and current coordinate and restores the position from the stream rather than reconstructing it from setup placements.

Timing is pure named checkpoints plus a validated serializable Active-effect lifecycle. Definition content owns affected scope, trigger set, uniqueness key, `This Turn`/`This Round`/`Next Turn`/`Next Turn after Playing Cards` duration, creation/activation/expiration anchors, cancellation conditions, cross-round policy, and the declared modifications the effect supplies; an instance carries controller/team, affected hero, source card/action, creation order, phase, and activation anchors. Every live effect is visited at every checkpoint, and the context hero narrows only the identity-targeted concerns - activation targeting, triggers, and `ControllerHeroDefeated` cancellation - so a Hero-scoped effect reaches its own expiration window regardless of which hero is acting. That cancellation reads the effect's controller rather than its declared scope, because p.13 cancels the defeated hero's own actions and p.17 ends an effect on your card when "you are defeated". Uniqueness suppression compares the declared scope identity rather than one global key namespace, so different controllers activating the same definition coexist. Dormant/active/ended state and Ultimate/token/marker continuous sources round-trip in the current schema, and typed lifecycle facts stay deterministic through live execution, restore plus continuation, replay, and retained-prefix Undo. Printed-text parsing and Wasp content remain absent; the current duration vocabulary permits expiration only at the turn-only and round checkpoints.

The coin ledger is pure and runs over the participant collection. A typed Defeat outcome names its stable source/target, defeated team, acting hero and verified amounts; typed coin-gain operations record recipient, kind and before/after balances. The acting hero takes the Defeat award and every other friendly participant the Assist award, duplicate outcomes and friendly sources reject without mutation, and all recipients are planned atomically before balances change. Milestone 8.19b adds a positive card-effect grant and a two-hero transfer to that same writer. Feasibility validates known participants and overflow without mutation; transfer rederives the exact integer choice, plans debit and credit together, and commits neither if either balance would be invalid, so it cannot mint or produce a negative balance. A linked removal result remains a separate fact, so prevented removal cannot erase or duplicate an awarded Defeat. p.13's hero amounts are declared content rather than literals: the Defeat coins are the defeated hero's current Level, and the assist coins and the Life counters its lowest non-Basic card Tier read over every card it owns. The standard-minion coin values and p.14's minion Defense modifier table moved into declared content beside them, closing `F-ER2-09` with every number unchanged.

Enemy minions are part of typed synthetic Attack targeting. Non-Ranged requires adjacency; Ranged uses current typed Range; Heavy remains Immune while a friendly non-Heavy remains in the current Battle Zone. A legal minion target skips Defense and the hero-only Attack Total comparison, records ordered Defeat, the declared 2-coin Melee/Ranged or 4-coin Heavy reward, a removal attempt and a removed-or-prevented result, resolves the attacking turn card, and mutates authoritative occupancy. That last result comes from the same shared removal stage the standalone Remove and p.13's hero Defeat ask, and [BOARD_OPERATIONS.md](BOARD_OPERATIONS.md) owns it. No prevention source can answer yes yet: the only one the rules express is immunity, and an immune target is never a legal one. Milestone 8.13 opens the same minion Defeat stages to completed effect-frame requests: High Voltage's request awards the same type-priced coins, asks the same removal decision, mutates the same occupancy, and records the same immediate last-minion Lane-Push cause without duplicating the Attack path.

The verified general synthetic Hero Attack/Defense order is complete: stable target validation precedes Action Start/Before Attack, typed Attack Total, defender-owned response, explicit Block-or-numeric comparison, After Attack and action end, over the shared non-Ranged and Ranged target paths, with printed, minion, item, card-text, Active-effect and continuous-source contribution kinds keeping typed provenance. Trusted Attack definitions may now declare typed card-text Attack or Range additions whose match counts are rederived from current target/history/presence facts; Range is recomputed before target derivation and Attack after target selection, in both live and restored paths. An incoming Attack may also forbid only a `PrintedPrimary` Defense origin while preserving a legal Secondary reaction. The last contribution kind is rulebook p.19's persistent non-Active-effect source - a placed token's property today, an unlocked Ultimate since milestone 7.3 - and milestone 7.9 is when the projection learned to read it: before that it labelled the first three sources and threw on this one, so building a Defense preview for a hero who controlled a placed Hazard, whose property grants its controller Defense +1, made the match unplayable. The label is read off the contribution's own stable source id rather than resolved back to the object, exactly as an Active effect's is, so a fifth kind of continuous source needs nothing here. A successful Hero Attack resolves p.13's complete "When a Hero is Defeated" list in the page's order and inside the Attack, which p.14's numbered order places at step 6 ahead of the card's After-the-attack text; what that list does line by line is stated once in [Rules/RULE_SYSTEMS.md](../Rules/RULE_SYSTEMS.md). A successfully defended Attack runs none of it.

Spending a team's last counter ends the match; that path, p.8's Final Wave and its Throne capture now end it through one contract: the winner, the loser and the sentence that decided it are derived from the Lane-Push transition's recorded cause and winning side, the Life counters and the round phase, all already persisted. One event reports every ending, an ended match offers no decision and refuses every later command as `GameAlreadyEnded` without discarding where it ended, and an incoherent payload is refused on restore. The turn-command boundary that records an ending asks which ending the state holds rather than whether the Life counters ran out, so p.8's mid-turn Lane Push - the one consumed inside the turn's own Initiative advance, after the resolved card was acknowledged - records its ending like any other; the ended turn it leaves has no actor deciding what acts next, and the payload reader admits that shape from the phase it has already read. A push ending names an end-of-round stage only when an end-of-round push produced it, which is what distinguishes the two arms.
