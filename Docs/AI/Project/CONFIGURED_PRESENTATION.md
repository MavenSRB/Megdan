## Configured presentation surfaces

Corrective milestone 5.1a increases each of the ten explicitly synthetic test-card Movement values by one while preserving every other card value and stable definition ID. Its content revision advances so older synthetic snapshots reject instead of silently acquiring the new values.

Corrective milestone 5.1b made every configured legal Defense-card invocation carry the authoritative card display name and typed printed Defense value under the explicit `PRINTED DEFENSE` qualifier, and 5.1c removed the clipping width that reading had outgrown. What stays here is the boundary: the projected typed commands, the legal offer set, the unchanged `DECLINE DEFENSE` invocation and the compact fallback are authoritative, and presentation calculates no modified Defense Total, infers no eligibility, and resolves no reaction. How the response is drawn - structural since 7.8a, whole card faces since 8.11b - and the viewports it is proved readable at are [MATCH_HUD.md](MATCH_HUD.md)'s, which owns that surface.

Milestone 5.8b adds a pending-response-only immutable Defense-preview projection. It carries the complete ordered Attack contribution list/total, one authority-computed preview for every legal Defense card plus Decline, complete ordered Defense contributions, optional Defense Total, explicit Block result, predicted outcome, and readable typed-source labels. The application uses the same pure Defense query and shared typed comparison factory as command resolution; presentation only formats those facts and retains the existing resolution/choice-qualified one-click commands. How that popup is raised, dismissed and placed is [MATCH_HUD.md](MATCH_HUD.md)'s.

The same pending projection independently supplies the exact signed `MinionId` set from the already-calculated minion Defense modifier even when Decline is the sole choice. The passive board gives contributing minions persistent board-facing `+1` or `-1` text cues with smaller signed text, vertically oval dark-grey backings, and distinct restrained green/red sign colours, so sign never depends on colour alone and remains separate from selectable piece-target rings and washes. The number-only local horizontal inversion corrects the inherited token orientation; renderer-bounds compensation preserves horizontal centering, and a reviewed downward optical offset corrects the font's visually high vertical placement without changing the symmetric backing or adding an input surface. Cues remain stable across preview changes and rebuild only from a new immutable projection after response completion, rejection, Undo, restart, or restore.

The configured board merges Attack, Minion Battle, effect choices, correction subjects, and Clear into one `PieceTargetCueViewModel`, keyed by stable hero/minion/token identity and carrying state plus typed consequence. Each `PrimitiveHeroView`, `StandardMinionView`, and `PlacedTokenView` retains one target-cue child: its board-facing ring meets the renderer's local circular contour or convex base hull, while a genuinely blended wash repeats that footprint just above the art. Eligible uses eight thick segments, selected uses two rings, and the constructed but unprojected hover treatment uses one brighter solid ring with four connected ticks. Attack remains typed apart from Removal although both use the approved blood red; Affect uses the approved green; [PRESENTATION.md](PRESENTATION.md) owns the neutral keyline and bounded state-opacity table. Source art, labels, and signed Defense cues stay legible above the wash. The cue adds no collider or input surface and retains one mesh plus three materials across state changes and editor restoration. Presentation derives no candidate, allegiance, distance, Range, immunity, reward, outcome, or removal legality. Hero and minion selectors forward only stable identity; later projections such as Undo/restore can re-enable a retained view and reconstruct its cue.

Corrective milestone 5.8a1 makes the configured minion presentation reload-safe. Each generated `StandardMinionView` serializes its stable binding `MinionId` independently of transient setup objects, so editor backup/domain restoration can retain and reconfigure the correct object. When the grid deliberately destroys/replaces views, it publishes the complete current set only after rebuilding; the composition root atomically replaces `BoardInteractionUnityView` and `PrimitiveHeroPointerInput` bindings, creates/configures exactly one selector and collider per view, and removes destroyed references before any later session refresh. Rebuild, Play Mode entry, scene reload, commitment, target refresh, removal, Undo, restart, and restore retain the same twelve configured identities without `MissingReferenceException`, duplicates, or presentation-owned legality.

Milestone 6.1a adds the player-facing Minion Battle removal choice over milestone 6.1's unchanged authority. A sixth passive view/presenter pair - `IMinionChoiceView`, `MinionChoiceUnityView`, and `MinionChoicePresenter` - builds its whole model from the immutable pending choice: chooser team and identity, required count, current selection, one control per candidate the choice currently offers a select or deselect invocation for, and a confirmation control only while the choice reports `CanConfirm`. A candidate the authority withholds, which is how a Heavy appears while a non-Heavy is unselected, carries no control at all, so presentation never reproduces the Heavy-last ordering that withheld it. Controls read `REMOVE`/`KEEP` plus team, intrinsic type, and the candidate's ordinal in the projection's own candidate order, and the `CONTINUE END OF ROUND` control is withheld while a choice stands because the authority rejects that advance. Pointing at or focusing a control reports one stable `MinionId` to `BoardInteractionPresenter` and gives it the shared eligible Removal cue; a chosen identity keeps the selected Removal cue, which outranks eligible while the pointer moves. The cue is anchored to the projection it was read from, so an accepted command, refusal, restore, replay, or Undo drops it. Submitting matches the control against the current projection and forwards only that projected typed invocation through `IMatchSession`; a stale or refused control executes nothing and rebuilds the visible selection from the returned projection.

Milestone 6.4b routes the displaced-minion correction through that same interface rather than a second prompt framework: the one view model gained a prompt kind - Minion Battle removal, correction subject, correcting Move, fallback Place - and the presenter's submit, confirm, and stale-refresh paths never branch on it. A correction prompt is addressed by the owning action's identity, names the displaced stable minion, and offers one control per projected candidate: the deciding team's displaced minions at the subject stage (the correction-order amendment), or the exact equal-shortest route endpoints or equally-nearest Place spaces at the destination stage, labelled `RETURN`, `MOVE TO`, or `PLACE AT` so removal, correcting Move, and fallback Place stay distinguishable, with the shared gutter naming the family from the model. Selection is the advance, so a correction carries no separate confirmation. The board adapter lights a projected destination through the same highlight material Movement and Fast Travel use - since milestone 6.2d the one whose control is currently pointed at rather than all of them at once - and marks the displaced subject with the selected cue throughout a destination choice; clicking a space, lit or not, forwards only the projected invocation - presentation calculates no path, distance, Battle-Zone membership, occupancy, or Tie-Breaker ordering. Deterministic no-choice corrections render no prompt at all, and Actions, Fast Travel, and End Turn are withheld while a correction stands, because the authority holds the action open until it completes. The configured-scene driver gained the coordinate-valued submitters 6.8k deferred to this consumer: an effect-frame value candidate, a correction subject, and a correction destination are each addressed by what they select.

Milestone 6.5c gave a paused effect frame's decision the surfaces the rest of the configured match already uses, split on the option/value distinction the authority itself draws rather than on any card. A mode candidate keeps the control milestone 6.8a gave it, including its explicit confirm. **Every value candidate is answered on the board**, which is where milestones 8.11a and 8.11c took it: one naming an empty space lights it with the highlight material Movement, Fast Travel and corrections share; one naming a hero, a minion or a token receives the shared fitted piece cue and lights the space the authority projects the consuming operation would leave it on, in a second, distinctly dark material that is never selectable. Clicking either forwards that candidate's projected select invocation together with the confirm the authority offers immediately afterwards, so an exactly-one choice is answered in a single click without presentation composing a command of its own. Each keeps the name it always had - a hero's configured display name, a minion's projected team and intrinsic type, and since milestone 6.2d a token's projected definition name, the ordinal kept only where one name is offered twice - in its hover and assistive reading rather than as a control.

Milestone 8.11c made "names a piece" independent of how the candidate spells it. `EffectChoiceCandidatePreview` resolves a `HeroId` or a `MinionId` through authoritative occupancy and a coordinate through whatever stands on it, so a rider publishing the hero - Reflect Projectiles' does, because a discard cannot be asked of a space - is clicked like every other piece; two candidates resolving to one space is refused rather than collapsed; and "stands nowhere" is the board's answer rather than the value's kind, which keeps milestone 6.5b's supply arm below exactly as wide as it was. A zero-space Push marks the target's own space, since standing still is a legal result rather than a refusal. Both cues are anchored to the projection they were read from, so an accepted command, refusal, restore, replay, or Undo drops them, and a control whose candidate the current projection no longer offers submits nothing.

That preview is authority-computed, the way milestone 5.8b's Defense preview is: the projection carries, per space-valued candidate, the board object standing there and the space a consuming Push would leave it on, derived by reading the paused frame's own definition graph for the node that binds this choice's output and asking the same displacement query command resolution asks. Presentation derives no direction, distance, occupancy, or obstacle result, and no label or branch reads printed card text or a card, hero, or node name.

Milestone 6.5b adds the third arm of that split, for the value kind naming a piece that stands nowhere: a Spawn candidate names a miniature in the supply, which no board cue can point at and no highlight can light. It is a control like a mode, labelled from the declared setup roster's team and intrinsic type in the same form a standing minion's control uses, so a supply candidate and a board candidate read alike instead of one of them falling back to an ordinal. Nothing else about the branch reaches presentation: its destination is the miniature's own declared Spawn point rather than an offer, so there is nothing to light and nothing to confirm beyond the choice itself.

Milestone 6.2d gives a placed token a surface and retires milestone 6.2's decision that presentation receives no token projection. `MatchSessionProjection` carries the board authority's own placed-token instances - stable identity, coordinate, and the trusted definition object the catalog supplied - so a reader names a token from projected state and no presentation type holds a component catalog reference. The configured scene deals five cards per hero, the same five the default configuration every pinned digest and card-matrix assertion measures. Milestone 6.2d had dealt milestone 6.2's Cache card beside them as a sixth, because it was then the only route by which a player could put a token on that board; Blue Feint carries all three of that card's modes now, so the placement is a mode of the hand rather than a sixth card. It is on Feint rather than on Blue Sentinel, whose Swap/Replace/Remove family it belongs to, because rulebook p.8 removes every placed token at the end of a round and a card is played at most once in a round: a Sentinel supplying its own Hazard would never have one standing when it returned to hand. Feint at Initiative 12 and Sentinel at 11 are two cards of one hand, so a single round still holds the play that places the token and the play that acts on it. A projected token is drawn as a flat disc under a board-facing name at its coordinate, smaller than a unit and lower. It carries no pointer selector: a prompt control projects stable token identity into the shared piece cue. Its views follow the retained discipline hero and minion views follow, reached the only way a runtime-minted identity can reach it: a view is built the first time an instance is seen, bound while that instance is projected, released and hidden when it is not, and rebound when it returns, so removal, replacement, Clear, restore, replay, and retained-prefix Undo all redraw rather than leave a hole, and a projected token the board cannot bind is the same loud fault a projected minion without a view is. A Replace needs no separate handling, because both halves arrive in one projection and the space is never drawn empty between them.

The same milestone makes a destination highlight follow the pointer wherever the destination carries its own control. The pending correction's endpoints are that case: each already draws a `MOVE TO` or `PLACE AT` control, and lighting all of them at once said only that several spaces were involved. The board view model now carries a single nullable correction destination rather than a list, which is the shape the Hero Respawn highlight already had, and the prompt reports the whole pointed-at candidate rather than its unit alone - every candidate of a destination prompt shares the one displaced subject, so the unit could not tell them apart. The other families that draw one control per destination already behaved this way: Hero Respawn options and milestone 6.5c's named target controls each report a single pointed-at control. A candidate whose only surface is the board keeps its standing highlight, because there the lighting is the affordance and clicking the space is how the choice is answered; Movement, Fast Travel, and the effect choice's empty-space candidates are unchanged. Which spaces are legal, which one a click answers, and what any command carries are all exactly as before.

Milestone 8.16c applies that board-only contract to rulebook p.8's blocked-Spawn-point Lane-Push Place, the space-valued system-transition family the earlier board-first audits missed. The board model carries every equally-nearest empty destination in continuation order, paired one-to-one with the exact `SystemTransitionChoiceCommand` already offered; every space receives the standing selectable highlight before pointer movement. A coordinate click re-reads the current immutable description and forwards only its paired `AnswerSystemTransitionStep` command, so an unoffered coordinate, retained model, forged option, or repeated click executes nothing. Accepted placement, refusal, Undo, restart, restore, replay, transition completion, and rebuild clear or reconstruct the set from the current projection. The board derives no nearest-space, occupancy, obstacle, Spawn, Tie-Breaker, or option-identity rule.

Corrective milestone 6.3a gives milestone 6.3's implemented Clear the surface that milestone deliberately left out, and routes every control that names a token back to the piece it means. The projection carries one Clear option per currently available replaceable Attack - bound to that exact source card, actor, and replaced action - together with the adjacent tokens the existing authority query admits and the exact canonical `PerformClear` invocation for every confirmable subset, the empty one included, because p.13 permits any number and milestone 6.3 already accepts none. The configured action row draws `CLEAR (REPLACES ATTACK)` beside the card's other legal actions; a card with no current replaceable Attack draws nothing, which is a projected fact rather than a view's decision. Opening the selection accepts no command and changes no authoritative state. Since milestone 8.15a, at the user's direction, the offered tokens are chosen on the board: the prompt draws only `CONFIRM CLEAR` and `CANCEL CLEAR` - the two decisions with no board surface - and each offered token is marked and toggled where it stands. The presenter tracks only which projected candidates are selected, matches that set to one projected invocation, and forwards it unchanged - it derives no adjacency, eligibility, or replacement legality and composes no payload - and the confirm joins the legality set the round presenter validates every submission against. Cancelling submits nothing and leaves the card unresolved and the action choice untouched; the open selection is anchored to the projection it was read from, so a projection-version change, an accepted or refused command, End Turn, Undo, restart, restore, or replay drops it and rebuilds from current state.

The same milestone makes a token a thing the board can mark by identity: a candidate naming a token carries the stable `TokenInstanceId` rather than a display name or coordinate, and the board resolves that identity against the current projected token list alone, so presentation never reads occupancy back out of a space and a stale selection naming a moved, replaced, or removed piece marks nothing. `PlacedTokenView` retains the shared cue, and since milestone 8.15a its clear-cue input is the open selection itself rather than pointer state: every offered token wears `Eligible` where it stands from the moment the selection opens, every chosen one `Selected`, and a click on the offered token's space toggles it through the shared board intent chain - the token still carries no pointer selector, so the click resolves through its space. The same identity path covers token-valued effect targets. The fitted hex ring and wash remain distinct from destination highlighting, and pool reuse clears the prior identity before confirmation, cancellation, refusal, projection change, restore, replay, or Undo can redraw it.

Milestone 8.5a frames the board into the viewport the HUD leaves it rather than into the whole viewport. A tray card carries its face's complete printed text now, so the tray is taller than milestone 7.8a made it, and a map centred in the viewport had its lower-left corner behind the cards. `PlaceholderTestGridView` therefore reserves a declared share of the viewport's width along the left edge, sizes the map to the band that is left, and moves the camera left by half that reserve so the map is centred in the band - the camera looks straight down with world `+Z` up, so its screen right is world `+X`. The map widens only when it no longer fits the narrower band, which on the official map is barely at all: it is close to square and the viewport is 16:9, so most of what the tray needs is width the board was never using. Every space stays inside the camera's view, which is the property the framing case has measured since milestone 4.4, and the Play Mode HUD case adds the one this milestone is about, stated as the asymmetry the reserve produces rather than as a pixel count: the map's viewport margins are unequal, which the centred framing this replaced made equal by construction. The reserve is one serialized number, it was set by eye to where the board looked right rather than to the value that would clear the tray completely - so a little of the map is still behind the cards' corner - and zero restores the plain centred framing.

Milestone 8.11b draws each mirrored hero as the official circular token when a prepared face is present. It replaces the view's own mesh and materials rather than adding an object, so the `HeroId` everything binds to, the target cue, movement, Defeat and respawn visibility, Undo, restore and replay are untouched by the swap, and the capsule collider follows whichever piece was built, so picking stays what a player sees. The face is selected by projected content identity and authoritative team, never read: `Tools/Prepare-WaspHeroTokens.ps1` settled which source face is which off-line, under the reviewed Steam/orange-to-Red mapping. Those faces carry the source set's team colours rather than this project's, so the piece states the team again in a generated rim in this project's own board Red or Blue, from `TeamSide`. The artwork is private and ignored; a checkout without it keeps the prism.

## Maintenance rule

Corrective milestone 6.5aa widens the Minion-Battle surface from `MinionId` to projected hero-or-minion `RuleObject` candidates, so a counts-as hero receives the same authoritative remove/keep control and fitted Removal cue without presentation deciding its effective type. It also adds the p.7 Hero Respawn surface: one button per authoritative destination plus `RESOLVE WITHOUT ACTION`. Pointing at or keyboard-focusing a destination button previews exactly that coordinate using the same board highlight material as Movement; exit/blur clears it, and stale projection identity cannot submit or retain the preview.

Corrective milestone 6.5ab makes hero visibility projection-owned in the same retained-view style as minions. If a configured hero identity has no occupancy in the current projection, the board clears its cue and deactivates that hero GameObject and collider. If the identity returns through Respawn, Undo, restart, or restore, the board reactivates the same view and configures its new authoritative coordinate; presentation creates no removal or respawn rule of its own.

Corrective milestone 6.5ac gives that decision one more shape: an option the acting player cannot perform. Which options those are is projected by the authority and never inferred here. Such an option keeps its label and its ordinal and is drawn as a disabled control beside the ones that can be taken, so a player reads that the mode exists and cannot be chosen; there is no reason text, by the 2026-08-16 user decision recorded as open rules question 11. It carries no command at all rather than a refused one, which is why it is a view-model shape of its own, and the round presenter refuses any command naming it even when one survives on an older model. Corrective milestone 6.5af applies the same treatment one level out, to an action offer. A Skill the authority judged unstartable keeps its label and its place among the actions and is drawn as a disabled control carrying no command, so a player reads that the action exists, cannot be taken, and that the card's other actions and Hold are still theirs. Which offers those are is projected, not inferred here, and the round presenter refuses a start naming one even when a command survives on an older model. Milestone 8.4 gave that same disabled control its third and currently most common cause: since the configured scene deals verified Wasp content whose card text the 8.6-8.13 slices still owe, every printed primary the scene offers is drawn greyed, and what a player takes instead is p.12's Hold, p.13's Clear or Fast Travel, or the card's printed secondary. Nothing about the drawing changed; only how often it is reached.

Corrective milestone 6.5ae then gave that decision its own way out. Where no option of a mandatory choice can be performed, every option is drawn greyed and the authority projects one command: presentation renders it as the same End Turn control a paused Movement's stop has always used, and forwards it only when the player clicks it - never on a timer, and never as a consequence of any other command. Every other control stays withheld exactly as before, so End Turn is the only thing on screen that does anything, and nothing about the paused decision moves until it is taken.
