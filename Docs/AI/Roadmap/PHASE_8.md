### [x] 8.35z3 Degrade gracefully when a seat projection outgrows the session frame

Corrective, hardening the failure mode 8.35w's diagnosis recorded but its window could not remove: the completed-round window bounded the growth term, yet the host's own publish path still had no answer for an over-bound frame. `PrivateSessionFrameCodec.EncodeFrame` throws, the throw lands after `NextEnvelope` has already advanced the connection's send sequence, it unwinds `PublishProjections` for every seat behind the first, and the resync that should recover is served down the same throwing path - the recorded live shape (`tmp/private-online-solo-host.log`) is a whole table frozen mid-match with nothing on any screen. A sibling latch in `PrivateSessionClient` had the same silent-freeze character: a correlated projection judged stale under a reordered delivery was discarded before releasing `IsWaitingForHost`, muting that one seat forever.

Observable outcome: a seat projection message past the sealed frame bound is either delivered with the OLDEST match-log rows dropped - the log's own recorded scrollback semantics - or, when the projection payload alone cannot fit any legal frame, refused loudly for that one connection with `SessionRejectionReason.OversizedFrame` and a named detail while every other seat is still served; the connection's send sequence advances only beside a frame that exists. A reordered correlated projection judged stale still releases the client's input latch on its way out.

Design: `NetworkMessageCodec.TrySeatProjectionFrameWithin` composes the frame to a caller-stated budget, measuring each log row through the one real row writer and keeping the largest newest suffix; it returns false, never throws, when the prefix alone cannot fit. The host's one budget is `PrivateSessionHost.ProjectionFrameBudgetBytes = PrivateSessionSecureChannel.MaxSealedPayloadBytes` - the tighter of the two bounds every frame must eventually pass, so `Seal` can never throw either. `PublishProjections` iterates a snapshot so the refusal's `RejectAndClose` cannot unwind the loop, `TrimmedProjectionLogRows` counts degradation observably (zero in any healthy match), and `ResyncServed` is recorded only when a frame was actually delivered.

Acceptance checks:

- An over-bound projection-and-log message composes to a trimmed frame that decodes under the ordinary read discipline carrying exactly the newest row suffix, with the raw encoder's `"A payload is bounded."` throw pinned beside it; a payload only the looser plaintext bound could carry is refused, proving the budget is the sealed bound.
- A message already inside the bound crosses the bounded compose byte-for-byte identical to the plain encoder, so byte-level convergence still measures one encoder.
- The reordered correlated-echo case fails on the permanently latched seat with the client release reverted and passes with it restored.

Non-goals: no wire vocabulary, protocol version, or schema movement - `OversizedFrame` is 8.31 vocabulary and a shorter row list is a smaller legal payload in the unchanged frame layout; no change to what any healthy match sends (byte-identity is an acceptance check); the 8.35w growth windows and their deliberate-resize guards are untouched.

### [x] 8.35z4 Fix the four defects the first live internet playtest found

Corrective, live-defect-driven, and the first milestone whose defect list was written by a real six-human internet session rather than by a rehearsal - though its own evidence, as always, is local. Territory: Presentation/Match only - no Core, Application or Network file is touched. The four defects are not independent: the first starved the surfaces the second and fourth are read on, which is why the playtest reported them as one vague "nothing animates and the words are wrong".

Observable outcome: on every client, the round panel narrates the true state instead of freezing at its last good sentence; the top bar, narration and END TURN banner name the seat's own hero rather than treating every hero's decision as the viewer's; one press of END TURN ends the turn over the real network client, not two; and a floating attack or defense figure appears at the moment its lunge lands rather than before it, holding legibly for about a second.

Design:

- **The keystone.** `RoundActionPresenter.FormatStatus`'s two team-agreement branches handed an EMPTY owed-set to `NarrationLabels.WaitingOn`, which refuses it by design. An agreement every participant has already answered is an ordinary transient state that every seat refreshes through, so from the first one the panel threw on every refresh. Milestone 8.35h's panel isolation then contained the throw to that one panel - which is what turned a crash into something quieter and worse, because everything else riding the same refresh, the kinetic cue emission included, was starved. Fixed at the CALLER via `FormatAgreementWait`: an empty owed-set is a real state and gets `NarrationLabels.EveryAnswerIn`. `WaitingOn` stays defensive so the next forgetful caller is still caught.
- **The identity.** A networked client cannot know its hero at composition time; the lobby grants the seat afterwards. `ConfiguredMatchSceneRuntime` composed all three seat-scoped presenters with `Seat?.HeroId ?? default` and repaired exactly one, the other two holding their identity in `readonly` fields. The undefined hero is not a neutral blank - it is the DIRECTOR's identity, and the Director owns every decision at once, so every "is this mine?" predicate read an unrepaired seat as "yes". New `ISeatScopedPresenter`, both fields made mutable with a validating `SetHomeHero` that drops the per-projection cache, and one `BindHomeHero` fan-out over `SeatScopedPresenters` that refuses loudly rather than leaving a half-bound HUD.
- **The one press.** The compose loop in `SubmitEndTurn` re-reads `session.Projection` after every step, which is exact against a synchronous client and wrong against the wire: `NetworkMatchClient.Submit` returns as soon as the command is SENT, handing back the pre-command projection, and `PrivateSessionClient` refuses every further submission while `IsWaitingForHost`. The press is now remembered and resumed - armed when the input gate is closed and the state still concerns the press's own actor, continued from `Refresh`, which the online frame loop already calls - bounded to that one actor and to `MaxEndTurnResumes` resumptions so it can neither spend a second player's turn nor become permanent. **That bound was unreachable as first written**, and the six-client rehearsal is what found it: `ArmOrClearEndTurnContinuation` reset the counter unconditionally, and every resumption re-arms through it, so the comparison could never be met and a press whose submissions kept being refused resumed on every frame forever - live, one seat spent 17,486 refused submissions at a single authoritative version while the table stood still. The budget now belongs to the press rather than to each resumption of it: only a fresh press resets it.
- **The pace.** `Begin` started the floating figure at `Elapsed = 0`, the same instant as its cue, but a lunge's `Strike` curve does not peak until 34% in, so the number preceded its own cause by about 95 ms. The figure now begins one impact-delay behind the clock, derived from the curve's own peak rather than a literal, and `FloatingNumberSeconds` moves 0.90 -> 1.5.

Acceptance checks:

- The round panel survives every projection of a real seeded six-seat match for every viewer, the Director included, with the panel failure sink empty - the guard that catches this whole class rather than the one branch that failed.
- Every phase that can build a waiting-on line is staged with an empty owed-set, and the `WaitingOn` vocabulary still refuses to name nobody.
- Every presenter constructed with a viewer identity declares itself seat-scoped, the runtime exposes exactly those, and the fan-out binds exactly that roster - so a fourth seat-scoped presenter cannot be added and forgotten the way two were. A binding for a hero this build does not have is refused rather than stored.
- One END TURN press over an ASYNCHRONOUS client ends the turn with no second press, and a second impatient press mid-round-trip still lands exactly one `ReleaseTurn`. The existing one-press case is unchanged, so the ONLY variable between them is the client.
- A press whose submissions are always refused spends its budget instead of resuming forever: bounded at one press plus twelve resumptions, and asserted to be greater than one so it cannot pass against a mechanism that never armed. Observed at 201 - one submission per frame, without end - with only the reset guard reverted.
- The two multi-command press shapes the batch owed are driven over the asynchronous client, not only the Hold: the movement Stop at two commands, and the open Attack target at three, the deepest composition there is.
- A figure appears at its lunge's impact and never before it, and holds for about a second before beginning to fade. Three cases that asserted the old timing are re-timed with them - a deliberate behaviour change, not a weakened assertion.

Non-goals: no Core, Application or Network change; no wire protocol, snapshot schema or content revision movement; the `!IsDefined` Director branches are deliberately left standing, because they are correct and load-bearing for the Director, whose runtime legitimately passes the undefined hero.

### [x] 8.35z5 Close Living Tsunami's empty Confirm, and the whole class it belongs to

Queued corrective. Arien's Living Tsunami - "Once per turn, before performing an Attack action, you may move 1 space" - projected BOTH a Confirm carrying an empty selection and a Decline for the one "no" answer, and the rules accepted the Decline while refusing the Confirm as `TargetUnavailable` / `IncompleteSelection`. A player who pressed the offered Confirm was refused for choosing nothing when choosing nothing was legal, just spelled differently.

Observable outcome: a `may`-wrapped step whose handler needs a real selection offers exactly one empty answer - Decline - and never a Confirm the rules will refuse; selecting and confirming still performs the step.

Design: `EffectResolutionSession.ApplyCapabilityResult` forced a `may` node's projected `minimumSelections` to 0 (milestone 8.20a's fix for "Living Tsunami reached the table as four spaces and no way to stay put"), and that same 0 also relaxed `PendingEffectChoice.ConfirmInvocation`'s gate. Two different questions - is the whole step declinable, and how many must a taken answer name - were answered with one number. The printed reading settles which owns the truth: "you may move 1 space" leaves the distance exact (p.11's unqualified number) and makes only the move itself optional, so Confirm keeps the handler's own floor unmodified and Decline is offered from the node's `may` policy directly, through a new `declinableRegardlessOfMinimum` flag derived fresh at every pause and restore - never persisted - exactly as `UnavailableCandidates` and `StopInvocation` already are. `up to` needs no change: a handler admitting it declares its own floor of zero. The fix is at the seam, so it repairs the whole class with no content edit - Tigerclaw's five "you may move 1 space" faces, his Evade's retrieve, and Sabina's and Brogan's `may`-wrapped Retrieve and owned-card Discard.

Acceptance checks:
