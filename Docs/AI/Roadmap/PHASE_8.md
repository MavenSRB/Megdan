### [x] 8.35z13 Make the practice bots fight: greedy-legal seat drivers instead of random-legal

Third of the four wave-one integration milestones, on milestone 8.35q's spawned test drivers.

Observable outcome: a bot-filled seat attacks when an Attack is offered, prefers the target it is closest to killing, walks toward the fight rather than at random, and spends a Defense card when declining would kill it - so a solo playtest shows a match that progresses instead of six seats milling about.

Design: three deterministic preferences layer over the random picker in `PrivateSeatDriver`, each reading only facts already published on the seat's own `IMatchReadModel` and each falling back to random-legal the instant its own facts are absent. Preferring to START an Attack is safe unconditionally because `TurnResolutionAuthority.CanStartAttack` already refuses to offer one with no legal target. Movement prefers the lowest hex distance to the nearest enemy hero or minion, minion position standing in for the lane battle because the live Battle Zone id is Director-only board state a seat never receives. Every preference is a filter computed BEFORE the random tiebreak, so per-seed determinism is preserved.

**A finding recorded as content, not as a rule (user, design-side).** In the current content a Decline is unconditionally `AttackSuccessful`. That is CONTENT-DEPENDENT and must never be promoted to an invariant: unimplemented heroes may carry decline-triggered saves, and removal prevention already exists today as a post-hit save. No code change was owed because the bot reads the per-state preview rather than baking the assumption in, and that guard is the load-bearing line.

Acceptance checks:

- Each preference has a positive case and a fallback case, and a same-seed-twice case proves seeded determinism survives.
- Commitments, leveling picks and team-agreement convergence stay random/untouched, as durable non-goals rather than oversights.
- A measured offline comparison records the change in attackable turns converted into an Attack.
- The SixClient Solo rehearsal completes with bot-filled seats at the wave's closing revision.

Non-goals: no search, no evaluation function, no difficulty setting, no rules or wire change; commitment quality stays out of scope by design.

Historical evidence: [Phase 8 handoff](../Archive/Phase-Handoffs/PHASE_8.md#milestone-835z13-make-the-practice-bots-fight-greedy-legal-seat-drivers-instead-of-random-legal-completion-evidence).

### [x] 8.35z14 Name the blocker in a mid-resolution fizzle, and advance the wire to /8

The SIXTH amendment's remaining known-problems entry. Last of the four wave-one integration milestones.

Observable outcome: when a mandatory effect step cannot be performed and the action fizzles mid-resolution, the notice names WHO blocked it and with WHAT - the same "blocked by HERO's CARD" vocabulary milestone 8.35l's offer-time tooltip already uses - instead of naming only the acting hero and card.

Design: `EffectOptionFeasibilityQuery`'s two-step refusal question (the refusing node's own explaining handler, else the consumer that narrowed a selection to nothing) is extracted and reused by `EffectResolutionSession` at both sites that produce `ImpossibleMandatory`. The answer rides the existing `EffectResolutionEvent` as a new `Blocking` property and deliberately NOT `EffectNodeOutcome`/`PendingResolution`: that record persists into a snapshot only while a resolution is genuinely paused, and `ImpossibleMandatory` always completes the whole resolution - so **no snapshot payload and no schema is touched, and the schema stays v36**. The blocking source is always a standing PUBLIC Active effect (rulebook p.17), so naming it reveals nothing a viewer's projection does not already carry. `NetworkMatchLogRow` gained two optional fields appended after `amount`, which is a wire-format change: `guards-private-online` 7 -> 8, riding the existing BlockedAction row rather than a new message kind, on the same reasoning 8.35r and 8.35z3 record.

- The log projection carries both halves only when defined, and the notice falls back to its generic line wherever either half is absent - never a guessed blocker.
- The wire round-trips a row with and without a blocker, bare and through the full seat-projection frame, and the seeded six-seat walk's frame-size guard still holds with the new fields present.

**A deterministic brain cannot escape an infeasible offer by luck, and the first live Solo cohort is what proved it.** The shipped driver did nothing on a refusal: it re-ticked, recomputed the same decision from the same unchanged projection, and submitted the identical command again. Under random-legal, and under the tier-0.5 pass that falls back to it, the next tick drew something else and the seat moved on. Tier-1 is deterministic, so one offered-then-refused command became a hot infinite loop - the cohort spun more than eleven thousand refusals on a single seat at a frozen version and the match never advanced past it. The cause is not the brain; it is the offer-feasibility defect recorded below as corrective debt, which the brain turned from a latent hazard into a permanent one. The driver now remembers what was refused at the current projection version, drops it, and chooses again from what remains - which is exactly what the self-play harness already did, and therefore what makes the shipped driver behave the way the measured configuration behaved. It never widens the candidate set, so every answer is still one the seat was offered; and when every offer at a version has been refused it says so once rather than spinning silently.

**Two engine defects this milestone found and did NOT fix, both reproducible and neither in bot territory.** The projection offers a Defense card the rules then refuse, with the preview itself listing it (three independent seeded repros; the same family as milestones 8.35l and 8.35z15). ### [x] 8.35z24 Answer a Defense pause whose target stopped being reachable, instead of refusing every answer to it

Corrective for the second of the two engine defects milestone 8.35z22 found and deliberately did not fix, and it was worse than that milestone could see. The report was that the projection and the seat's decision contract offer a Defense card the rules then refuse. What the reproduction found is that the refusing guard sits ABOVE BOTH of rulebook p.14's answers, so it refused the whole pause - every card and the Decline alike. **A pause no answer can leave is a dead end, not an infeasible offer.** It is what the tier-1 practice bot livelocked on: a deterministic brain re-picks identically, so it re-submitted a refused answer for ever, and 8.35z22's driver fix - dropping a refused command for that projection version - contains that symptom without curing it, because with every answer refused the seat still has nothing legal to send. A human reaching the same state has a match that cannot continue.

**The projection was right and the rules were wrong**, which is the opposite of the two most recent members of this family. `AnswerDefense` re-derived whether the attacker can still REACH his target, where p.14 asks only whether the target is still STANDING. That narrowing is not new: milestone 8.23 made it, recorded it at length on the comparison site, and wired it into exactly one of the three places that ask the question. This is milestone 8.35z15's unwired-probe class exactly - a rule recorded once and implemented by one of its callers - and the reason it went unwired is that the rule lived only in a code comment. It is now a row in [RULE_SYSTEMS.md](../Rules/RULE_SYSTEMS.md), where the next caller will find it.

Observable outcome: A Defense pause is answerable. The targeted player may spend an eligible Defense card, or decline, and the attack resolves either way, in the state where the attacker named a legal target at p.14's step 2 and then legally moved out of contact before step 3. No answer the seat was offered is refused for a reason p.14 does not print, and the offer set is unchanged - nothing is newly greyed and no refusal reason is minted, because under `PLAN.md` RULING 17 an option is greyed only when it cannot be performed, and every one of these answers can be. Reconciling the other way would have withheld the Decline as well and left the pause with no answer at all.

**How the state is reached, and it needs no defender card text.** 8.23's case needed a Defense text that moved the defender at step 4. The face refutes it: it moves *another* unit adjacent to the target, so attacker and target stay exactly as far apart as they were. The discriminator that is actually right is the move's ORIGIN - a body that relocates the attacker names his own space first - which the card text says and the node graph does not.

**Recorded, not decided.** The printed order is move-then-target; the engine asks target-then-move. That deviation is what makes this state reachable at all, and changing it is an offer-feasibility content decision of the 8.35p kind, needing a content ruling rather than a corrective. Milestone 8.23 shipped and verified the current ordering, so it is recorded here and left alone.
