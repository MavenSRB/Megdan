### [x] 8.35f Give each seat a bounded undo inside its own turn's card resolution

Corrective milestone from the user's live multiplayer testing on 2026-09-03, approved the same day ahead of milestone 8.36's human gate and paired with 8.35d and 8.35e. Undo was the Director's alone - `UndoRestartPresenter` is null for a seat-scoped runtime and `SeatSubmission` deliberately could not name it - so a player who misplayed a step of their own resolution had no way back at all. The user's rule is printed: each player gets an undo, usable only while their own cards are being resolved, clickable step by step all the way back to the beginning of their turn, and never through an Attack, because once a target has been selected the defender's answer is information that cannot be un-shown.

Observable outcome: During its own turn's card resolution a seat presses one UNDO control to step the match back one accepted command at a time, as far as the state its turn began in and no further, and never into or through an Attack whose target it has chosen; every other seat sees the board step back with it, and nothing else about who may do what changes.

Acceptance checks:

- A seat resolving its own turn is published a rewind exactly while the latest accepted command stands above the floor its turn began at; each accepted press reverts exactly one command and moves the authoritative version forward; the presses run out at that turn's own beginning rather than reaching its commitments, the Initiative that selected its actor, or a previous turn.
- After a target is chosen, no sequence of presses ever takes back the target selection or anything inside rulebook p.14's action: the acting seat is published no rewind while the action resolves and is refused by name when it asks anyway, and the floor stands immediately above the settled action.
- The same claim stated over a whole run rather than one position: a seeded seat walk pressing the control at every third state that offers one never lowers the count of Attack target selections in the recorded stream, and the case fails rather than passes when it observed no Attack or took no rewind.
- Off-turn seats, teammates and opponents are published no rewind and are refused `UndoUnavailable`; the Director's own unbounded Undo, Restart and snapshots stay unreachable from every player surface, and the seat's own request carries no seat, no count and no target - the host answers all three.
- A stale expected version and a repeated submission identity rewind exactly once between them, so a double click cannot take two steps; a refused rewind moves no version, no recorded stream and no snapshot.
- Determinism holds across a rewind: the retained prefix replays to the state the rewind left, and that state's snapshot restores to itself.
- Across the wire, six clients converge on the rewound board with every other hero's hidden cards still absent from the bytes; a protocol 2 peer is refused before any payload is interpreted, because the seat record carries the new fact in the middle of it; and a rewind asked while a reserved seat holds the session is refused `SessionPaused` without moving anything and is taken after that seat returns.
- The seat HUD draws the control as a square on the status strip's top edge immediately left of END TURN, glyph only and no words, standing exactly when the host published the rewind and absent from the Director's drawer.

Non-goals: No redo. No undo of commitments, Initiative, agreements, the Minion Battle, end-of-round decisions, or anything outside the acting seat's own resolution; no Director change and no seat restart. No change to the persisted snapshot schema, to rulebook p.14, or to milestone 8.36's execution. The explicit turn release the user amended in on 2026-09-03 is milestone 8.35g's work, not this one's. The pre-existing malformed-checkpoint replay refusal this milestone's own sweep reached at a deep history - which the Director's unbounded Undo answers identically from the same state, and which no part of this milestone introduced - is recorded here and corrected separately.

Historical evidence: [Phase 8 handoff](../Archive/Phase-Handoffs/PHASE_8.md#milestone-835f-give-each-seat-a-bounded-undo-inside-its-own-turns-card-resolution-completion-evidence).

### [x] 8.35g Hold a finished turn until its own player releases it

User-approved amendment of 2026-09-03, added to milestone 8.35f's approval the same day once the user saw what the undo could not reach: "If user makes the wrong movement, the turn auto ends, so no undo will fix that. We need to stop auto ending turns." Today a card resolution completing is the hand-off - `TurnResolutionAuthority.AdvanceInitiative` acknowledges the resolved card and selects the next actor inside the same accepted command - so the last action of a turn is unreachable by 8.35f's rewind, whose floor and walls only exist while that turn is still being resolved. A seeded walk shows the shape exactly: at seed 3 an Attack's target selection at command 287 both resolves the attack and ends Arien's turn. This is a digital-UX affordance rather than a rules change: tabletop turn handoff is implicitly consensual, and the release makes that consent explicit online.

Observable outcome: When the acting hero's card resolution completes, the match holds still in a `turn-release` pause owned by that hero's seat instead of advancing. The seat's UNDO keeps working through the pause and reaches back into the resolution it just finished. END TURN, pressed again, is the release: it runs the end-of-turn timing and hands the turn on to the next actor, or into end of round where milestone 8.35d's continuation takes over.

Acceptance checks:

- A completed card resolution leaves the match in the release pause rather than selecting the next actor: the same round, the same turn index, and the acting hero's own seat owning the one standing decision. The pause is the eleventh declared pending family, crossing the projection and the wire like the other owned ones.
- The release is one recorded `DirectorCommand`, so replay, retained-prefix Undo, snapshots and restore see it as the ordinary stream entry it is. End-of-turn timing - the turn-only cancellation checkpoint and `TurnEnded` - fires at the release and not at resolution completion, so an undo inside the pause never crosses checkpoint state.
- Milestone 8.35f's rewind is available through the whole pause and steps back into the just-finished resolution under its unchanged floor and walls; the amended availability rule is "while this seat's resolution is active OR while its completed resolution awaits release".
- Only the seat whose hero resolved the turn may release it: a teammate's and an opponent's release are both refused without moving the version, the stream or the snapshot, and the Director releases directly from its own door.
- The pause follows every resolution completion uniformly, including one caused by an explicitly labelled END TURN command - a Movement Stop, an effect stop or a Hold - so an explicit stop needs a second press and that second press is the turn's point of no return. A hero that passed or holds an empty hand resolves no card and gets no pause. A resolution stopped by the game ending gets none either, because nothing further is asked of anyone.
- A mid-turn Lane Push standing at its own checkpoint is answered first and the release pause opens after it, so two decisions never stand at once; the same holds for a triggered effect that stopped on a choice.
- The persisted pause moves the snapshot schema to v35 with the full re-pin: every superseded version is rejected rather than migrated, the pinned-constant ledger's six documents state v35, the corpus fixtures are renamed and re-digested, and the byte delta against v34 is measured rather than computed.
- Every driver and seeded journey that ends a turn presses the release: the deterministic match loop, the player smoke driver, the private-online rehearsal driver and the Play Mode journeys. Their re-derived seeds are recorded, because one extra command per turn reroutes every seeded trajectory.
- Both complete suites pass at the final revision.

Non-goals: No rules change - rulebook p.12's turn structure is untouched and the release adds no step to it. No redo, no change to milestone 8.35f's floor or walls, no Director undo/restart change, no presentation pacing, no wire protocol change beyond the new command kind and the new pending family, and no 8.36 execution.

### [x] 8.35i Restore a snapshot captured while a choice is paused inside an Attack

User-approved corrective, queued after 8.35g from milestone 8.35f's own finding. 8.35f's rewind sweep reached a refused Undo at a history of 1,262 commands - `Undo replay failed without changing the match: Snapshot payload is malformed or internally inconsistent` - and the diagnosis relocated it out of the undo machinery entirely: `MatchSession.Reconstruct` replays from a retained checkpoint, and deserializing that pre-image threw. Replaying the same stream into a fresh host and round-tripping after every command named the real cause at command 1,067: **`Snapshot pending effect choice names node 'noble-other', whose capability no longer requests a choice.`** That is milestone 8.29c's documented recurrence, and it is shared authority rather than the HUD path 8.29c assumed, which is why it is corrected here rather than there.

The cause is a question asked with half of what it reads. Candidates are never written to a payload - a capability's come from asking the trusted handler again, which is what stops a payload inventing an authoritative option - so a restore rebuilds the question. Arien's Noble Blade binds its selection to `EnclosingTargetBeforeConsequences`: "another unit adjacent to the target of this Attack". The restore path rebuilt that question with no enclosing Attack at all, so the origin resolved to nobody, the handler offered no choice, and the payload was refused for describing a pause its own content "no longer" requested. The content had never changed.

Observable outcome: A match paused on a capability choice inside an Attack's card text captures and restores like any other, offering the same candidates it offered before, and the Director's Undo and a seat's rewind both step through such a state instead of refusing at it.

Acceptance checks:

- A snapshot captured while a capability choice is paused inside an Attack restores, and the restored pause names the same node, the same candidates and the same selection bounds - then takes its own published answer. Asserting only that it restores would pass a build that restored the pause and forgot what it offered, which is the same defect one layer down.
- Every state of the reproducing command stream round-trips through a snapshot, checked after each accepted command rather than at the end, because the defect was invisible until one specific intermediate state was captured. The case fails if the stream never passes through a choice paused inside an Attack, so it cannot pass by walking a match that never reaches the position.
- The enclosing Attack is rebuilt by the authority's own `EnclosingAttackOf` rather than by a second builder in the serializer - the method the live path already uses, whose own record refuses "a second place the same fact could be wrong".
- No byte moves and no version changes. The Attack is written after the frame that pauses inside it, so the frame is read as a function of it, which is the answer milestone 8.23 gave for the opposing hero the payload also names late. Snapshot schema stays v35 and the wire stays `guards-private-online/4`.
- The serializer's generic wrapper names its cause in the message rather than only chaining it, because callers surface a refusal by `Message` alone and an `InnerException` nobody reads is a cause nobody reads. Diagnostic only: the rejection reason and the refusal behaviour are unchanged.

Closing check: `AttackCardTextSnapshotRestoreTests` - both cases - recorded failing with the fix off at its one behavioural site and passing with it on.

Non-goals: No tolerance is widened and nothing is caught more broadly; a payload that really is malformed is refused exactly as before. No schema, wire, command, rules or presentation change. Milestone 8.29c is not reopened: its own HUD-path symptom remains unreproduced, and what is fixed here is the shared-authority mechanism 8.35f recorded under its name. ### [x] 8.35k Give a relaunched player a road back to their own reserved seat

User-approved corrective of 2026-09-03, reported from the live playtest verbatim: "rejoin does not work, if user disconnects, its seat stays away, and connection is refused." Milestone 8.34 built the whole return mechanism - a reserved seat, a host-minted capability, a reclaim message, a rotated epoch - and it works. What it never had was a road to it. The capability lived in one private field of one live `PrivateSessionClient`, and three ordinary player actions end that object: the process exiting, the process crashing, and pressing LEAVE. After any of them the lobby could offer only a FIRST join, which the host refuses by name because the seat is reserved. Every reconnect fixture at every tier - the six-client rehearsal included - replaced only a socket beneath a surviving client, which is the one path a real player never walks.

Observable outcome: A player whose game died relaunches it, enters the same invitation, and is returned to their own seat with a rotated epoch and a complete projection. A player who deliberately leaves gives that seat up instead. And the host can hand a reserved seat back by a deliberate act, for the evening where a player's machine is gone and no stored capability can exist.

### [x] 8.35t Dial the address the host actually bound, not a hardcoded loopback constant

Corrective, live-defect-driven. Milestone 8.35q's FILL WITH TEST DRIVERS spawned every test-driver child with its dial address hardcoded to `"127.0.0.1"`, regardless of what the host itself had bound - so a host bound to this machine's own real network address (the shape the tester instructions' own solo-practice paragraph invites, and the one a host rehearsing its real port-forwarded setup even alone requires) spawned children that dialed an address nothing was listening on. Each pressed Join, and each then sat forever: `PrivateLobbyPresenter.Press` returns true the instant the control is offered, saying nothing about whether the join it triggered ever reached anyone, so nothing ever told the driver its dial had failed - the live report named 34 orphaned `GuardsDigitalSmoke.exe` processes left running after repeated FILL presses tried to work around it.

Observable outcome: a spawned test driver dials whatever address the host actually bound - loopback by default, this machine's own address when the host bound one, falling back to loopback only for a wildcard bind, since every spawned child runs on the host's own machine regardless. A join that cannot reach anyone now fails within its own dial attempt, is logged and reported on the host's own screen by name, and the driver process exits promptly instead of hanging. A second FILL press no longer spawns a redundant child onto a seat already carrying an outstanding attempt, and every spawned child now also watches its own spawning host's OS process id and exits if that host disappears without a clean Leave.

Design:

- `PrivateSessionEndpoint.ResolveLocalDialAddress()` (Network) is the one place a wildcard bind resolves to loopback and any concrete address is returned verbatim - confirmed directly against a real `TcpListener`/`TcpClient` pair, not assumed. It lives in the adapter, not beside its one caller in Presentation, because that caller may name no socket type of its own (`PrivateSessionArchitectureConformanceTests.SocketTypes_LiveOnlyInTheNetworkAdapter`).
- `PrivateSeatDriverOptions.TryParse` no longer confines a spawned driver's dial address to loopback - the assumption the live defect disproved. `-guardsPrivateClient` has always been free to dial any address with any key, and the session key is what actually gates entry here too; only a numeric, well-formed endpoint is still required.
- `PrivateLobbyDriver.BeginJoining`'s two silent failure paths - a malformed endpoint and a failed `Dial()` - now set `LastJoinFailure` and log through `PrivateOnlineMarkers.Fail`, which a spawned `PrivateSeatDriver` checks immediately after pressing Join and fails loudly on if set.
- `FillEmptySeatsWithTestDrivers` tracks a 12-second per-seat join deadline: a seat still open when its deadline passes is reported by name, in the log and the lobby's own feedback line, and freed for a later FILL to retry; a seat already carrying a live deadline is skipped by a repeated press rather than sent a second child.
- Every spawned driver also carries its parent host's OS process id and polls it independently of `Leave()`/`OnDestroy()`, so a host that dies without a clean shutdown still orphans nothing.

Acceptance checks:

- `PrivateSeatDriverTests`: `ResolveLocalDialAddress` returns a concrete bind verbatim, falls back to loopback for both the IPv4 and IPv6 wildcard; a non-loopback dial address is now accepted where it was refused, a malformed one is still refused; a parent process id parses when present and leaves the watchdog off when absent or unparsable. Pre-fix red, isolated (both call sites behavior-reverted in place, new vocabulary kept present and inert, per this repo's own ceremony rule): exactly the two cases pinning the defect fail, 18/20; restored, 20/20.
- `PrivateLobbyPlayModeTests.AJoinToAHostThatIsNotThere_IsDrawnOnTheEntryForm` now also expects the new host-log FAIL line the fix introduces (`LogAssert.Expect`), a companion fixture update this milestone owes since it added that line.
- `Invoke-PrivateOnlineRehearsal.ps1 -Topology Solo -FaultPlan None`: the packaged interactive-lobby command line (both shipped `.bat` launchers' own flag shape) still boots cleanly to its real lobby; a join dialed at a port nothing is listening on now fails fast and visibly (exit 1) instead of hanging; the host presses FILL WITH TEST DRIVERS twice back to back and spawns exactly five test-driver clients, not ten, all five dialing the host's own address, before all six seats claim and the match begins.

Non-goals: No wire, protocol, or schema change. No change to the host's own bind rules (8.35a1's unkeyed-bind refusal is untouched). No AI, strategy, or difficulty change to the test-driver's own random-legal play.

### [x] 8.35u Level every hero at the same time

FOURTH amendment: "leveling up should be simultaneous for all players, no need to go one at a time." Rulebook p.15 says heroes level "simultaneously" and then wait for each other; this build serialized that, and the serialization is what this milestone removes.

Observable outcome: every hero that bought a level is asked its Upgrade a Card at the same moment, each question owned by the one seat whose hero it is about, a hero with nothing to level still passed over silently, and the end-of-round continuation resuming when the LAST answer lands rather than the first. A disconnected player cannot be skipped, because the transition advances only when the set empties.

Design:

- The code's own position enabled this rather than a new reading: `HeroLevelUp` has documented since milestone 7.5 that its hero-id order is "a serialization of the page's simultaneity rather than a rule about who goes first", and `SeatProjection` has said since 8.30a that the commitment surface "authorizes every awaiting seat at once". This milestone removes a serialization; it does not invent concurrency.
- ONE pause carrying `OpenItems` - a list of `SystemTransitionOpenItem` (item, chooser, options) - and never N pause records, because a second pause would be a second copy of the resume location, which is the exact defect `PendingSystemTransitionStep` exists to make unrepresentable. `CompletedStepIds`/`CompletedItemIds` are unmoved: several questions, still one record of where the transition is.
- The open set is RE-DERIVED on every advance rather than mutated. `SystemTransitionAuthority.Advance` already re-runs the step rules after each answer, and those rules compute the outstanding set from the completed items and the board - so order-independence is structural, and a hero who answers can re-enter the set with its next level while others are still choosing.
- The single-item accessors `ItemId`, `ChoosingSide` and `Options` THROW when several items stand, rather than returning the first. That is the audit line: a silently wrong first element is the failure mode that survives to a playtest, and it is what found four consumers of the changed invariant that reading the inventory did not. `Invocations` stays well defined across the whole set, because every command names the item it answers - which is why the Director's surface needed no change to be able to answer all of them.
- Ownership is evaluated PER ITEM. `PendingDecisionOwner` names every awaited hero, and `AuthorizedCommands` filters a system-transition contract by each command's own item, exactly as the Minion-Battle arm already filters by command kind. Without that filter, a teammate who is also being asked would pass the contract-level ownership test and receive this hero's upgrades too - milestone 8.35d's live defect, one level up.
- A seat is projected only its own open item, and another hero's options are ABSENT rather than hidden, because an option list names cards in that hero's hand. Who is still choosing stays table-public through `DecisionOwner`; what they may choose does not. It is the same line rulebook p.7's commitments already draw.
- The Director keeps its serialization deliberately: one controller asks one question at a time, answering it brings up the next, and it reaches every hero. Simultaneity is a seat-surface property.

Acceptance checks:

- `SimultaneousHeroLevelUpTests` proves both heroes are asked at once from a rules-produced state (p.8's pity coin makes the round after a barren one a round everybody must level in), that answering one leaves the other standing and the stage where it is, that the reached state is identical whichever order two independent answers arrive in, that a partially answered set restores to the same remaining items and spends nothing verifying itself, that the Director is offered every open item and answers each directly, that the single-item accessors refuse a concurrent pause, and that an already-answered item is refused as stale.
- `EndOfRoundContinuationTests.ASeatBeingAskedItsOwnUpgrade_IsStillRefusedATeammatesOpenItem` is the regression for the recurrence: two seats of one side both awaited, each offered only its own hero's answers, the other's refused as `UnauthorizedCommand` with nothing moved, and the transition still standing after one of them answers.
- `EndOfRoundContinuationTests.EveryUpgradePauseOfARoundIsOwnedByExactlyTheSeatWhoseHeroItNames` runs the ownership rule once per open item and asserts `DecisionOwner.HeroIds` names every hero still choosing.
- `LanePushTransitionTests` is unmoved: p.8's blocked respawn Place stays one item at a time, because a placement changes the board the next placement reads. The set machinery is opt-in per step, declared by which describing interface a step's rules implement.
- `SnapshotCompatibilityCorpusTests` re-pins at schema v36; `OnlineMatchHudRulesPrivacyTests.SeatWireMeaning_UsesProtocolSeven_AndRejectsEveryOlderFrame` refuses versions 1 through 6.

Non-goals: No HUD work - the tray shine, the "waiting for N players" line and the per-hero prompt are the UX track's, consuming `DecisionOwner.HeroIds` and the per-item descriptions this milestone publishes. p.16's Ultimate unlock stays self-resolving and asks nobody. A hero that can afford several levels still finishes each before buying the next.

Historical evidence: [Phase 8 handoff](../Archive/Phase-Handoffs/PHASE_8.md#milestone-835u-level-every-hero-at-the-same-time-completion-evidence).

### [x] 8.35v END TURN is omnipresent during the acting seat's own turn and one press cancels and ends it

THIRD amendment, REVISED SEMANTICS (2026-09-04): "end turn button should be present the whole time on players action turn, even if he has attack selection on. If you click end turn, just cancel selections and end the turn" - superseding 8.35g's shipped double-press with one: an explicit END TURN press discards the seat's own open, not-yet-committed selection AND performs whatever legal completion it implies (stop, decline, or Hold) AND releases the turn, all in the same press. "Undo is for that, end turn is for ending the turn."

Observable outcome: during the acting seat's own turn, END TURN is always drawn - mid-movement, with an Attack target open, with an undeclined optional branch standing, or with nothing started at all. One press composes whatever the state calls for and hands the turn on in the same accepted-command sequence: a movement Stop, an effect choice's own Decline or Stop, or p.12's Hold, each immediately followed by the release once it stands. Where a printed rule leaves the step no legal decline (a Movement below its required minimum, an effect choice with real candidates and neither a Decline nor a Stop of its own), or the resolution has moved past anything this seat can answer or cancel (an Attack target already accepted and awaiting its defense - milestone 8.35f/8.35p's own wall), END TURN is drawn DISABLED rather than hidden, its cursor tooltip naming the reason from the SIXTH amendment's fixed vocabulary (`MANDATORY STEP` / `ANOTHER SEAT'S DECISION STANDS`).

Design:

- `MatchFeaturePresenters.CreateEndTurnInvocation` gains a fourth branch, checked ahead of its existing Stop/Release branches: `PendingEffectChoice.DeclineInvocation` - the amendment's own named "undeclined optional branch" case, missed in the first pass of this design and caught by re-reading the amendment's exact wording. It reads the SAME pre-built invocation the contextual region's own ACCEPT/DECLINE banners answer, never composing a new one.
- `RoundActionViewModel` carries two new, mutually exclusive facts alongside `EndTurnInvocation` (the constructor throws if more than one is set): `EndTurnCancelsOpenAttack` (true exactly when `PendingAttackTargetChoice` stands open and the seat's own 8.35f/8.35p bounded rewind, `IsSeatUndoAvailable`, is genuinely legal - the wall has not risen) and `EndTurnUnavailableReason` (the disabled tooltip text, gated on a new `IsViewerTheActingHero` helper comparing `SelectedActor.ActorId` against the viewer's own hero).
- `RoundActionPresenter.SubmitEndTurn()` is the one press's whole composition: while a Clear selection (6.3a) is open it is cancelled first (`CancelClear`, a pure presentation reset - nothing was ever submitted for an unconfirmed Clear, so there is nothing to rewind), then whatever `EndTurnInvocation`/`EndTurnCancelsOpenAttack` next offers is composed - a direct completion via `Execute`, or the seat's own `SubmitSeatUndo()` - bounded at 8 iterations. The whole loop is bounded to the ONE actor the press is for: `pressActorId` is read once, and every iteration verifies the projection still concerns that hero (still resolving it, or still awaiting ITS OWN release) before composing anything else, so a Director client - which sees every actor's own END TURN in turn, unlike a seat - can never carry one press past its own release into a second player's turn. `ConfiguredMatchSceneRuntime.SubmitEndTurn()` forwards to it, so every existing caller (every Play Mode journey filler) keeps its method name and signature. The drawn control raises a new parameterless `EndTurnRequested` event (mirroring 8.35f's `SeatUndoRequested`) instead of the generic `ForwardInvocation`/`InvocationSubmitted` path; `RoundActionPresenter.Submit` separately gained its own one-hop auto-chain-to-release for direct callers (a raw `ForwardInvocation`/`Submit` of the drawn invocation, which several existing Edit Mode cases and the live HUD's simple states still exercise).
- `UiToolkitMatchHudViews.RebuildEndTurn` no longer lets `HasExclusiveDecision` hide the control - that predicate used to suppress END TURN during exactly the mandatory-step and another-seat's-decision states the new disabled reason now names, which is precisely what the amendment asked fixed. The disabled state draws the identical `PewterBanner` shape, greyed via `HudElements.MarkUnavailable` (8.29d's own precedent), tooltip set to the reason.
- Three real defects caught by validation, not by review, and fixed before shipping:
  1. **Scoping.** The first draft of the disabled-reason check gated only on `IsTurnActive && SelectedActor.IsDefined` - both PUBLIC, unscoped facts (every viewer's board marks the same acting piece, milestone 8.35n) - so it read true for every seat while anyone's turn was active: a DEFENDER's own screen would have shown a disabled END TURN reading "ANOTHER SEAT'S DECISION STANDS" during their own Defense answer, and a pure spectator would have seen the same for no reason at all. The identical trap `AwaitedInputQuery` already names for the top-bar predicate. Fixed with `IsViewerTheActingHero`, which required a third public `RoundActionPresenter.CreateViewModel(projection, homeHeroId)` overload, because the two existing ones both default it to the Director sentinel.
  2. **Clear ordering.** `CreateEndTurnInvocation` does not read `ClearSelection` (6.3a) at all, so with nothing else pending it already projected the ordinary Hold alongside a still-open, unconfirmed board selection - submitting Hold first would have left the Clear orphaned rather than cancelled. Caught by the 8.35u worker's own Edit Mode run against this milestone's uncommitted work (`PromptReachabilityConformanceTests.TheOpenClearSelection_IsAnsweredOnTheBoardAndKeepsNoCandidateControl`, which now also expects the omnipresent control's own harmless presence). Fixed by checking Clear first in `SubmitEndTurn`'s loop.
  3. **Actor crossing.** The first working draft of `SubmitEndTurn` kept composing off `EndTurnInvocation` alone; for a Director client (which answers for everybody) this correctly chained a Hold into its own auto-released Stop/Decline, but the very next loop iteration then found the NEXT actor's own untouched-card Hold and submitted that too, silently spending a second player's turn inside a press that was only ever about the first - caught by `RoundPresenter_SubmitEndTurnCollapsesAMovementStopAndReleaseIntoOnePlayerPress` failing deterministically (`Expected: StopMovement But was: PerformHold`) in an otherwise-green complete Edit Mode run. Fixed with the `pressActorId` boundary described above.
- One further conformance fix, not a production defect: `PendingDecisionContractConformanceTests`'s own Effect-family command aggregation counted the Decline invocation twice (once via `EffectChoices`, once via `EndTurnInvocation`, the identical object reference) once finding #1 above shipped; its `SurfaceCommands` helper now folds END TURN's command in only when it is not already one of the ordinary controls.

Acceptance checks:

- One press from every state reaches the release (or completes the turn) in the same accepted sequence: mid-movement (`RoundPresenter_SubmitEndTurnCollapsesAMovementStopAndReleaseIntoOnePlayerPress`), untouched card (`RoundPresenter_SubmitEndTurnCollapsesHoldAndReleaseIntoOnePlayerPress`), an open Attack target selection pre-target over a real seat client (`RoundPresenter_EndTurnCancelsAnOpenAttackTargetSelectionAndReleasesInOnePress`, driven from the official six-seat state via `OwnerScopedProjectionTests.DriveThroughSeats` - `IsSeatUndoAvailable` is host-tracked from the turn's own beginning and cannot be proved by wrapping an already-mid-turn session), an open Clear selection (`RoundPresenter_EndTurnCancelsAnOpenClearSelectionFirst`), and a naturally-completed resolution already standing in the release pause (`RoundPresenter_SubmitEndTurnReleasesANaturallyCompletedTurnInOnePress`).
- Discard-does-not-commit: cancelling an open Attack target selection never answers it - the recorded stream never contains a `ChooseAttackTarget` for it; cancelling an open Clear never contains a `PerformClear` (same two cases as above).
- Disabled-with-reason: a Movement below its required minimum and a mandatory effect-choice mode both draw `MandatoryStep` (`RoundPresenter_EndTurnIsDisabledWithMandatoryStepWhenMovementCannotStopYet`, `RoundPresenter_EndTurnIsDisabledWithMandatoryStepForAMandatoryEffectChoice`); a disabled control refuses `SubmitEndTurn()` rather than silently doing nothing wrong.

- A non-owner seat draws no END TURN control at all - enabled, cancelling, or disabled - extending `SeatScopedMatchHudTests.EndTurnStandsAloneForTheSeatThatMayEndTheTurnAndIsDrawnForNobodyElse`, which was itself corrected to thread each seat's own `homeHeroId` (finding #1) rather than the Director-shaped overload it had been calling.
- Complete Edit Mode: 2,510/2,510 (up from the 2,502 baseline this milestone's own eight new cases and one new fixture file account for). Complete Play Mode: 278/278.
