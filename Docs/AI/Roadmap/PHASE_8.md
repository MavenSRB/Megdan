### [x] 8.35z8 Give every named control one shared treatment, and say when a step applied nothing

User-directed, the EIGHTEENTH amendment: "make these buttons nicer... they need clicking animations, they need to sit nicer in the trays... they look pasted over".

Observable outcome: every named control on screen - in the match and in the lobby - shares one chrome and one press-and-hover motion, and a resolution step that applied nothing says so and waits for an acknowledgement instead of passing in silence.

Design: `MatchHudTheme` gains the button chrome constants and `Lighten`/`Darken`; `HudElements.ApplyButtonMotion` wires the pointer events to a USS `scale` transition - idle 1, hover 1.035 over ~120 ms, pressed 0.94 over ~80 ms - gated on `enabledInHierarchy` so a disabled control never animates, with the background tint eased toward the control's OWN colour captured lazily on first hover, so a caller that recolours a button after construction still eases toward its own colour. `HudElements.Control` carries it to eleven existing call sites at once; `PewterBanner` and the card-faced buttons take scale-only motion layered over their established look rather than a chrome change. `PrivateLobbyViews`' HOST/JOIN/FILL/LEAVE and the seat PICK button drew NOTHING but Unity's stock button skin before this - no themed background, no border - which is very likely the largest single contributor to "pasted over".

The fizzle notice is the SIXTH amendment's deferred part b. Verified before designing rather than assumed: `EffectExecutionResult.ImpossibleMandatory()` is a parameterless factory, the two sites producing it record only a node id and bare kind, and milestone 8.35l's blocking-source channel is threaded through the OFFER-time feasibility walk alone. So acting-player scoping IS derivable - a seat is one hero, and the log's `SubjectHeroId` states whose resolution stopped - while the blocking hero and card are NOT derivable from any fact presentation can read today. That gap is RECORDED as open Core work rather than filled with an invented reason. `FizzleNoticeStream` mirrors `KineticCueStream`'s three rules (emit once, prime silently on first sight, re-prime on a rewind) with a latch that persists until acknowledged, and the notice draws as an overlay on the contextual region with an OK button built through the shared `Control`.

Acceptance checks:

- Every named control kind carries the shared chrome and motion - END TURN, the seat UNDO, an agreement choice, a Defense option, the Director's UNDO/RESET/EVENT LOG, and the lobby's HOST/JOIN and seat PICK - while a disabled control still never animates.
- A new `BlockedAction` row naming this viewer's hero latches one notice; it survives repeated observation until acknowledged; a rewind drops it silently; and an undefined viewer is the Director, who owns every hero's fizzle - the same reading `OwesACommitment`'s own undefined-viewer branch gives.
- The notice names the card and, honestly, not the blocker.

Non-goals: no rules, schema or wire change; the printed-action-region hotspot and `ReplacementRibbon` keep their own visual identity; naming the blocker is deferred to Core work this block records rather than performs.

### [x] 8.35z9 Make the awaited shine sweep reach its hosts, and keep gold for what is genuinely awaited

Three findings from the user's own live testing of the 8.35z4-fix package, taken together because they are one question - what the HUD's gold and kinetic language says, and to whom.

Observable outcome: the slow circling light actually travels the gold on a lit tray and a shining card instead of appearing nowhere; a player nobody is waiting on wears no gold at all, while the hero whose turn is under way still does; and the floating damage and block figures are gone from the board entirely.

Design:

- **The sweep found nothing, and had not since it shipped.** `CollectSweepHosts` asked for `Image` elements NAMED `gd-card-shine` - milestone 8.30d's prepared printed-artwork overlay, a different family altogether and unrelated to awaitedness. The awaited gold is a `VisualElement` named `gd-card-awaited-shine` on a card, and on a hero rail it is not an element at all: the rail's own border and background are painted. Wrong name AND wrong type, so the query matched neither, returned empty, and the caller returned early - silently, because an empty host set is legitimate on a HUD with no shine. Fixed with ONE shared marker, `MatchHudTheme.AwaitedShineClass`; a class rather than a name, because these are different element types on different surfaces and a class is the one thing all of them can carry. The sweep's own doc comment, which claimed it travelled the shine of heroes being waited on, becomes true for the first time.
- **The gold leaked.** During another hero's release pause the viewer's own committed card still wore the gold that means act now. Diagnosed rather than assumed: not legacy styling but the predicate. `isResolvingThisCard` is true from a card's REVEAL until it finishes resolving, and every hero reveals at the start of the round while turns run one at a time, so a card claimed the gold through as many as five other heroes' turns. It now additionally requires the turn to be under way for this hero - `IsTurnActive` and `SelectedActor` - which is the same pair the tray-light arm uses, so tray and card now agree by construction. Every other gold treatment was audited and left alone as already correctly gated.
- **The floating numbers are retracted**, on the user's own call after live testing: the figure cues, their printed-colour tinting and their impact timing are removed, keeping the lunge, flash, defeat fade, pulse and sweep. This deliberately retracts part of milestone 8.35z4, whose fourth defect was those figures appearing before their own cause; the timing fix was correct and is not being reverted as wrong - the feature above it is withdrawn. A replacement attack presentation is the user's own separate future design.

Acceptance checks:

- A composed HUD showing an awaited card AND a lit tray yields a NON-EMPTY sweep host set, containing one host from each surface and staying inside the recorded concurrent bound - the guard whose absence let an empty list ship four times.
- A hero the match is not waiting on has no awaited answer on any tray card AND nothing on that hero's whole surface carries the shared marker, while the acting hero's card still shines - which is what says the grammar was narrowed rather than removed.
- Every lunge, flash, slide, defeat, respawn, hitch, curve and sweep case stays green; only the five figure cases and the fade curve they alone used are removed.

Non-goals: no Core, Application or wire change; no new animation; the pewter banner's own resting glow and the hover states are that control's established identity and are not the awaited language.

Historical evidence: [Phase 8 handoff](../Archive/Phase-Handoffs/PHASE_8.md#milestone-835z9-make-the-awaited-shine-sweep-reach-its-hosts-and-keep-gold-for-what-is-genuinely-awaited-completion-evidence).

### [x] 8.35z10 Fix the match-log z-order, retire the Tie Breaker disc for a rail banner, centre the narration bar, correct the Fast Travel ribbon's anchor, and stamp the build identity

User-directed presentation batch from 8.35z9 live testing (2026-09-05), plus a coordinator-directed small QoL item folded in the same session.

Observable outcome: the match-log drop-down always paints above both hero rail columns; the narration bar stands centred at the strip's own top, keeping its gold treatment, typography and log chevron, with the drop-down unrolling centred beneath it; the Tie Breaker disc is retired and its fact reads as a slim team-coloured `TIEBREAKER` banner above the HOLDING team's own rail column, easing its position between the two columns on handover; the Secondary Fast Travel ribbon touches the printed boot/value ink it names instead of floating over the artwork beside a padded highlight ring; and the running build's own identity stamp is visible in the OS window title and a corner of the lobby screen.

From the user's own live evidence: no packaged build has ever shown a tooltip. Milestone 8.35s put blocked-action reasons on greyed controls, and 29 sites across the HUD set `.tooltip`, none of which had ever appeared outside the Editor.

Observable outcome: hovering any control that carries a tooltip - a greyed printed action, the tiebreaker rail banner, a hero plaque, a coin, a card reading - shows a popup in a packaged Player build, positioned near the cursor and clamped inside the panel.

Design: the premise was verified rather than assumed. Unity's own manual calls the tooltip event "an Editor-only event", and a Unity staff reply states that runtime tooltips need a custom solution - which matches the user's evidence exactly. `RuntimeTooltipLayer` is therefore one popup plus one root hook that serves every existing `.tooltip` string as it stands: no call site was touched.

The one structural wrinkle is that a blocked reason lives on a control `MarkUnavailable` has already disabled. The layer never registers anything on the hovered element at all: one capture-phase `PointerMoveEvent` handler on the HUD root - the pattern the match-log drop-down's click-away handler already ships - resolves `evt.target` and reads `.tooltip` as a plain property. Picking is governed by `pickingMode` alone and is unaffected by `enabledInHierarchy`, so a disabled control is still the resolved target and its string is still readable without asking it to handle anything. The popup is split into a pure hover state machine, pure clamped anchor math and an ancestor walk for "closest tooltip wins", so the timing and geometry are provable without a live panel. It eases in on opacity alone over the existing motion language and opts out of the ambient sweep both structurally and by class.

Acceptance checks:

- The hover state machine delays, shows, follows, hides and treats a stale timer as a no-op, and a hide followed by hovering the same element again starts a fresh delay.
- The clamping math holds at all four edges and in the degenerate case where the popup is larger than the viewport.
- The candidate lookup walks to the nearest ancestor's tooltip and still reports one when the element or an ancestor is disabled.

### [x] 8.35z21 Fire the last-minion Lane Push at the end of each action instance, repeats included

From the designer-ruling cross-check run against the shipped implementation on 2026-09-06 (official rules channel `#rules-questions-goa2`, designer Arty, 2026-06-18, harvested with that thread's other rulings): defeating the last enemy minion in the Battle Zone with an attack that also prints "after the attack" text pushes the lane *"At the end of the action. However, repeat instances are separate actions."* The first half already conformed - p.8's push cannot resolve while p.14's page is still open, because every Attack completion point gates on the same `TheTurnsActionIsUnderWay()` predicate the drain reads. The second half did not. Both shipped repeat families start their next instance on the far side of the single drain site inside `AdvanceInitiative`, so one push fired after all instances; and the build recorded that ordering deliberately, in a comment whose stated justification - the turn is stopped from the moment the first cause is recorded - is false across a repeat, because a running repeat instance keeps `HasPendingResolution` true.

Observable outcome: A push owed by one action instance resolves before the next instance of that action begins, so a rulebook p.10 repeat chooses its target on the already-pushed board - Wave counter flipped, Battle Zone advanced, the previous zone's minions removed and their respawns placed. A second qualifying cause arising inside a repeat instance owes its own push rather than being dropped by a cap written for one action. The side the push moves is the one the end of the action names, not the one frozen when the cause was recorded. A push that stops on p.8's sidebar Place now stops inside the action, and answering it resumes that action's own tail.

Design: the drain body moves out of `AdvanceInitiative` into one `ResolveOwedImmediateLanePush`, called first at every action-instance completion boundary - `CompleteAttackOrStartItsRepeat`, `CompleteAttackAndAdvance`, `CompleteMovementAndAdvance`, `CompleteSkillStep`, `CompleteGeneralRuleActionAndAdvance`, and `FinishTriggerAnswer` only where an action is held - and last at the old site as an unchanged backstop. The guard is asked inside the drain rather than at each call, so the drain and the six Attack completion points cannot disagree about whether p.14's page is open. The mid-action resume needs no new state: the continuation discriminates on the still-selected actor, which no non-boundary drain site can present, because acknowledging Initiative is what clears the selection. The winner is re-derived at the end of the action by re-asking the recorded cause against today's board; where the end of the action names no winner - both sides hold a minion, or neither does - the recorded answer stands, because p.8 says nothing about a push whose subject has gone, and dropping it would invent an outcome the rulebook does not print.

**Schema v36 to v37, required rather than incidental.** Draining inside an action creates a state v36 could not encode: a push stopped on the sidebar Place with a repeat instance decided and not yet started. The four owed-repeat fields were never persisted, because a v36 build always started an owed repeat inside the command that decided it. That is not only a reload concern - `MatchAggregate` captures a pre-image on every command and rolls a refused one back by deserializing it, so the decision vanished at the next refused command. v36 is rejected rather than migrated on `RISK-COMPAT-001`'s standing ground: the absent field's only reading, that no repeat is owed, is false in exactly the state a migration would have to be right about. Measured cost is +10 bytes on every payload whose turn carries a card-action authority and none at all where it carries none. In passing this closes a gap milestone 8.22 opened and never knew about, its after-push trigger having already been able to stop the page with an owed repeat recorded.

Acceptance checks:

- A push owed by the first instance resolves before the repeat instance asks for its target, driven on the official map with Arien's Violent Torrent; the measured event stream places the push's request and resolution between the two instances rather than after both.
- The repeat instance's target question is asked on the pushed board, composed against milestone 8.35z15's repeat-offer feasibility rather than asserted beside it.
- A push stopped inside the action holds the owed repeat across a snapshot byte-for-byte, and answering the Place resumes the action and starts the repeat on the pushed board.
- A winner the rest of the action changed is the one the push uses, and an end of action naming no winner leaves the recorded answer standing. Both halves are pinned, not only the one the ruling names.
- The turn release is refused while a push is stopped, and a standing repeat offer still keeps the common-rejection gate open, so the new ordering introduces no deadlock.
- The sixteen compatibility fixtures are renamed and re-pinned at v37 with v36 explicitly rejected, the nine aggregate-invariance digests re-pinned with their three equality groups unchanged, and the five literal version assertions extended rather than merely renumbered.

### [x] 8.35z22 Give the practice bots a brain a seat can actually run

The FOURTEENTH amendment (user, 2026-09-04: "tier one sounds good"), riding the THIRTEENTH's bot-seat vehicle. The spawned test drivers that fill empty seats for solo playtesting answered at random-legal, and milestone 8.35z13 gave them a greedy pass. This is phase A of the tier-1 brain: a static evaluation plus policies over it, measured against the shipped greedy pass rather than against an opinion. It amends this project's durable "AI opponents" non-goal for the first time, deliberately and narrowly, as the amendment required.

Observable outcome: A practice bot filling a seat plays a recognisable game. It starts the action that most improves its own position rather than the first legal one, commits a card by what that card's printed structure offers, picks attack targets by what the attack achieves, spends a defense when taking the hit is what costs its team, and closes on the fighting instead of drifting. Measured against the shipped greedy pass over twenty seeds played from both sides: 82.5% (33W/7L), 80.0% on held-out seeds it was not tuned on, 94.9% against random-legal. Decision latency 0.045-0.051 ms against a 2.0 s watchable pace.

**The commissioned one-ply lookahead cannot ship to a seat, and that is milestone 8.32's privacy law working rather than a defect.** The amendment asked for candidate-action simulation over match copies, the engine's own snapshot/restore as the cloning mechanism. A practice bot is a spawned process holding an ordinary network seat: from `AttachOnlineClient` onward every mutable read is rebuilt from the wire, the locally composed host is never advanced and so captures a match that has not started, and the wire publishes other heroes' hands as counts without identities and carries no command log to replay into a shadow session. A bot that could simulate the live match would be a bot that could see six hands. The lookahead therefore ships as an optional capability wired only where full information exists, and the gate was measured on the SHIPPED configuration with it off, because measuring one configuration and shipping another is the probe-fidelity mistake milestones 8.35g and 8.35u both record.

Design: nine evaluation terms, all public, all content-generic, integer-scored so ties are exact - team Life advantage, board presence, level advantage, own level, own coins, minion pressure, engagement proximity, threat adjacency scaled by rulebook p.13's price of this hero's own Defeat, and own hand size. A maintenance covenant is recorded on the type itself: every term reads a structural fact the rules publish for all content, and no term names a card, hero, action or effect, because the roster is expanding and a per-card term rots the day a new hero lands. Where card knowledge is read at all, it reads printed structure - does this card offer an Attack, what Range does it print - never identity. One rulebook fact is used as a public approximation and labelled as one, p.13's Level-to-Tier row pricing a Defeat, because the real authority reads the hero's own cards and a bot cannot see them. Every gameplay decision draws from a deterministic xorshift generator rather than `System.Random`, whose algorithm differs between Mono and .NET and would otherwise make a harness number a statement about a harness-only twin.

**Acting FIRST is a disadvantage, and the measurement is what says so.** The commitment policy first weighted printed Initiative positively, from a true fact - the resolution takes the maximum total, so higher Initiative genuinely acts first - and an inference from it that self-play falsified. Flipping the sign alone moved 51.3% to 82.5%, and the ablation moved the commitment family from 2.6 points to 33.8. It is recorded at length on the constant, because a negative weight there reads like a typo a later reader would fix, and recorded as a measurement rather than a rule, because it is a property of the current content and match length.

**A deterministic brain cannot escape an infeasible offer by luck, and the first live Solo cohort is what proved it.** The shipped driver did nothing on a refusal: it re-ticked, recomputed the same decision from the same unchanged projection, and submitted the identical command again. Under random-legal, and under the tier-0.5 pass that falls back to it, the next tick drew something else and the seat moved on. Tier-1 is deterministic, so one offered-then-refused command became a hot infinite loop - the cohort spun more than eleven thousand refusals on a single seat at a frozen version and the match never advanced past it. The cause is not the brain; it is the offer-feasibility defect recorded below as corrective debt, which the brain turned from a latent hazard into a permanent one. The driver now remembers what was refused at the current projection version, drops it, and chooses again from what remains - which is exactly what the self-play harness already did, and therefore what makes the shipped driver behave the way the measured configuration behaved. It never widens the candidate set, so every answer is still one the seat was offered; and when every offer at a version has been refused it says so once rather than spinning silently.

**Two engine defects this milestone found and did NOT fix, both reproducible and neither in bot territory.** The projection offers a Defense card the rules then refuse, with the preview itself listing it (three independent seeded repros; the same family as milestones 8.35l and 8.35z15). And `CaptureSnapshot` produces snapshots the same session cannot restore, reproduced with no bot involved at all, which bears on the seat undo of 8.35f and the snapshot restore of 8.35i because all three stand on the same machinery. Both are recorded as `[Explicit]` diagnostics rather than silently tolerated, and both owe their own correctives. The second is also why the lookahead is not merely unavailable at a seat but not yet trustworthy anywhere: it latches a poisoned flag on a throwing or refused restore and degrades to the static judgment rather than taking a playtest build down.

Acceptance checks:

- The tier-1 brain beats the shipped greedy pass over twenty seeded self-play matches played from both sides, and again on seeds it was not tuned on; it beats random-legal; and the moved greedy pass still beats random-legal, so the tier-0.5 relocation is proven not to have changed it.
- One seed names one match: the brain is deterministic under its own generator.
- The brain only ever answers with a command it was offered, and the evaluation reads only what a seat can see - both asserted structurally rather than by sampling.
- Team agreements stay deterministic and unchanged, because convergence beats cleverness where each bot runs its own die and nothing is scripted to resolve a disagreement.
- Four diagnostics ship `[Explicit]` - side bias, ablation, lookahead cost, and the snapshot round-trip - so they are reproducible on demand and join no suite total.
- The Solo rehearsal cohort passes end to end: a host process fills its own table through its own shipped control, one scripted seat and five spawned tier-1 drivers play a complete match to one converged outcome, and every spawned driver is gone when the host session ends. A refused offer no longer stops the match: the passing run records 645 accepted submissions against 24 refusals, where the pre-fix run recorded more than eleven thousand refusals and no progress at all.
- Complete unfiltered dotnet harness, Edit Mode and Play Mode pass.

Non-goals: no difficulty tiers, no product-AI claim, and no single-player campaign; no wire, schema, protocol or rules change; no change to what any seat is authorized to submit; no bot reading state its seat is not projected; and no lookahead in the shipped configuration.
