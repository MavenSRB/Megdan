## Problem — maximum 300 words

Some adult players still value the strategic coordination, distinct hero roles and shared victories of multiplayer online battle arena games, but no longer want continuous reflex-driven execution to determine their contribution. Our hypothesis is that these players lack a sufficiently appealing way to retain individual responsibility within a team while shifting competition toward deliberate tactical decisions.

The starting point is the applicant's experience building and playing a private tactical prototype. Its match histories provide behavioral evidence that this form of play can sustain extended sessions. They do not yet establish the size of the target segment or prove demand for the proposed original product.

We will test the problem with current and former MOBA players, distinguish execution pressure from time constraints and learning barriers, and compare responses with observed play. We will not assume that older players universally dislike real-time games or promise short sessions without evidence.

## Affected users and current alternatives — maximum 200 words

Our initial customer hypothesis is adult PC players with MOBA experience who enjoy coordinated competition with friends but prefer deliberate decisions over continuous mechanical execution. Early adopters may overlap with tactical-strategy and tabletop-game communities.

The direct behavioral alternative is continuing with a real-time MOBA such as Dota 2, which already supplies one-hero responsibility, role mastery, parties and matchmaking without an upfront game purchase. Tactical alternatives solve different parts of the problem: Skygard Arena offers 1v1 turn-based control of three-Champion squads; Gloomhaven offers cooperative card-driven hero tactics; and Blood Bowl 3 offers competitive turn-based positioning with one person controlling an entire team.

Atlas Reactor is the closest identified historical precedent: coordinated 4v4 arena combat with one Freelancer focus and simultaneous timed turns. Its 2019 shutdown for lack of sustainability makes acquisition, retention and service cost central risks rather than evidence of an empty market. Megdan must demonstrate that three independently controlled heroes, card-driven initiative and lane/minion objectives create sufficient value to overcome group formation, match length and purchase friction. [Dated comparison and sources](SMART_START_REQUIREMENTS.md#initial-competitor-evidence).

## Solution — maximum 300 words

We propose an original 3v3 turn-based tactical game in which each player controls one hero and contributes to a shared team objective. The intended experience combines hero specialization, positional decisions and team coordination while moving skill expression away from continuous reflex execution.

The next phase builds on a self-funded functional prototype and delivers an original, validated six-hero 3v3 MVP suitable for publisher due diligence. Six proven kits retain gameplay while receiving new identities, 108 playable card-face layouts and 108 distinct original MVP-quality compositions; six unused Handicap faces remain optional. A human-directed system may reuse rights-cleared original models, renders, environments and effects without duplicating card compositions or requiring 108 premium standalone paintings. Support would fund six hero and three minion model/rig families, one coherent original 3D map, required tokens/cues, a free camera, bounded animation/VFX and functional audio. Screen-space cards and controls remain 2D. Premium polish, broader content and launch infrastructure remain for later publisher-financed production.

The working original six-hero presentation roster is Svarog, Stribog, Morana, Mokoš, Veles and Perun. Their distinct mythology-inspired expression, card presentation and production assets form part of the funded original-MVP work.

[Approximately twenty further heroes remain a post-grant ambition. Clearly labelled AI-generated placeholder visuals may support the slice but are not presented as final production assets. Game audio is future grant-budgeted work.]

Development will combine implementation, observed playtests and structured user feedback. AI tools assist code and visual prototyping, including the planned vertical slice; AI generation itself is not the customer value. The project will test whether the intended audience understands the game, returns voluntarily and values it enough to support a viable business.

## Differentiation — one or two sentences, maximum 200 words

For players who want MOBA-style coordination and individual hero responsibility without continuous reflex execution, Megdan is an original 3v3 card-and-initiative tactical arena in which each person owns one hero and deliberate decisions shape team success. Unlike squad-control tactics, and unlike Atlas Reactor's timed simultaneous prediction, Megdan combines independently controlled teammates with visible initiative, lane/minion pressure and a deliberately paced shared plan; this is a testable value proposition, not proven superiority.

## What already works

The self-funded prototype is a playable game, not just a concept or visual mock-up. The following summarizes the implementation owners rather than promising new grant deliverables.

| Capability | Existing behavior and evidence boundary |
| --- | --- |
| Team play and setup | Private Host/Join, individual hero seats, 2v2 through 5v5, 32 configured heroes, four maps, host-selected Waves/Lives. The proposed commercial focus remains 3v3. See [current implementation](CURRENT_IMPLEMENTATION.md). |
| Complete tactical match | Card commitments/reveal, initiative and turns, movement and positional choices, attack/defense and abilities, minions and lane push, defeat/respawn, coins and level-up, victory and per-hero scoreboard. See [implementation](CURRENT_IMPLEMENTATION.md), [progression](HERO_PROGRESSION.md), [combat](COMBAT_AND_CONSEQUENCES.md). |
| Large-map play | Two 4v4/5v5 boards, two independent lane battles/pushes with a shared turn order; either Throne capture ends the match. See [maps](../Maps/README.md). |
| Player interface | Board interaction, hero/card trays, card inspection, legal action/choice presentation, runtime tooltips, turn/phase feedback and optional experimental timers/overlays. See [HUD](MATCH_HUD.md) and [presentation](CONFIGURED_PRESENTATION.md). |
| Multiplayer continuity | Direct keyed TCP sessions, seat ownership and reconnect/resynchronization; compatible build/data required. Not public matchmaking, a hosted scalable service or blanket proof of internet reachability. See [implementation](CURRENT_IMPLEMENTATION.md). |
| Technical foundation | Rules independent of Unity presentation; typed commands/events, deterministic replay, versioned snapshots and bounded player Undo, plus Director diagnostics. These are implemented technical capabilities, not all end-user product features. See [architecture](ARCHITECTURE.md). |
| Experimental cooperative mode | One to three players share three Blue hero seats against Melee/Ranged/Heavy enemy behavior, with shared lobby and host authority. Separate from the grant's core competitive proposition; see [PvE implementation](PVE_IMPLEMENTATION.md). |

The latest Windows W079 package includes the large-map fixes; later W080–W082 changes are source-only. Mac W075 is older and native launch/cross-platform networking are not verified.
