## RISK-STATE-001

| ID | Concrete failure mode and impact | Evidence / confidence | Trigger or detection | Earliest affected milestone | Retirement or reassessment | Approved mitigation or explicit acceptance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `RISK-STATE-001` | Snapshot restore entry points accept optional and defaulted parameters, so adding a persisted field and omitting its restore argument compiles cleanly and silently drops that state on every restore, replay, and Undo. The defect surfaces as divergent state rather than as an error. | Verified 2026-08-13: thirteen restore back doors across eleven types, with defaulted parameters that make omission legal. No compiler enforcement exists. Recorded as `F613-07`. | Any addition or change to persisted authoritative state. | 6.1g | Reassess at 6.1g; retire when an incomplete restore has been demonstrated to fail compilation. | Replace optional restore parameters with one required typed restore-state value per authority so omission cannot compile. |

Resolution note (2026-08-13): Milestone 6.1g replaced every optional and defaulted restore parameter with one required typed restore-state value per authority, collapsing seventeen restore entry points across eleven types - and the sixteen distinct omission routes they exposed, four more than the register's original estimate of thirteen - into one entry point each. The states are immutable, validate in their constructors, and carry exactly the fields their matching `Write` emits; reconstruction context such as participants, definitions, and the board stays a separate required argument so that no deconstructed member is one the writer must ignore. Each state also declares an explicit `Deconstruct` that the serializer's write path consumes, so adding a persisted field now breaks capture, write, read, and restore together on arity rather than being silently left unwritten. The register's approved mitigation covered only the restore direction; the write direction was added under the user's explicit scope decision.

Closure evidence is the recorded compile transition, not a passing test. At `4a5b337` a probe restoring a round while omitting `pendingMinionBattleChoice` and `pendingLanePushRequest` compiled cleanly, and the probe type was confirmed present inside the compiled `GuardsDigital.Core.dll`, proving the omission reached the production assembly. After the fix the equivalent omission fails with CS7036. Byte identity is unchanged: the five pinned schema v6 compatibility-corpus SHA-256 digests are identical across the refactor, and both complete suites passed at Edit Mode 354 and Play Mode 17 with zero failed, skipped, or inconclusive. The risk is retired because the failure mode it describes can no longer be expressed in C#.

Resolution note (2026-08-12): Milestone 5.8a1 gives each generated minion view serialized stable binding identity independent of its transient setup object, retains correctly identified views across editor backup/domain restoration, and makes the grid publish one complete current view set after rebuild or deliberate replacement. The composition root atomically replaces the passive board and pointer-input caches, ensures exactly one selector/collider per current view, and configures every selector against the same board adapter. Focused lifecycle tests reproduce transient identity loss and destroyed-view replacement; the configured Play Mode scenario covers rebuild, commitment, target refresh, removal, Undo, restart, restore, and scene reload; and the targeted fixed-launcher Editor transition passed with `BatchMode: 0`. Final focused, affected, and complete suites passed with no `MissingReferenceException`, missing-script warning, duplicate binding, or log-hygiene match. The risk is retired and remains scheduled for independent reassessment at milestone 5.8c.
