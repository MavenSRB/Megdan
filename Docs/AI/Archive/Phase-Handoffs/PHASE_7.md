| 7.1 | Phase 7's first milestone, and the first change since 5.1a that reaches all ten configured card definitions at once. `CardProgressionProfile` is a required `CardDefinition` argument with three factories and no overload that omits it, which is what makes acceptance check two's "explicitly represents its applicable typed `CardColor` or verified absence" true by construction rather than by convention: a fixture card that models no progression has to say so through `NotStated`. The colour set is the rulebook's own - p.7's "Primary Action Icons (Always match the card's color)" fixes the five, p.15 names Gold and Silver as the Basic pair that "are never upgraded, have no Tier, and are not considered when checking your lowest card Tier", and p.6 deals the starting hand as "the three Tier I cards and the two basic cards with no Tier". `CardTier` deliberately stops at III: Tier IV is p.16's Ultimate, which milestone 7.3 owns, and the Handicap "H" Tier is returned to the box at setup, so adding either would put a value in the type that no definition declares and no query answers for. `CardProgressionProfile.BasicCard` accepts Gold and Silver alone, which is p.15's own claim; the converse - that Gold and Silver are always Basic - is inferred from the five-colour hand rather than stated, so it is deliberately not enforced. Each configured hand is p.15's five distinct colours with the Basic pair, and the assignment is chosen so that nothing can reproduce it: **card colour is not team identity**, because the Red team holds the Red-coloured Red Bombard and the Blue-coloured Red Bulwark while the Blue team holds the Red-coloured Blue Sentinel and the Blue-coloured Blue Feint; not the display name, because every card in a hand shares that hand's word while its five colours differ; not the hand slot, because the Basic pair sits at positions one and three of the Red hand and three and five of the Blue; and not the printed action icon, because Red Lancer prints a Ranged Attack and is Gold while Red Bulwark prints a Skill and is Blue. That last mismatch is deliberate evidence rather than sloppiness - p.7 ties icon to colour for official cards, these are synthetic cards whose primary actions the showcase milestones fixed and milestone 7.9 gates unchanged, and no implementation may read a colour off an icon. Each of the six non-Basic Tier I cards supplies the four same-colour cards p.15 has it upgrade into: two Tier II, and the two Tier III **both** of those lead to, because the page sends the player back to the same deck with the same instruction and the other Tier II card has been set aside as an item by then. Twenty-four generated definitions, three colours of five cards plus the Basic pair per hand, which is p.6's eighteen-card deck less the Ultimate this milestone does not add. **None of the twenty-four is dealt**: p.15 finds them in the player's own deck, milestone 7.5 is the first that moves one, and dealing them would have changed the five-card hand every pinned digest, card-matrix fixture and configured scene measures for behaviour this milestone does not add. `PlaceholderMovementHand` gained `DealtDefinitions` beside `Definitions` because the two differ for the first time, and the fixtures that assert the five-card shape now name the one they always meant. What the generated cards print is minimal and says so - one printed Primary Movement carrying the source card's value, plus the source card's Defense reaction - and copying the source values rather than raising them is what keeps this from rebalancing anything. `CardProgressionValidator` checks every stated candidate against p.15 over the whole definition set at setup: resolvable, same colour, exactly one Tier higher, non-Basic, and two of them for a Tier I or II card and none for a Tier III. It runs from `CardInitialSetupValidator`, so the configured content is checked at composition rather than at first use, and its four refusals are asserted with hand-built content. Colour therefore constrains progression without ever determining it, which is the distinction acceptance check five draws: nothing derives a candidate from a colour, and the relationship is content the card states. `CardCharacteristicQuery` is the pure query the card-system contract specifies, and it is the only place a colour is read. It narrows on colour, Tier, classification, owner, location, played state and resolution status, and every omitted field means "do not narrow", so the criteria that names nothing selects every card. Candidates come back ordered by instance id, which is `DefeatRewardAccounting`'s precedent and which makes the answer independent of the order the caller supplied its collections in; an empty result is an ordinary answer. **Information availability is a rule and not an option**: a facedown card has no rules-facing colour, Tier or classification and cannot satisfy a predicate about any of them, while a criteria that asks only about location or ownership still finds it, because a facedown card is still somewhere and still belongs to someone. The fixture proves both halves and the recovery - revealing restores the reading, so the suppression is about orientation rather than about having been played - and it asserts that the definition data remains inspectable throughout, which is exactly the point the contract makes about content data not being the acting context's to read. `TryGetLowestNonBasicTier` answers over whatever scope its criteria states rather than choosing one: p.15 asks the question of a hand, milestone 7.4 will ask it of a hero being defeated mid-round whose cards are scattered, and a query that picked a scope would make every caller inherit it. False means no non-Basic card was selected, which is an answer rather than a failure. `ColorRestrictedDiscard` is the composition acceptance check four asks for, built out of the three pieces the state-capability contract names: the query derives the candidates, the effect frame's own `EffectChoiceRequest` carries the chooser and the ordered `CardInstance` candidates, and the mutation performs the discard. The division of labour runs one way and the fixture proves it from both sides - an answer naming a card of the wrong colour is refused as `ChoiceNotOffered` without mutating it, while the same card handed straight to `TryDiscardFromHand` is discarded, because whose card it is and what colour it is were never that mutation's questions. `Resolve` re-derives the candidate set when the answer arrives rather than trusting the offer, which is the rule every target selection in this build already follows. `CardLifecycleAuthority.TryDiscardDefenseFromHand` became `TryDiscardFromHand` and changed nothing else: its behaviour was never Defense-specific, and a second general discard beside a Defense-named one would have been two names for one transition, which is the shape milestone 6.13's audit removed from retrieval. Three new transitions carry p.15's own sentences - return to deck, take into hand, and set aside Facedown - and the fixture proves that owner and definition revision survive all three, which they do by construction since only `CardInstance.State` moves. Facedown beside the dashboard, a set-aside card's colour stops being rules-facing, which is the same suppression rule and the correct outcome: p.16 turns it Faceup when it becomes an item, and that is milestone 7.2's transition. Hero Level is the milestone's one piece of new authoritative state. `HeroProgressionState` initializes every participating hero to Level 1 from p.15's first sentence, `HeroLevel` bounds itself to that page's 1-8 table, and **nothing in this build changes one** - the purchase, its coin cost, its mandatory ordering and the upgrade it forces are milestone 7.5's, which this milestone's non-goals exclude. It is persisted anyway, because Phase 7's durability rule requires the milestone that adds authoritative state to close its persistence boundary and says a later integration gate is not substitute evidence. What that buys is concrete rather than ceremonial: restore refuses a payload whose Levels do not cover exactly the participating heroes, and the fixture proves a non-default Level survives capture and restore along with four separate coverage refusals - a missing hero, a duplicate, an unknown one, and an undefined Level - which is the evidence that the state persists a value rather than a default. Schema v16 moved to v17, and v16 is rejected rather than migrated for a reason stronger than "one read path per version": a v16 payload records no Level at all, and reading one as Level 1 would be a guess about a value p.15 makes authoritative - a guess that is right for every payload this build can produce, precisely because nothing here changes a Level, and therefore one that must not be written into the reader, since the first payload it would be wrong about is the first one 7.5 writes and by then the migration would look established. The durability evidence runs through the public command path: a refused command leaves the whole payload byte-identical, retained-prefix Undo truncates to the earlier state, replaying the same command reconstructs the later one, Restart replays from the start rather than retaining anything, and a second session restored to the intermediate payload continues to the identical later one. No projection field was added, which follows the coin-balance precedent exactly: the balances have never reached `MatchSessionProjection` either, no presenter reads a colour, a Tier or a Level, and the milestone's non-goals exclude card-colour presentation. **Both Windows-only digest fixtures were re-pinned** for three causes together - the schema move, the ten content revisions to `phase-7-1-rev-1`, and the twenty-four new definition-table entries - and the delta was measured on the compatibility corpus in a throwaway worktree at pre-change revision `a9fda8b` rather than assumed. It closes exactly: the definition table gains 1060 bytes for twenty-four entries of a one-byte length prefix plus a 26-to-29-character id plus a one-byte prefix plus the fifteen-character revision, loses 10 because each of the ten configured revision strings goes from sixteen characters to fifteen, loses 10 more where the ten instances name their definition references, and gains 42 for the hero-Level section - an int32 count plus, per hero, a one-byte length prefix, a fourteen-character hero id and an int32 Level. 1060 - 10 - 10 + 42 = 1082, which is seven of the eight fixtures; `pending-choice-v17` is 1081 because its paused Movement continuation records the acting card's definition reference and that card is one of the ten, while the three other fixtures with a paused resolution are unaffected because theirs are the fixture-only Skill cards whose revisions this milestone leaves alone. The common prefix is 30 bytes in every fixture, which is where the version field sits. The aggregate-invariance equality groups are deliberately unchanged - accepted, rejected and stale still share one pair, restored and replayed still match their live counterparts, and the defense-discard family still differs - which is the check that the schema and the content moved and no behaviour did. Six configured-card fixtures and one Play Mode fixture were re-pointed at the new revision, and `PushRepeatSkillPlayModeTests` was re-pointed **before** the run rather than after, which is milestone 6.4c's recorded lesson applied rather than rediscovered.
